Our Update Policy

We treat corrections as part of our editorial commitment, not as a failure.

We update content when:

  • New data becomes available — e.g., a fresh batch of enquiry data, updated supplier quotes, or a new manufacturer TSB.
  • A factual error is identified — by our own team, our expert panel, or a reader.
  • Our understanding evolves — e.g., a failure pattern becomes better understood over time.
  • Market conditions change — e.g., significant shifts in vehicle values or labour rates.

When we update a page, we:

  1. Note the change in this log, with a clear description and reason.
  2. Update the dateModified in the page's schema markup.
  3. Ensure the change is reflected consistently across all related pages (e.g., if we correct a cost on the engine knowledge centre, we check economics and comparison pages).

We also review all pages at least once a year to ensure they remain accurate. Our editorial standards explain our overall approach to accuracy and independence.

How We Verify Changes Before Logging

We don't make changes lightly. Every correction goes through a verification process.

When we identify a potential error:

  • Investigate — We re‑check the original source data (quote, survey, TSB, etc.) to confirm the error.
  • Consult expert panel — If the error is technical, we ask at least one member of our expert panel to review the new information.
  • Cross‑check — We verify against at least two independent sources before making a change.
  • Log and update — Once confirmed, we update the page, log the correction, and update the dateModified.

If a correction involves a previously published percentage or count, we also note the old and new values in the log, and we ensure that any pages that cited the old value are updated accordingly. Our data verification page explains our sourcing principles in more detail.

Recent Examples of Significant Updates

To illustrate how we handle corrections, here are a few notable examples.

  • 306DT cost range (2026-02-15) — A supplier gave us updated pricing for reconditioned 306DT units. The previous range was outdated, and we updated it across all relevant pages. The log entry explains the source.
  • 204DTD timing chain (2026-02-10) — A new Land Rover TSB clarified that even post‑2019 Ingenium diesels can experience chain stretch under certain conditions. We adjusted the language on the Knowledge Centre and failure pages.
  • Freelander economics (2026-02-05) — Reader feedback and survey data showed that many owners were replacing engines in vehicles worth less than the repair cost, despite our advice. We made the 60% threshold guidance more prominent and added a specific scrapping recommendation for low‑value examples.

These are the kinds of changes we log — not cosmetic edits, but substantive shifts that affect decision‑making. We believe this transparency is essential to building trust.

How to Report an Error

We welcome your feedback. If you spot something that looks wrong, please tell us.

You can report an error via our contact page. Please include:

  • The URL of the page where you found the error.
  • A clear description of what you believe is incorrect.
  • If possible, a source or reference that supports your correction (e.g., a link to a TSB, a quote from a specialist, or a screenshot of a conflicting source).

We investigate every report. If we confirm an error, we will make the correction and log it here. If we disagree, we will explain why. We aim to respond to all error reports within 5 working days.

We also encourage you to review our editorial standards and data verification pages to understand how we maintain accuracy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you log every single change?

We log every substantive change — i.e., any change that affects the meaning of a claim, advice, or data. Minor typographical corrections, formatting tweaks, and non‑substantive copy edits are not logged.

How do I know a page has been updated?

Every page carries a dateModified in its schema markup and often a visible "Last updated" note near the footer. We also list major updates in this log.

What if I disagree with a correction?

We welcome debate. If you believe a correction is incorrect, please contact us with your evidence. We will re‑examine the issue and, if warranted, revise our position and update the log accordingly.

Do you correct errors from other sources?

If we cite a third‑party source (e.g., a TSB or a market report) and that source is later found to be erroneous, we will update our content and note the correction. Our primary reliance is on our own verified data, but we cross‑reference many sources.
