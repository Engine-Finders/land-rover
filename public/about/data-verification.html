Our Data Sources

We build our content on a foundation of real, verifiable data — not hearsay or anecdotes.

Our primary source is 24,650 unique quote requests collected through EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk during the full year 2025 [EM-VERIFIED]. These are real enquiries from UK owners looking for replacement engines, and they give us a direct view of what actually fails, what owners are quoted, and which models generate the most demand.

We supplement this with:

  • Supplier quote notes — mechanics listing specific failures when quoting, which we use to identify qualitative failure patterns ([EM-INTERVIEW])
  • Post‑sale customer surveys — feedback from 100+ owners 3–6 months after replacement, giving us insight into outcomes and satisfaction ([EM-SURVEY])
  • Manufacturer Technical Service Bulletins (TSBs) — official documents from Land Rover, Jaguar, BMW, Mercedes, and others, which we use to validate failure modes (Tier 1 sources)
  • Public forums, Honest John, Parkers, and other owner communities — we cross‑reference our proprietary data with what owners report online (Tier 2 sources)
  • Third‑party market data — vehicle value bands from Auto Trader, Motors.co.uk, and CarGurus, which we cite as [THIRD-PARTY]

We never rely on a single source. Every claim is triangulated from at least two independent channels before we publish it.

The Tagging System

Every number carries a tag that tells you exactly where it came from.

We use a standard set of tags across all our content. When you see a number with a tag, you know whether it's a verified count, a cost estimate, a qualitative pattern, or a third‑party figure. This transparency is central to our editorial standards.

Tag Meaning Source
[EM-VERIFIED] Exact count or percentage from our internal CRM (enquiry volumes, model requests, condition split) 24,650 quote requests, Jan–Dec 2025
[EM-QUOTE] Pricing ranges derived from actual supplier quotes, cross‑checked with marketplaces Supplier quotes + interviews
[EM-SURVEY] Post‑sale customer survey results (e.g., satisfaction rates) 100+ surveys 3‑6 months after replacement
[EM-INTERVIEW] Direct statements from mechanics, workshop owners, or supplier managers Hundreds of conversations 2024–2025
[PATTERN DATA] Qualitative failure patterns derived from expert consensus, not proprietary counts Triangulated from enquiry descriptions, TSBs, forums
[THIRD-PARTY] External data (Auto Trader, Parkers, manufacturer TSBs, etc.) Named source + date
[INDUSTRY ESTIMATE] Informed estimate where no proprietary or third‑party data exists Workshop consensus / market observation

We never publish a number without a tag. If we don't have reliable data, we say so — using phrases like "typically" or "many owners report" — and we never present an estimate as a verified fact.

Our Methodology

We collect, clean, and cross‑reference data to ensure consistency and accuracy.

Our full methodology is published on the Data Methodology page, but in summary:

  1. Collection — We aggregate enquiry data from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, including free‑text problem descriptions, quote amounts, and vehicle details.
  2. Cleaning — We remove duplicates, anonymise personal data, and categorise engines, models, and failure types using a standardised taxonomy.
  3. Cross‑referencing — We compare our proprietary data against manufacturer TSBs, public forums, and expert interviews to validate failure patterns and cost ranges.
  4. Tagging — Every number is assigned a source tag (as above) so readers can verify the origin.
  5. Publication — We publish data in tables, charts, and articles, always linking back to the underlying sources and our methodology.
  6. Review — Content is reviewed by our expert panel of specialist mechanics before publication.

We update our data annually, and we track changes over time to spot emerging trends. Our Engine Failure Index is the flagship output of this process.

Verification Process — How We Check Every Number

We don't take any number at face value. Every figure goes through a multi‑stage check.

  • Internal consistency — We check that counts add up (e.g., per‑engine totals match global totals) and that percentages are calculated correctly.
  • External validation — We compare our figures against publicly available data where possible (e.g., DVLA vehicle parc data, manufacturer recall volumes) to ensure they are plausible.
  • Specialist sign‑off — Our expert panel reviews any new or updated figures to confirm they match real‑world workshop experience.
  • Correction loop — If we find an error, we correct it and log it in our corrections log, explaining the change and the reason.

We also note that request volume does not equal failure rate — a high number of enquiries may reflect a large vehicle parc or audience bias, not necessarily poorer engineering. We always interpret data in context, and we never use volume alone to make reliability claims.

Corrections & Updates

We are transparent about our mistakes and keep our content current.

We maintain a public corrections log that records every substantive change we make to published data, cost figures, or advice. Minor typographical fixes are not logged, but any change that affects the meaning of a figure or recommendation is recorded with the date, the original content, the correction, and the reason.

We also update pages when new data becomes available — for example, when the annual enquiry dataset is refreshed, or when a manufacturer issues a new TSB that changes our understanding of a failure mode. All pages carry a dateModified in our schema markup so that readers and search engines can see when the content was last reviewed.

If you spot an error, please contact us — we will investigate and correct it if warranted.

Expert Review & Oversight

Our content is not published until it has been reviewed by working specialists.

We have an expert panel of independent mechanics and workshop owners who specialise in Land Rover, Range Rover, Jaguar, and other premium brands. They review our technical content for accuracy, including failure patterns, diagnostic steps, and cost estimates.

Panel members are paid for their time, but they do not influence our editorial verdicts or our choice of which specialists we recommend. They are selected for their practical experience, not their commercial relationships. Their feedback is incorporated before publication, and any disagreements are resolved through additional research and consultation.

We also consult our panel when we update data or when new failure patterns emerge — ensuring that our content stays grounded in real‑world workshop experience.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you know your data is accurate?

We triangulate every number from at least two independent sources. Our primary data is real UK enquiry counts, which we cross‑reference with manufacturer TSBs, specialist interviews, and owner surveys. We also maintain a corrections log and update content as new evidence emerges.

What does [EM-VERIFIED] mean?

It means the number comes directly from our internal CRM of 24,650 unique quote requests collected in 2025. These are exact counts, not estimates.

How can I cite your data?

We encourage citing our data, especially from our Engine Failure Index. Please cite the page and date, and reference the source tags used. We provide a citation block on our methodology page.

Do you use AI to generate your data?

No. All our data is sourced from real enquiries, interviews, and verified external sources. We use editorial tools to write content, but the numbers themselves are human‑verified.
