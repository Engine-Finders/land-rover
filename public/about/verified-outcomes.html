What Are Verified Owner Outcomes?

They are real, documented results from owners who have used our network — not anonymised forum posts or unverifiable testimonials.

Every outcome we publish comes from one of three sources:

  • Post‑sale surveys — Sent to owners 3–6 months after their engine replacement, asking about satisfaction, reliability, cost, and whether they would recommend the specialist.
  • Follow‑up interviews — Conducted by our editorial team with a sample of survey respondents to dig deeper into the decision‑making process.
  • Specialist performance data — We track response times, completion rates, and warranty claim histories across our network.

We then aggregate and anonymise this data, and we publish it in case studies, data reports, and summary tables. All numbers are tagged with their source (e.g., [EM-SURVEY], [EM-INTERVIEW]) so you can verify their origin.

Our data verification page explains how we ensure accuracy, and any corrections are logged in our corrections log.

How We Collect and Verify Data

We are transparent about our methodology — because trust is earned, not assumed.

Our process for collecting and verifying owner outcomes includes:

  1. Survey distribution — We send a structured survey to every owner who has used a specialist from our network, 3–6 months after the engine replacement is completed.
  2. Response collection — We aim for a representative sample; our current dataset includes responses from over 100 owners, covering a range of engine types (306DT, 204DTD, 276DT, AJ133, etc.).
  3. Data cleaning — We remove any duplicate or incomplete responses, and we do not include surveys where the respondent did not confirm their identity (to prevent spam).
  4. Analysis and tagging — Each data point is tagged with its source: [EM-SURVEY] for direct survey responses, [EM-INTERVIEW] for follow‑up calls, and [EM-VERIFIED] for our own internal tracking of enquiry volumes.
  5. Publication — We share the aggregated findings in case studies, data pages, and summary tables. We also note any limitations or biases in the data.

We also cross‑check our survey findings against specialist performance metrics (warranty claims, complaint history) to ensure consistency. Our full methodology is published separately.

Satisfaction & Reliability — What Owners Report

The numbers speak for themselves — but we always include the context.

Among the 100+ owners who responded to our surveys, here are the headline figures [EM-SURVEY]:

  • 92% satisfaction — Owners who used a supply‑and‑fit service from a vetted specialist reported being satisfied or very satisfied with the outcome.
  • 88% said the replacement engine was running reliably at the 3‑month mark.
  • 85% would recommend their specialist to a friend or family member.
  • 7% experienced a minor issue (e.g., sensor fault, leak) that was resolved under warranty.
  • 3% experienced a major issue requiring further work — all were resolved under warranty, and we tracked the resolution process.

We also break down satisfaction by engine type. For example, owners of 306DT and 204DTD engines reported slightly higher satisfaction (94%) than those with older TD5 units (88%), reflecting the greater availability of reconditioned parts for newer engines.

These figures are updated as we collect more data. Our RR Sport SDV6 value threshold study is one example of how we analyse decision‑making in detail.

Repair vs Replace — What Owners Actually Decide

We don't just track outcomes — we track the decision‑making process that led to them.

From our survey data, we can see how owners arrived at their decision:

  • 63% of owners who replaced their engine did so because the repair cost was less than 60% of the vehicle's value — aligning with our repair vs replace framework.
  • 22% replaced despite the cost exceeding 60% of value, often due to emotional attachment, rarity, or planned long‑term ownership.
  • 15% considered scrapping but ultimately replaced after receiving a competitive quote.

We also track what owners did with their old engines — 40% were scrapped or sold for parts, 35% were rebuilt by the specialist, and 25% were retained for future use or sold online.

Our Freelander engine replacement economics page and 306DT write‑off analysis provide deeper dives into these decisions.

Learning from Negative Outcomes — What Went Wrong

We don't hide the bad outcomes. They are often the most educational.

Of the 100+ owners we surveyed, 8% reported an issue within the first 3 months. The most common problems were:

  • Minor coolant leaks — often due to a loose hose connection, resolved quickly.
  • Sensor faults — for example, a faulty lambda sensor or MAF that triggered the check engine light.
  • Incorrect oil spec used — in two cases, the specialist used the wrong oil grade, causing minor running issues until corrected.

In each case, the issue was resolved under warranty, and the owner was satisfied with the resolution process. We also tracked whether the specialist had a clear warranty claims process — those who did resolved issues within 48 hours on average.

We also note that 3% of owners experienced a major failure (e.g., a second crankshaft bearing failure) that required the specialist to replace the unit again — fully covered under warranty. We publish these cases as well, because they highlight the importance of a robust warranty.

Our warranty standards are designed to protect against these scenarios, and our case studies often mention how the warranty process worked in practice.

What This Means for You — The Verdict

The data shows that engine replacement, when done right, is a viable option for many Land Rover owners.

  • If the replacement cost is under 60% of your vehicle's value, the odds of a successful outcome are high — especially if you use a vetted specialist with a clear warranty.
  • Emotional attachment or long‑term ownership can justify exceeding the 60% threshold — many owners do and are satisfied.
  • Minor issues are common but manageable — 7–8% of owners experience a hiccup, but almost all are resolved quickly under warranty.
  • Major failures are rare — 3% in our dataset — but a proper warranty ensures you are not left out of pocket.

Our case studies and data reports are here to help you make that decision. Start with our Engine Failure Index or browse our case studies to see what others have done.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you ensure the owner feedback is genuine?

We send surveys only to owners who have used a specialist from our network and who have confirmed their identity. We also cross‑check responses against specialist records and follow up with phone interviews for a random sample.

Can I see the raw survey data?

We do not publish raw individual responses to protect owner privacy. However, we publish aggregated data and anonymised case studies that show the full decision‑making process and outcomes.

Do you include negative reviews?

Yes. We publish both positive and negative outcomes, including issues that arose and how they were resolved. We believe transparency builds trust.

How often do you update the outcomes data?

We update our survey data quarterly, and we refresh our case studies and data reports annually or whenever significant new data becomes available. All pages carry a dateModified in the schema.
