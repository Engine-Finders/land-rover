What We Require From Every Specialist

We have a non-negotiable warranty standard that every specialist in our network must meet.

We require a minimum warranty period of 12 months on both the engine unit and the labour to fit it. The warranty must be:

  • In writing — A verbal assurance at the point of sale is not acceptable. The warranty must be a formal document with clear terms, exclusions, and a claims process.
  • Transferable — Where the vehicle is sold within the warranty period, the cover should transfer to the new owner (subject to the specialist's standard terms).
  • Backed by a clear process — The specialist must have a documented procedure for handling a warranty claim, and evidence of having used it.
  • Honoured without dispute — We check that specialists have a track record of honouring claims, not disputing them unfairly.

We also prefer specialists who offer extended warranties (e.g., 24 or 36 months) or who include additional cover for ancillary parts (e.g., turbos, injectors, EGR coolers) as part of the same warranty. These are not required, but they are a positive signal.

Our full specialist vetting process is published on our specialist vetting page.

Supply-Only vs Supply & Fit — Warranty Differences

The type of warranty you receive depends on whether you buy the engine only or have it fitted by the supplier.

We distinguish between two common arrangements:

  • Supply-only — You purchase a reconditioned or used engine from a specialist, but arrange your own fitting (or fit it yourself). In this case, the warranty typically covers the engine unit only, and the period may be shorter (e.g., 6–12 months) than a supply-and-fit arrangement.
  • Supply and fit — The specialist supplies and fits the engine. This is the arrangement we recommend for most owners, because the warranty covers both the unit and the labour. We require a minimum of 12 months for supply-and-fit, and we prefer specialists who offer longer.

Our supply and fit page explains the practical differences in more detail, including cost implications and what to look for when comparing quotes.

We also note that a supply-and-fit warranty is typically more valuable because it removes the risk of disputes between the engine supplier and the fitting garage. If the engine fails and the fitting is done by the same specialist, there is no finger-pointing — the responsibility is clear.

How We Verify Warranties Before Routing Enquiries

We don't take a specialist's word for it. We verify their warranty standards independently.

Our verification process includes:

  1. Review of the warranty document — We ask for a copy of the standard warranty terms and check them for clarity, exclusions, and the claims process.
  2. Track record check — We ask the specialist for examples of warranty claims they have handled in the past 12 months, and we follow up with a sample of customers to confirm that the claim was handled fairly and promptly.
  3. Third-party validation — Where possible, we cross‑reference against online reviews and industry feedback to see if there are recurring complaints about warranty disputes.
  4. Ongoing monitoring — We review warranty performance as part of our ongoing specialist review process. If a specialist's warranty record deteriorates, we reduce or stop routing enquiries to them.

We also check that the warranty is not full of unreasonable exclusions. A warranty that excludes half the possible failure modes is not a real warranty. Our editorial standards require us to flag any specialist who uses overly restrictive terms.

What We Do Not Accept

We have clear red lines that exclude a specialist from our network.

  • No written warranty — A verbal assurance, or a guarantee that is not documented, is an automatic disqualifier. We will not route enquiries to any specialist who cannot produce a written warranty.
  • Warranty of less than 12 months — We consider anything shorter than 12 months to be insufficient for a major investment like an engine replacement.
  • Warranty that covers the unit but not the labour — A supply-and-fit warranty that excludes labour leaves the owner exposed to significant costs if the engine fails. We do not accept this.
  • A pattern of disputed claims — If a specialist has a history of disputing legitimate warranty claims or delaying payments, they are removed from our network.

We also look out for warranties that are offered with so many exclusions that they are effectively worthless. If a warranty excludes "wear and tear" or "any failure caused by pre-existing conditions" without defining those terms, we treat it as a red flag.

Comparing Warranty Standards — What to Look For

When you compare quotes, don't just look at the price. Compare the warranty terms as well.

Here is what we recommend you look for when comparing warranty offers:

  • Duration — 12 months is the minimum we accept, but 24 or 36 months is better. Some specialists offer a tiered warranty (e.g., 12 months standard, 24 months on a premium option).
  • What is covered — Does the warranty cover the full engine assembly, or just specific components? Does it cover labour? Does it cover consequential damage (e.g., if a failed engine damages the cooling system)?
  • Exclusions — Look for reasonable exclusions (e.g., abuse, racing) versus unreasonable ones (e.g., "any internal failure after 6,000 miles"). We flag the latter.
  • Claims process — Is it clear how you make a claim? Does the specialist have a 24‑hour helpline, or do you need to send a letter? We prefer specialists with a simple, transparent process.
  • Transferability — If you sell the vehicle within the warranty period, can the warranty be transferred to the new owner? This adds value.

We also recommend reading the warranty document before you commit. If it is longer than two pages and full of legal jargon, ask for a plain‑English summary. If the specialist is reluctant to provide that, it is a red flag.

Why Warranty Standards Matter for Land Rover Engine Replacements

A proper warranty is not a nice‑to‑have — it is a fundamental protection against a second catastrophic failure.

Land Rover engines are complex, and even the best reconditioned units can fail due to factors outside the specialist's control — such as a pre‑existing oil contamination issue, a latent manufacturing defect in a supplied component, or an installation error. A good warranty protects you against those risks, and it also gives the specialist a financial incentive to do the job right the first time.

Our is it worth replacing a Range Rover engine and similar economics pages often refer back to the warranty standard as a key factor in the decision. For example, a £6,500 replacement engine with a 12‑month written warranty is a very different proposition from a £5,000 engine with a verbal "we'll look after you" promise.

We also believe that a strong warranty is a marker of a professional specialist. The best specialists are confident enough in their work to offer a long, clear warranty. The ones who try to minimise their exposure with restrictive terms are often the ones we avoid.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum warranty you accept?

We require a minimum of 12 months on both the engine unit and the labour to fit it. We consider anything shorter to be insufficient for a major investment.

Do you accept supply-only warranties?

Yes, we accept supply-only warranties, but we prefer supply-and-fit because it covers both the unit and the labour, reducing the risk of disputes between separate parties.

How do you verify a specialist's warranty?

We review the warranty document, check the specialist's claims history, speak to previous customers, and monitor ongoing performance. If we find a pattern of disputes, we reduce or stop routing enquiries to that specialist.

What if a specialist refuses to give me a written warranty?

We would not route enquiries to any specialist who refuses to provide a written warranty. If you encounter this, we recommend walking away — a verbal assurance is not enforceable.
