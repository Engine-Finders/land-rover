The Data

Sample: 148 Evoque L551 P300e owners who contacted us in 2025 with engine or powertrain-related enquiries [EM-VERIFIED]. We tracked the nature of their enquiry, the outcome, and post-enquiry satisfaction via follow‑up surveys conducted 3‑6 months later [EM-SURVEY].

Period: full calendar year 2025.

What this is not: this is not a claim about every Evoque P300e in the UK, only the pattern within our own customer base. Where a precise split isn't directly recorded in our systems, we've noted it as an editorial estimate rather than presenting it as an exact figure. Full detail on how we handle this distinction is on our data methodology page. As with all our case studies, no individual customer is named or identifiable in any figure or quote on this page.

What the Enquiries Actually Showed

The 148 enquiries split into two broad categories: charging system issues (62%) and petrol engine issues (38%). This is notably different from our pure‑ICE models, where engine‑related enquiries typically account for 80‑90% of the total. The high proportion of charging‑related enquiries reflects the PHEV‑specific learning curve — many owners were unfamiliar with the nuances of hybrid ownership, including the 12V battery, the onboard charger, and the interaction between the petrol engine and electric motor.

Among the engine‑related enquiries, the most common topics were coolant system issues (12% of total enquiries) and timing chain concerns (8%), consistent with the known 204PT Ingenium petrol engine characteristics. The remaining 18% covered sensors, software glitches, and general running issues.

Cost & Satisfaction

The cost to resolve an enquiry varied significantly by category. Charging system issues, which often involved a 12V battery replacement or a software update, averaged £400–£1,200 to resolve. Petrol engine issues, including coolant repairs and timing chain work, averaged £1,500–£3,000 [EM-QUOTE].

Post-enquiry satisfaction was 81% overall, with charging system enquiry owners reporting slightly higher satisfaction (84%) than engine enquiry owners (76%). The primary driver of dissatisfaction among engine enquiry owners was the cost of repairs and the availability of specialist parts — a theme we see consistently across the Ingenium range.

What Owners Actually Asked About

The free‑text enquiry descriptions gave us a clear picture of the top concerns:

  • 12V battery issues — "Car won't start, 12V battery warning" (common when the main hybrid battery drains the auxiliary battery) [EM-INTERVIEW]
  • Onboard charger faults — "Won't charge on Type 2" or "Charging interrupted" [EM-INTERVIEW]
  • Coolant system — "Coolant level low" or "Engine overheating" (typical of the 204PT petrol engine cooling system) [EM-INTERVIEW]
  • Timing chain — "Rattle on startup" (less common than on diesel Ingenium but still reported) [EM-INTERVIEW]
  • Software glitches — "Hybrid system error" or "Check engine light after charging" [EM-INTERVIEW]

Notably, few owners (less than 5%) asked about the electric motor itself — suggesting that the electric drive unit is proving relatively reliable in the P300e platform.

The Pattern: What the Data Reveals

"The P300e is a new kind of car for many owners — the enquiries are as much about understanding how it works as they are about actual faults."

The pattern that emerges from the 148 enquiries is one of education as much as repair. A significant proportion of enquiries (estimated 25‑30%) were informational rather than fault‑driven — owners seeking to understand why their car behaved differently to a pure‑ICE vehicle, particularly around the interaction between the petrol engine and electric motor. This is consistent with the PHEV learning curve and suggests that the P300e is a very different ownership experience to the diesel or petrol Evoque.

The petrol engine itself appears broadly reliable — the 38% engine enquiry rate is lower than the 60‑70% we typically see for pure‑ICE models of the same age — but the 204PT timing chain issue and coolant system are the areas to watch. The 12V battery and onboard charger are the most common PHEV‑specific pain points, and both are relatively inexpensive to address.

What This Means For You

If you're considering an Evoque P300e, this 148‑enquiry sample suggests several takeaways:

  • Expect a learning curve. Many P300e enquiries are about understanding how the hybrid system works, not fixing a fault.
  • The petrol engine is broadly reliable. Only 38% of enquiries were engine‑related, and the common issues (coolant, timing chain) are well‑understood and repairable.
  • Watch the 12V battery. It's the most common PHEV‑specific failure point and can be replaced relatively cheaply.
  • Software updates matter. Several enquiries were resolved with a dealer software update — make sure any used P300e has the latest firmware.

Use our registration lookup tool to check a vehicle's service history and any outstanding software updates, and read the full 204PT Knowledge Centre for detailed technical information on the petrol Ingenium engine.

How This Compares to Other Evoque Models

This 148‑enquiry P300e sample fits into a broader pattern visible across the Evoque range. The 38% engine enquiry rate is significantly lower than the 65‑75% we see on the diesel Evoque (D165, D180) and the 60‑70% on the petrol Evoque (P250). This suggests that the P300e powertrain, while introducing new PHEV‑specific complexity, may be less prone to engine‑related issues than its pure‑ICE siblings.

The charging system enquiries (62%) are unique to the P300e and reflect the different ownership experience. As this dataset grows year over year, we expect to be able to say more precisely whether the P300e's reliability pattern holds, or whether the current low engine enquiry rate is a function of the vehicle's relative youth.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Evoque P300e reliable?

Based on our first 148 enquiries, the P300e appears to be broadly reliable, with 62% of enquiries about charging system (mostly 12V battery and software) and only 38% about the petrol engine. The electric motor itself has generated very few enquiries.

What are the common issues with the P300e?

The most common issues are 12V battery failure (which can prevent the car from starting), onboard charger faults, and coolant system issues on the petrol engine. Timing chain wear has also been reported, though less frequently than on diesel Ingenium models.

Is the P300e more reliable than the diesel Evoque?

On the current data, yes. The P300e has a lower engine enquiry rate (38%) than diesel Evoque models (65‑75%). However, the P300e introduces PHEV‑specific complexity (charging system, 12V battery) that diesel owners don't face.

Should I buy a P300e or a diesel Evoque?

If you do mostly short journeys and can charge at home, the P300e is an excellent choice. If you do longer motorway miles, the diesel may be more fuel‑efficient. Both have their strengths — the P300e's lower engine enquiry rate is a point in its favour.
