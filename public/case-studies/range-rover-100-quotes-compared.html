The Data

Sample: 100 Range Rover owners (L405, L494, L322) who requested engine replacement quotes through our network in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED]. All quotes were supply‑and‑fit from UK independent specialists. We tracked the quoted price, the final accepted price, the engine type, the condition of the replacement unit (used, reconditioned, rebuilt), and post‑sale satisfaction via surveys conducted 3‑6 months later [EM-SURVEY].

Period: full calendar year 2025.

What this is not: this is not a comprehensive market analysis of every Range Rover engine quote in the UK. It is a pattern analysis of 100 real enquiries within our own network. Where precise splits are not directly recorded, we use editorial estimates clearly noted. Full methodology details are on our data methodology page.

What the Quotes Actually Showed

The average quoted cost across the 100 quotes was £6,200, but the range was significant: the lowest quote was £4,200, and the highest was £8,000 — a gap of £3,800. This variance was not random. It was driven by three primary factors: (1) the engine being replaced (SDV6, TDV8, or SDV8), (2) the condition of the replacement unit (used vs reconditioned vs rebuilt), and (3) the specialist's labour rate, which ranged from £65 to £110 per hour.

By engine type:

  • 306DT (SDV6/TDV6): £5,500–£7,500 (average £6,400) [EM-QUOTE]
  • 368DT (TDV8 3.6): £5,500–£8,000 (average £6,800) [EM-QUOTE]
  • 448DT / 508PS (4.4 SDV8): £6,000–£9,500 (average £7,800) [EM-QUOTE]

By unit condition: Reconditioned units (82% of accepted quotes) averaged £6,500 supply‑only; used units averaged £4,200; rebuilt units averaged £7,800 [EM-QUOTE].

What owners actually paid was often lower than the initial quote: the average final accepted price was £5,900, reflecting negotiation and comparison‑shopping. Owners who obtained at least three quotes saved an average of £1,100 compared to those who accepted the first quote [EM-SURVEY].

The Hidden Costs That Weren't Always in the Quote

Beyond the engine itself, owners in our sample faced additional costs that weren't always included in the initial quote. The most common were:

  • Ancillary parts: New belts, hoses, gaskets, and fluids added £300–£800 [EM-QUOTE]
  • Oil system flush: Essential after a bearing or turbo failure, £200–£400 [EM-QUOTE]
  • Recovery and towing: Up to £300 if the vehicle broke down away from home [EM-INTERVIEW]
  • Main dealer diagnostics: Some owners paid £150–£300 for a dealer diagnosis before seeking indie quotes [EM-INTERVIEW]
  • Warranty on the replacement: Typically included with reconditioned units, but some used engines came with only 30‑90 days [EM-INTERVIEW]

The total fitted cost, including these extras, averaged £6,500 — slightly above the initial quoted average of £6,200. Owners who asked for a "full fitted price" upfront avoided most of these surprises.

Why Quotes Vary: The Real Drivers

The £3,800 gap between the lowest and highest quotes wasn't arbitrary. Our analysis identified four key drivers:

  1. Engine type: SDV8 units are significantly more expensive than SDV6 — the 4.4 litre V8 requires more parts and labour.
  2. Replacement unit condition: Reconditioned units with warranties cost more upfront but offer peace of mind; used units are cheaper but carry higher risk.
  3. Specialist location: London and South East specialists charged 40‑60% more per hour than those in the Midlands and North.
  4. Inclusion of ancillaries: Some quotes included belts, pumps and fluids; others quoted only the engine itself, leaving the owner to pay extra later.

The lesson is clear: when comparing quotes, you must compare like‑for‑like. A £4,800 quote for a used engine with no warranty and no ancillaries may cost more in the long run than a £6,500 reconditioned unit with a full warranty and all ancillaries included.

What This Means For You

If you're facing a Range Rover engine replacement, this 100‑quote sample suggests three clear takeaways:

  • Get at least three quotes. Owners who did saved an average of £1,100.
  • Compare like‑for‑like. Make sure each quote includes the same scope of work — ancillaries, fluids, warranty, and labour.
  • Reconditioned is the most common choice. 82% of accepted quotes were for reconditioned units, reflecting the added confidence a warranty provides.

The Pattern: How Owners Actually Decided

"I got three quotes, and the middle one was the best — not the cheapest, but the specialist was the most thorough and the warranty was better."

Owners in our sample didn't simply choose the cheapest quote. The decision pattern was more nuanced:

  • 58% chose the second‑cheapest quote, prioritising specialist reputation and warranty over the lowest price [EM-SURVEY]
  • 22% chose the cheapest quote, primarily on lower‑value vehicles where cost was the overriding factor [EM-SURVEY]
  • 20% chose the most expensive quote, driven by specialist pedigree or a preference for a specific type of replacement unit (e.g., a genuine JLR‑sourced engine) [EM-SURVEY]

Satisfaction mirrored this: owners who chose the second‑cheapest quote reported the highest satisfaction (94%), followed by those who chose the most expensive (88%) and then the cheapest (72%). The lowest‑cost group were more likely to report post‑replacement issues, consistent with the higher risk of used or low‑spec units.

Where the Minority Were Right

The 18% who didn't choose reconditioned weren't making a mistake. Owners who chose used engines were overwhelmingly on L322 models with lower vehicle values, where a reconditioned unit's cost would have pushed the repair‑to‑value ratio close to or above the 60% threshold [PATTERN DATA]. A used engine, even with a shorter warranty, kept the decision financially viable. Owners who chose a rebuild were usually on high‑value L405 or L494 models with documented service histories, where the rebuild was a strategic choice to preserve originality.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the average cost of a Range Rover engine replacement in the UK?

Based on our 100‑quote sample, the average quoted cost was £6,200, with a range of £4,200–£8,000. The final paid price averaged £5,900 after negotiation and comparison‑shopping.

Why do quotes vary so much?

Quotes vary due to engine type (SDV6, TDV8, SDV8), unit condition (used, reconditioned, rebuilt), specialist location (London/South East vs Midlands/North), and whether ancillaries and labour are included. Always compare like‑for‑like.

Is it worth getting multiple quotes?

Yes. Owners who obtained at least three quotes saved an average of £1,100 compared to those who accepted the first quote.

Should I choose the cheapest quote?

Not necessarily. The cheapest quote often comes with a shorter warranty or a used unit with no ancillaries. The second‑cheapest quote was the most popular choice (58%) and had the highest satisfaction rate (94%).
