The Verdict

The L663 is the better vehicle for 95% of buyers. It's safer, more comfortable, more reliable, and much better on the road. The classic Defender is a specialist tool — wonderful for those who value its character, simplicity, and off‑road purity, but it's a compromise in every other area. Our 2025 data shows the L663 is in high demand, reflecting its broad appeal. If you can afford the L663 and need a daily driver, it's the obvious choice. If you're looking for a project, a weekend toy, or a true off‑road machine, the classic remains peerless.

Classic Defender Deep Dive

The classic Defender (L316 and earlier, built until 2016) is a utilitarian icon. Its ladder‑frame chassis, live axles, and simple mechanicals make it supremely capable off‑road and straightforward to repair. Engines like the 300Tdi and TD5 are robust but ageing; the TD5, in particular, has known injector loom issues [PATTERN DATA]. Replacement engines are relatively cheap — a used TD5 can be found for £1,500–£3,000, and a 300Tdi for less — but the rest of the vehicle may be in worse condition. Corrosion is the biggest enemy, and a thorough inspection is essential. The classic Defender's value has risen sharply in recent years, making it a potential investment, but running costs can be high due to age‑related maintenance. For a deep dive into the TD5 engine, visit our TD5 Knowledge Centre, and for the 300Tdi, the 300Tdi Knowledge Centre. If you're considering a classic, our Defender Economics Guide is essential reading.

L663 Deep Dive

The L663 Defender (2020–present) is a thoroughly modern SUV, built on a unibody platform with independent suspension. It offers exceptional on‑road comfort, impressive off‑road capability, and a wide range of powertrains, including the 204DTD 2.0‑litre diesel (D200/D240) and the AJ20D6 3.0‑litre straight‑six (D250/D300). The 204DTD has a documented pre‑2019 timing chain tensioner weakness [PATTERN DATA], but this was revised from 2019. Replacement costs for the 204DTD are £3,500–£5,800 supply and fit [EM-QUOTE], which is relatively affordable for a modern Land Rover. The L663's electronic complexity can be a drawback for DIY enthusiasts, but its reliability and safety are in a different league from the classic. Full engine specs and reliability ratings are on our 204DTD Knowledge Centre and AJ20D6 Knowledge Centre. Our Engine Failure Index 2026 provides context on how the L663's engines compare to others in the range.

Ownership Costs Compared

The biggest cost difference is the purchase price — a used L663 typically starts around £35,000, while a classic Defender can be found for £10,000–£30,000 depending on condition. Running costs are also different: the L663 is more fuel‑efficient (around 30–35 mpg for the 204DTD) than the classic (TD5 around 25 mpg), but the L663's insurance and servicing are more expensive. Engine replacement costs are higher for the L663, but the classic may need more frequent repairs due to age. The 60% threshold [EM-INTERVIEW] applies to both: for a classic Defender valued at £15,000, a £4,000 engine replacement is 27% of value — a clear ✅ Safe decision. For an L663 valued at £40,000, a £5,800 replacement is 14.5% — also ✅ Safe. The real cost difference is in the ancillary repairs — the classic will need chassis welding and rust treatment, which can add thousands. Which Should You Buy?

🏞️

Serious Off‑Roader / Overlander

Choose the classic Defender. Its live axles, ground clearance, and simple mechanicals make it more repairable in remote locations. The L663 is capable, but its electronics and independent suspension are more complex and harder to fix in the bush.

🚙

Daily Driver / Family Car

Go L663. It's safer, quieter, more comfortable, and has modern infotainment and safety aids. The classic Defender is noisy, drafty, and lacks basic features like airbags on early models.

💰

Collector / Weekend Toy

Classic Defender all the way. Values are rising, and it's a joy to own and drive for fun. Budget for maintenance, but you're buying an icon, not a transport appliance.

🔁

Buying to Keep Long‑Term

If you plan to keep the vehicle for a decade or more, consider the L663 for its durability and parts availability. The classic will require ongoing restoration work, but if you're handy with a spanner, it can be a lifelong project.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is more reliable, classic Defender or L663?

The L663 is generally more reliable due to modern engineering and quality control. Classic Defenders are simple but age‑related issues like corrosion, electrical gremlins, and worn mechanicals are common. With proper maintenance, both can be reliable, but the L663 requires less constant attention.

Is the classic Defender cheaper to run than the L663?

Not necessarily. While parts are cheaper and engines are simpler to work on, the classic Defender will need more frequent repairs, especially for rust and suspension components. The L663 has higher service costs but fewer unexpected failures.

Which is better off‑road?

Both are exceptional. The classic Defender's live axles and shorter overhangs give it an advantage on extreme terrain, but the L663's Terrain Response 2 and electronic diffs make it easier to drive off‑road, especially for less experienced drivers. For hardcore overlanding, the classic is preferred for repairability.

Which holds its value better?

Both hold value well, but the classic Defender has seen dramatic price appreciation in recent years, making it a potential investment. The L663 is also strong, but its depreciation curve is more typical of a modern SUV.

Can I use a classic Defender as a daily driver?

Yes, but it's a compromise. It's noisy, not very comfortable, lacks modern safety features, and fuel economy is poor. Many owners do use them daily, but the L663 is far better suited to the role.
