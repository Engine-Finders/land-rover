Two iconic Land Rover nameplates, head to head. One is a rugged off‑roader with a cult following; the other is a spacious family hauler. We compare real 2025 UK enquiry data, engine reliability, running costs, and which one actually makes sense for you.

If you want a genuine off‑roader with modern comforts, buy the Defender. If you need to carry a family and their luggage in comfort, the Discovery is the better tool. Our 2025 data shows the Defender L663 generated 292 enquiries, while the Discovery 4 L319 generated 120 [EM-VERIFIED] — but both nameplates have loyal followings. The Defender is more expensive to buy, but its engine line‑up (largely 204DTD and the newer straight‑six) is generally cheaper to replace than the older 306DT V6 often found in Discovery 4. For most buyers, the Defender offers stronger residual values and a more distinctive character, while the Discovery gives you more space for the money.

The Verdict

The Defender is the more desirable and better‑retaining choice for anyone who values off‑road capability and a distinctive look. It generated more than double the enquiries of the Discovery 4 in our 2025 data, and its 204DTD engine is cheaper to replace than the V6 diesel commonly found in older Discoveries. That said, the Discovery makes more sense if you regularly carry five people and their luggage — the interior space and load area are significantly larger, and the ride comfort is more compliant. For buyers on a tighter budget, a well‑maintained Discovery 4 offers a huge amount of car for the money, provided you budget for the 306DT’s known risks.

Defender Deep Dive

The current Defender L663 is a modern evolution of the classic, with independent suspension, full‑time four‑wheel drive, and a wide range of powertrains. Most UK enquiries relate to the 2.0‑litre 204DTD diesel (D200/D240) and the 3.0‑litre straight‑six (D250/D300). The 204DTD is shared with many other JLR models and benefits from a large pool of used and reconditioned units. Its main documented weakness is pre‑2019 timing chain tensioner wear [PATTERN DATA], but this was substantially revised. The straight‑six is generally robust, though early examples can suffer from AdBlue system issues. Replacement cost for a 204DTD is around £3,500–£5,800 supply and fit [EM-QUOTE], making it relatively affordable for a modern Land Rover. Full engine details and reliability ratings are on the 204DTD Knowledge Centre and the AJ20D6 Knowledge Centre. If you’re looking at a used Defender, we highly recommend checking the service history and avoiding early‑build 204DTD units without a documented chain replacement.

Discovery Deep Dive

The Discovery nameplate covers the older Discovery 4 (L319) and the current Discovery 5 (L462). The Discovery 4 is a beloved workhorse, powered predominantly by the 276DT (2.7 V6) and 306DT (3.0 V6) diesels. These V6 engines are known for their torque and refinement, but the 306DT carries a well‑documented crankshaft bearing failure risk, typically occurring between 80,000–140,000 miles [PATTERN DATA]. Reconditioning a 306DT costs £6,500–£9,500 [EM-QUOTE], and labour is higher due to the V6 layout (15–20 hours). The Discovery 5 moved to the 204DTD and later the straight‑six, so it shares the cheaper engine options with the Defender. However, our 2025 data shows only 120 enquiries for the Discovery 4 — likely reflecting its age and the shift to the newer model. For families, the Discovery’s seven‑seat option and cavernous boot are unmatched by the Defender. Full details on the 306DT can be found on the 306DT Knowledge Centre, and the 306DT Crankshaft Bearing Failure page covers the repair decision in depth.

Ownership Costs Compared

Beyond the purchase price, the main cost difference is engine replacement. A Defender with a 204DTD is cheaper to fix than a Discovery 4 with a 306DT — roughly £3,500–£5,800 vs £6,500–£9,500. However, the Defender is significantly more expensive to buy used, so the total cost of ownership over five years may be closer than it first appears. Fuel economy favours the Defender’s smaller four‑cylinder (around 35–40 mpg real‑world) against the V6’s 28–32 mpg, saving roughly £500–£800 per year in fuel. Insurance and road tax are also slightly higher for the Discovery’s larger engine. Both models suffer from similar electronic gremlins and suspension wear, so a full pre‑purchase inspection is essential. For the 306DT, the Discovery Economics Guide breaks down whether a replacement is worth it based on vehicle value — our 60% threshold [EM-INTERVIEW] is a good starting point.

Which Should You Buy?

🏞️

Off‑Road Enthusiast / Overlander

Choose the Defender. Its ground clearance, approach/departure angles, and Terrain Response 2 system make it the more capable machine out of the box. The 204DTD is adequately torquey and cheaper to repair if things go wrong.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Family Hauler / Daily Driver

Pick the Discovery. The extra rear legroom, boot space (up to 2,500 litres with seats down) and seven‑seat option are unbeatable. If you go for a Discovery 5 with the 204DTD, you get the best of both worlds: space and a lower‑cost engine.

💰

Budget‑Conscious Buyer

A well‑maintained Discovery 4 with the 306DT can be a bargain, but only if you set aside £6,500+ for a potential engine failure. If that risk is too high, a later Discovery 5 or a used Defender with a proven history is safer, albeit pricier upfront.

🔁

Buying to Keep Long‑Term

Either model can reach high mileages with care. The Defender’s modern electronics are more complex, but the 204DTD is simpler than the V6. We’d lean towards the Defender for longevity, provided you avoid early 204DTD units and stick to post‑2019 builds.

Whichever you choose, the repair vs replace framework and the ownership cost calculator can help you decide whether to fix or move on when the bills arrive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is more reliable, Defender or Discovery?

It depends on the engine. The Defender’s 204DTD (post‑2019) is generally more reliable than the Discovery 4’s 306DT V6, which has a known crankshaft bearing failure risk. However, the Discovery 5 shares the same 204DTD as the Defender, so reliability is similar. The Defender’s electronic complexity can introduce other gremlins, but overall, both are capable of high mileages with proper maintenance.

Is the Defender more expensive to run than the Discovery?

Usually, yes – the Defender is more expensive to buy and insure, and its parts are often pricier. However, the 204DTD engine is cheaper to replace than the 306DT V6, so running costs can balance out. Fuel economy favours the Defender’s four‑cylinder, saving you money at the pump compared to the V6 Discovery 4.

Which is better off‑road?

The Defender is the more capable off‑roader, with better approach, departure, and breakover angles, plus superior wading depth. The Discovery is still very capable, but its longer wheelbase and softer suspension make it less agile on extreme terrain. For green‑laning and overlanding, the Defender is the clear winner.

Which should I buy for towing?

Both can tow up to 3,500 kg when properly equipped. The Defender’s shorter overhangs and Terrain Response give it an edge on uneven ground, while the Discovery’s longer wheelbase makes it more stable on the motorway. If you tow frequently, we’d lean towards the Defender for its off‑road stability when launching a boat or caravan on a muddy site.
