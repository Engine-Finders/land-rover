The Verdict

The Discovery 5 is the stronger choice for nearly every buyer. It's more refined, safer, and its post‑2019 Ingenium diesel options are significantly more reliable than the D4's ageing V6s. The D4 is a fantastic vehicle — capable, charismatic, and affordable — but its 306DT crankshaft bearing risk is a genuine financial time bomb. In our data, the D4's 120 enquiries [EM-VERIFIED] reflect its age and the fact that many owners are now facing expensive repair decisions, not that it's a better vehicle. If you can stretch to a D5, the extra comfort, modern tech, and lower long‑term risk make it worth the premium. The D4 only makes sense if you're a serious off‑roader on a tight budget who accepts the risks.

Discovery 4 Deep Dive

The Discovery 4 (L319, 2009‑2016) is the last of the traditional body‑on‑frame Discoveries. It's rugged, capable, and offers seven seats with excellent visibility. Its engines are the 276DT 2.7‑litre V6 diesel and the more powerful 306DT 3.0‑litre V6 diesel (sold as TDV6 and SDV6). These are well‑known units, but both carry risks: the 276DT has oil pump housing failures that can kill the engine, and the 306DT is infamous for catastrophic crankshaft bearing failure between 80,000 and 140,000 miles [PATTERN DATA] — full detail on our 306DT Crankshaft Bearing Failure page. Reconditioned supply for the 306DT is £6,500–£9,500 [EM-QUOTE] with 15–20 hours of labour. On a D4 worth £6,000–£18,000, that puts many examples into 🔴 Scrap Threshold territory. The D4 is still a capable off‑roader and a classic, but you need to go in with your eyes wide open. Full specs and fitment on the 306DT Knowledge Centre and 276DT Knowledge Centre.

Discovery 5 Deep Dive

The Discovery 5 (2017‑present) moved to a monocoque platform, dramatically improving on‑road refinement, ride quality, and interior space. It's a more comfortable, quieter, and more family‑friendly vehicle. Engine choices evolved: early cars used the 306DT V6, but the majority are powered by the 2.0‑litre 204DTD Ingenium diesel, or later the 3.0‑litre AJ20D6 straight‑six (a more robust unit). The 204DTD's pre‑2019 timing chain risk is well documented [PATTERN DATA], but it was substantially revised from 2019, making it a much safer bet than the 306DT. Reconditioned 204DTD costs are £3,500–£5,800 [EM-QUOTE], much cheaper than the V6. The D5 also offers a more modern interior, better safety tech, and stronger residual values. It's the better all‑rounder for families and daily use. Full details on the 204DTD Knowledge Centre and the AJ20D6 Knowledge Centre.

Five‑Year Ownership Cost Comparison

The cost difference over five years is stark. The D4 is cheaper to buy — typical values are £6,000–£18,000 — but the probability of a major engine failure (particularly the 306DT crankshaft) is high, and a replacement can cost £7,000–£10,000 fitted, often exceeding the vehicle's value. Many D4 owners are forced to scrap or sell their vehicles when the engine fails. In contrast, the D5's higher purchase price (£35,000–£65,000) is offset by significantly lower failure rates, especially on post‑2019 204DTD units. Routine servicing costs are similar, but the D5's more modern platform also means fewer suspension and drivetrain issues. Over five years, the D5 often works out cheaper to own, despite the higher entry cost. Our repair vs replace framework can help you decide if a D4 failure is worth fixing.

Which Should You Buy?

💰

Budget‑Conscious Buyer (Under £15k)

If your budget is tight, the Discovery 4 is your only option. Focus on post‑2012 cars with a full service history and, if possible, evidence of a recent engine rebuild or crankshaft work.

🛡️

Reliability & Family Use

Discovery 5, without question. The post‑2019 204DTD is reliable, the interior is vastly superior, and the safety tech is a generation ahead. Worth every penny of the premium.

🏔️

Serious Off‑Road Enthusiast

Discovery 4 — its body‑on‑frame construction and superior approach/departure angles make it the more capable off‑roader. Just budget for the engine risk.

🔁

Buying to Keep Long‑Term

Discovery 5 — it's more likely to reach 150,000+ miles without a major engine failure, and its modern platform will age better than the D4's.

To be clear: the Discovery 4 is not a bad vehicle — it's a legend for a reason. But the Discovery 5 is a more modern, more reliable, and more comfortable vehicle for 2026. Frequently Asked Questions

Which Discovery is more reliable, 4 or 5?

The Discovery 5 is more reliable, particularly with the post‑2019 204DTD diesel. The Discovery 4's 306DT V6 has a well‑documented crankshaft bearing failure risk, while the 276DT suffers from oil pump issues. The D5's Ingenium engines are simply a more modern design.

Is the Discovery 5 better off‑road than the Discovery 4?

No — the Discovery 4's body‑on‑frame construction gives it better articulation and durability for serious off‑roading. The Discovery 5 is still very capable, but it's designed more for on‑road comfort and occasional off‑road use.

What engines are in the Discovery 4 and Discovery 5?

Discovery 4: 276DT 2.7 V6 diesel and 306DT 3.0 V6 diesel. Discovery 5: early cars used the 306DT, but most have the 204DTD 2.0 diesel or the later AJ20D6 3.0 straight‑six diesel.

Is the Discovery 5 worth the extra money over the Discovery 4?

For most buyers, yes — the D5 offers vastly superior refinement, interior quality, safety, and reliability. The D4 is only worth considering if you're on a very tight budget or need the ultimate off‑road capability.
