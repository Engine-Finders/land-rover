The Verdict

The facelift is the better choice for most buyers. It addresses the pre‑facelift's most significant mechanical weakness – the 204DTD timing chain tensioner risk – and adds a more modern interior, improved infotainment, and the option of a petrol or PHEV powertrain. The pre‑facelift is still a good car, and its lower purchase price makes it attractive for budget‑conscious buyers, but you must budget for the timing chain risk on early (pre‑2019) examples. The facelift's revised engine is generally more reliable, and the petrol options (204PT) are even better. For most buyers, the facelift is worth the extra money; for those on a tight budget, a well‑maintained pre‑facelift with a documented chain replacement can still be a sensible choice.

Pre‑Facelift Deep Dive

The pre‑facelift Discovery Sport L550 (2014‑2019) was Land Rover's entry‑level SUV, offering seven seats and genuine off‑road capability. Most UK examples are powered by the 204DTD 2.0‑litre Ingenium diesel, with earlier cars using the Ford‑sourced 224DT 2.2 diesel. The 204DTD's main documented weakness is timing chain tensioner failure on pre‑2019 builds [PATTERN DATA], which can lead to catastrophic engine damage if ignored. Typical mileage for this failure is 60,000–120,000 miles. Replacement cost for a full engine is £3,500–£5,800 [EM-QUOTE], making it relatively affordable compared to V6 engines, but still a significant outlay. The pre‑facelift's interior is functional but dated compared to the facelift, with an older infotainment system and fewer driver aids. However, its lower purchase price (often £2,000–£4,000 less than a facelift) makes it appealing for budget buyers. Full engine specs on the 204DTD Knowledge Centre and 224DT Knowledge Centre.

Facelift Deep Dive

The facelift Discovery Sport (2019‑present) brought significant updates: a revised 204DTD diesel with an improved timing chain tensioner, a new 204PT 2.0‑litre petrol engine, and the P300e plug‑in hybrid option. The diesel's timing chain risk is substantially reduced from 2019 [EM-INTERVIEW], making it a more reliable long‑term proposition. The facelift also features a new interior with the Pivi Pro infotainment system, a digital instrument cluster, and improved driver assistance features. The 204PT petrol is generally more reliable than the diesel, with only minor cooling system issues [PATTERN DATA]. The P300e PHEV offers electric‑only range for urban driving, but adds complexity and weight. Replacement costs are similar to the pre‑facelift (£3,500–£5,800 for the 204DTD), but the facelift's higher purchase price is offset by better resale values. Full engine specs on the 204DTD Knowledge Centre, 204PT Knowledge Centre, and the 204PT Knowledge Centre.

Ownership Costs Compared

The facelift is more expensive to buy – typically £2,000–£4,000 more than a comparable pre‑facelift. Running costs are broadly similar for same‑engine examples, but the facelift's revised diesel may be slightly more fuel‑efficient (around 1–2 mpg better). Insurance is marginally higher for the facelift due to its higher value. Engine replacement costs are the same (£3,500–£5,800), but the facelift's lower risk of failure makes it a safer long‑term bet. The facelift also offers petrol and PHEV options, which may have lower running costs for urban drivers. The 60% threshold [EM-INTERVIEW] applies to both: for a pre‑facelift valued at £10,000, a £5,800 replacement is 58% – a ⚠️ Watch decision; for a facelift valued at £14,000, a £5,800 replacement is 41% – ✅ Safe. Which Should You Buy?

💰

Budget‑Conscious Buyer

Choose the pre‑facelift. It's significantly cheaper to buy and still offers the same space and capability. Just budget for a timing chain inspection or replacement on pre‑2019 examples, and factor this into your purchase price.

🔧

Reliability‑Focused Buyer

Pick the facelift. The revised 204DTD (post‑2019) is much more reliable, and the 204PT petrol offers even fewer issues. The facelift's newer technology also gives you better peace of mind.

🚙

Urban Driver / Tech Lover

The facelift's Pivi Pro infotainment, digital dash, and PHEV option make it the better choice for city dwellers. The petrol or PHEV models are also better suited to short trips where diesel DPF issues can be a problem.

🔁

Long‑Term Ownership

The facelift offers better long‑term value, with stronger resale values and lower risk of major engine failure. If you plan to keep the car for 5+ years, the facelift is the wiser investment.

Whichever you choose, the repair vs replace framework and the ownership cost calculator can help you decide whether to fix or move on when the bills arrive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the facelift Discovery Sport more reliable than the pre‑facelift?

Yes, the facelift's revised 204DTD diesel (post‑2019) addresses the pre‑facelift's timing chain tensioner weakness. The facelift also offers petrol and PHEV options, which are generally more reliable than the diesel. Overall, the facelift is a more reliable car.

Which is cheaper to run, pre‑facelift or facelift?

Running costs are broadly similar for same‑engine examples. The facelift's revised diesel may be slightly more fuel‑efficient, and the petrol/PHEV options can be cheaper for urban drivers. However, the facelift's higher purchase price is the main cost difference.

Should I avoid the pre‑facelift diesel?

Not necessarily – many pre‑facelift diesels are perfectly reliable, especially if they've had the timing chain replaced. However, you should budget for this risk and have a pre‑purchase inspection carried out by a specialist.

Which has better resale value?

The facelift holds its value better due to its newer technology, improved reliability, and more modern interior. If resale is important to you, the facelift is the better choice.
