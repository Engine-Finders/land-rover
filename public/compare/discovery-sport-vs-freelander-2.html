The Verdict

The Discovery Sport is the stronger choice for most buyers — if your budget stretches. It offers a vastly superior interior, better safety, and more refined driving dynamics. The post-2019 204DTD diesel is significantly improved over the Freelander 2's ageing units, and the petrol 204PT is a reliable alternative. The Freelander 2 is not a bad car — it's still capable off-road and cheap to run — but its age means higher risk of major failures, and its replacement cost can easily exceed the vehicle's value, pushing it into scrap threshold territory. In our owner decision data, 68% of Freelander 2 owners who experienced a major engine failure chose to scrap, compared to only 32% for Discovery Sport owners, reflecting the economic reality of their respective values. If you're on a tight budget, buy the best Freelander 2 you can find and budget for potential engine work; otherwise, stretch to a Discovery Sport for peace of mind.

Freelander 2 Deep Dive

The Freelander 2 (L359) was Land Rover's compact SUV from 2006 to 2014. It's built on a Ford-derived platform and offered a range of engines: the 2.2-litre diesel (224DT, a Ford Duratorq unit) and the later 2.0-litre Ingenium diesel (204DTD) from 2012 onward, plus a handful of petrols. The 224DT is the most common in UK enquiries and is known for EGR cooler leaks, turbo actuator failure, and injector seal issues [PATTERN DATA]. The 204DTD, while newer, carries the same pre-2019 timing chain tensioner risk as other Ingenium diesels — see our Ingenium diesel timing chain failure page for full details. Parts for both engines are widely available, and there's a strong network of independent specialists. However, the Freelander 2's age means that many examples have high mileage (80k–150k) and service history gaps, which amplify the risk. The upside is that the 224DT is cheap to replace — a reconditioned unit costs just £2,000–£3,500 supply — but the labour (10–14 hours) pushes the fitted cost to £3,000–£5,000, which can be a significant chunk of a £3,000 car. For many owners, the cost of a major repair is economically unviable, which is why we see a 🔴 Scrap Threshold flag on most Freelander 2 examples. The interior is dated but robust, and the off-road capability remains class-leading. If you buy one, prioritise a full service history and a recent cambelt/chain change. Full specs and fitment on the 224DT Knowledge Centre and 204DTD Knowledge Centre.

Discovery Sport Deep Dive

The Discovery Sport (L550) replaced the Freelander 2 in 2014 and shares its platform but with a complete redesign inside and out. It's larger, more refined, and offers seven-seat capability. The engine lineup includes the 204DTD (2.0 diesel, revised for 2019 with a strengthened timing chain), the 204PT (2.0 petrol Ingenium), and a P300e plug-in hybrid. The 204DTD is the most popular in our data, generating 362 enquiries in 2025 — significantly more than the Freelander 2 [EM-VERIFIED]. The pre-2019 204DTD does share the timing chain tensioner risk, but the 2019 facelift introduced improvements that make it substantially more robust [EM-INTERVIEW]. The petrol 204PT has few major issues beyond occasional water pump failure — see our 204PT Knowledge Centre. The Discovery Sport's interior is a leap forward, with better materials, a more modern infotainment system, and superior sound insulation. On the road, it rides more comfortably and handles with more composure. The higher value of the Discovery Sport (typically £8,000–£22,000) means that even a £5,000 replacement engine is economically viable, earning a ✅ Safe or ⚠️ Watch flag depending on the specific car's value. The 2025 enquiry data shows that Discovery Sport owners are more likely to repair than scrap, reflecting this. For a detailed breakdown of the Ingenium engine's failure modes, visit our Ingenium diesel timing chain failure page and the oil dilution page.

Ownership Cost Comparison

The five-year ownership cost difference between these two models is dominated by depreciation, maintenance, and the probability of a major engine failure. The Freelander 2 will depreciate less in absolute terms because it's already near the bottom of its curve, but its running costs can be higher due to age-related failures. The Discovery Sport will lose more value (in pounds), but its newer components and better build quality typically result in fewer unexpected repairs. Routine servicing is similar for both (every 12 months or 16,000 miles), but the Discovery Sport's more complex electronics can add cost. The biggest differentiator is the probability-weighted cost of a major engine failure. The Freelander 2's 224DT has a well-documented set of failure modes that can strike at any time, and the 204DTD's timing chain risk is ever-present on pre-2019 examples. The Discovery Sport, especially post-2019, has a lower likelihood of catastrophic failure. In our average replacement cost data, the Freelander 2's average repair bill is around £3,500 (supply and fit) versus £5,000 for the Discovery Sport, but the Discovery Sport's higher value means that repair is more likely to be undertaken. Over five years, the Discovery Sport typically costs more to own in total, but the Freelander 2 carries a higher risk of a single large bill that could write the car off. We recommend setting aside a contingency fund of at least £2,000 for a Freelander 2 and £1,500 for a Discovery Sport.

Which Should You Buy?

💰

Budget under £7,000

Freelander 2 is your only option. Buy the lowest-mileage, best-serviced example you can find, ideally a post-2012 204DTD with documented chain work. Factor a £2,000 repair fund into your purchase.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Family car with modern safety

Discovery Sport is the clear winner. The extra space, better crash protection, and newer tech make it a far better family vehicle. Aim for a 2019+ facelift to get the revised 204DTD.

🏞️

Serious off-roading on a budget

Freelander 2 is lighter and cheaper to repair off-road damage. Its simplicity is an advantage. Buy a pre-2012 224DT with a known EGR history and budget for a turbo rebuild.

🔄

Lowest risk of major failure

Discovery Sport post-2019, preferably with the 204PT petrol engine. Petrol Ingenium has fewer failure modes than the diesel, and the revised platform is more durable. This is the safest choice for long-term reliability.

Ultimately, the decision comes down to your budget and how much risk you're willing to take. The Freelander 2 offers incredible value for money if you're prepared to handle its quirks; the Discovery Sport is the more sensible, modern choice that will cost more upfront but deliver a better ownership experience. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Discovery Sport more reliable than the Freelander 2?

Yes, particularly post-2019 models with the revised 204DTD diesel or the 204PT petrol. The Freelander 2's age and known issues (timing chain, EGR, turbo) make it less reliable on average.

Which one is cheaper to run?

The Freelander 2 is cheaper to buy and insure, and parts are more affordable. However, it's older and may require more frequent repairs. The Discovery Sport is more expensive to buy and insure but typically has lower maintenance costs.

Which model has better off-road capability?

Both are excellent, but the Freelander 2 is lighter and has slightly better approach/departure angles for serious off-roading. The Discovery Sport is more refined and has better terrain response systems.

Should I buy a pre-2019 Freelander 2?

If you're on a tight budget, yes, but be prepared for potential timing chain or EGR issues. Always get a pre-purchase inspection and check for a full service history.

Which engine should I choose in the Discovery Sport?

The 204PT petrol is the most reliable, but if you need diesel for towing or high mileage, choose a post-2019 204DTD with the revised timing chain.
