Evoque L538 vs L551

The L551 Evoque is the better buy for most buyers — it benefits from the revised Ingenium engines (post-2019), a more modern interior, and better reliability. The L538 can be a great value proposition if you're on a tighter budget, but you must budget for the pre-2019 timing chain risk on diesel models and be prepared for older tech.

The Verdict

For the vast majority of buyers, the L551 is the smarter choice. It generates more enquiries in our data (349 vs 244 [EM-VERIFIED]), reflecting its popularity and the market's preference for the newer model. The L551's revised Ingenium engines are genuinely more robust — the post‑2019 204DTD has addressed the timing chain weakness, and the petrol 204PT is a reliable, modern option. The interior and tech are also a significant step up. The L538 can still be a good buy if your budget is tight, but you must go in with your eyes open about the timing chain risk and the older platform. If you can stretch to an L551, it's worth the extra outlay.

L538 Deep Dive

The L538 was the original Evoque, launched in 2011 and running until 2018. It was a style icon and sold in huge numbers. Most UK‑spec L538s are powered by the 204DTD 2.0‑litre diesel (pre‑2019 variant) or the Ford‑sourced 224DT 2.2‑litre diesel (used in earlier cars). Petrol options (2.0 Si4) are rarer.

The pre‑2019 204DTD is well‑known for timing chain tensioner failure, typically between 60,000 and 120,000 miles [PATTERN DATA]. This is a costly repair — chain replacement alone can be £1,200–£2,500, and a full engine replacement can hit £4,500–£6,500 [EM-QUOTE]. The 224DT engine also has known EGR cooler and turbo actuator issues, though these are generally less catastrophic. Many L538s are now approaching or past 100,000 miles, so a thorough pre‑purchase inspection is essential. Our 204PT Timing Chain Failure page covers the chain issue in detail (though that's for the petrol, the diesel chain issue is similar). Full engine specs and reliability ratings are on the 204DTD Knowledge Centre and 224DT Knowledge Centre.

Despite these risks, the L538 offers a stylish, capable SUV at a much lower entry price than the L551. If you can find a well‑maintained example with a full service history and (ideally) evidence of a timing chain replacement, it can be a bargain. Just budget for the potential repair.

L551 Deep Dive

The L551 (2019‑present) is the second‑generation Evoque, built on a new platform and featuring a more modern interior, improved infotainment, and crucially, revised Ingenium engines. The diesel 204DTD was updated from 2019 with a stronger timing chain design, making it significantly more reliable. The petrol 204PT is a smooth, torquey unit that avoids the DPF/EGR issues of the diesel, and its main weak point is the water pump and cooling system — a much cheaper fix than a timing chain.

The L551 also introduced the P300e plug‑in hybrid, which combines a 1.5‑litre three‑cylinder engine with an electric motor — it's a growing presence in our enquiry data (148 requests for the L551 PHEV [EM-VERIFIED]). The PHEV offers excellent fuel economy for urban drivers and low Benefit‑in‑Kind tax, making it a popular company car choice.

Overall, the L551 is a more rounded, reliable, and modern vehicle. It commands a higher price, but the lower probability of major engine failure and the better tech make it the better long‑term proposition. Full details on the petrol Ingenium are on the 204PT Knowledge Centre.

Five‑Year Ownership Cost Comparison

When comparing the cost of owning an L538 vs L551 over five years, the biggest factors are purchase price, depreciation, maintenance, and the probability‑weighted cost of a major repair. The L538 is cheaper to buy (by £10,000–£20,000), but it depreciates faster and carries a higher risk of a costly timing chain job. The L551 holds its value better, and its revised engines are much less likely to need a major repair. For the average driver covering 10,000–12,000 miles per year, the total cost of ownership can actually be similar — the L538's lower purchase price is offset by higher maintenance and repair risk. However, if you buy an L538 and avoid the timing chain failure, you could come out ahead. It's a gamble.

Our repair vs replace framework can help you decide if an engine failure on an L538 is worth fixing, but generally, a timing chain failure on a sub‑£8k L538 often pushes the car into scrap territory, whereas the L551's higher value makes a repair more viable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Evoque is more reliable, L538 or L551?

The L551 is more reliable, largely due to the revised Ingenium diesel (post‑2019) that fixes the timing chain weakness, and the availability of the petrol 204PT which has fewer emissions‑related issues. The L538's pre‑2019 diesel is a known risk.

Is the L538 Evoque still worth buying in 2026?

Yes, if you're on a tight budget and find a well‑maintained example. Focus on post‑2015 models with a full service history, and ideally one that has had the timing chain replaced. Be prepared for higher maintenance costs.

What are the main engine differences between L538 and L551?

L538 uses pre‑2019 204DTD diesel (timing chain risk) and some 224DT diesel (Ford). L551 uses revised 204DTD (robust), 204PT petrol, and P300e hybrid (1.5‑litre + electric).

Which Evoque has lower running costs?

The L551 with the petrol 204PT or P300e hybrid typically has lower maintenance costs (fewer emissions issues) and better fuel economy in urban settings. However, the L538's lower purchase price can offset higher running costs if you buy carefully.
