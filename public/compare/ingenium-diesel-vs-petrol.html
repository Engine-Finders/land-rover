Ingenium Diesel vs Petrol

The 2.0‑litre Ingenium family powers the majority of modern Land Rovers. The diesel (204DTD) is the traditional choice for economy; the petrol (204PT) offers refinement and fewer emissions‑related headaches. Here's our honest verdict.

The 204PT petrol Ingenium wins for most buyers — it's slightly cheaper to replace, more reliable (fewer DPF, EGR and timing chain issues), and its real‑world fuel economy is closer to the diesel than you might expect. The diesel still makes sense for high‑mileage motorway drivers who genuinely benefit from the 10‑15 mpg advantage.

The Verdict

For the vast majority of buyers, the petrol 204PT is the more sensible choice. It's cheaper to replace, has fewer documented failure modes, and avoids the diesel‑specific headaches of DPF regeneration, EGR clogging, and oil dilution. The diesel's fuel economy advantage is real, but the gap narrows significantly outside pure motorway driving, and the higher purchase price and maintenance costs often erase the savings. Only high‑mileage motorway commuters or those who genuinely need the towing torque should choose the diesel [PATTERN DATA].

204DTD Diesel Deep Dive

The 204DTD is the most‑enquired engine in our entire Land Rover dataset (1,150 requests in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED]), reflecting its dominance across Discovery Sport, Evoque, Velar and Discovery. Its main documented weakness is pre‑2019 timing chain tensioner wear — a known issue that was substantially revised from the 2019 model year [EM-INTERVIEW]. Additionally, diesel owners face DPF regeneration‑related oil dilution, especially on short urban trips; our Ingenium Oil Dilution page covers this in detail. The reconditioned supply cost of £3,500–£5,800 [EM-QUOTE] is modest, and the engine is widely supported by specialists. However, the diesel's complexity with emissions systems makes it more prone to costly ancillary failures over the long term. Full specs and fitment on the 204DTD Knowledge Centre.

204PT Petrol Deep Dive

The petrol variant of the Ingenium family is often overlooked, but it's the more reliable and simpler option. With 480 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], it's far less common but has a stronger reputation among specialists. The main reported issues are cooling‑system related — water pump and coolant crossover pipe leaks — but these are generally less catastrophic than the diesel's timing chain or oil dilution problems [PATTERN DATA]. Reconditioned supply costs £3,000–£5,000 [EM-QUOTE], and it shares the same labour hours with the diesel (10–14 hrs). The petrol delivers a smoother, quieter driving experience and is better suited to urban stop‑start cycles. It also avoids the EGR/DPF issues that plague modern diesels. Full specs and reliability verdict on the 204PT Knowledge Centre.

Five‑Year Ownership Cost

Beyond the initial purchase price, the five‑year cost difference comes down to three factors: fuel, servicing, and probability‑weighted repairs. The diesel averages 40–45 mpg real‑world against the petrol's 30–35 mpg — a meaningful advantage for high‑mileage drivers (say, 15,000+ miles/year). However, the diesel's servicing is more expensive due to DPF fluid (AdBlue) and more frequent oil changes to mitigate dilution. The probability‑weighted repair cost strongly favours the petrol: the diesel's timing chain and DPF issues can run into thousands, while the petrol's water pump replacement is a fraction of that [EM-INTERVIEW]. For the typical UK driver covering 10,000–12,000 miles/year, the petrol often works out cheaper over five years, despite the higher fuel consumption.

Which Should You Buy?

🛣️

High‑Mileage Motorway Commuter

Diesel — its fuel economy advantage is most pronounced at steady speeds, and the engine is happy with long runs that keep the DPF clear.

🏙️

Urban / Suburban Driving & Short Trips

Petrol — avoids DPF issues, smoother in traffic, and the fuel economy gap is negligible in stop‑start conditions.

🔧

Low Maintenance & Reliability Priority

Petrol — fewer emissions systems, simpler design, and lower probability of expensive failures according to specialist feedback.

🚤

Occasional Towing or Loaded Driving

Diesel — the extra torque (up to 430 Nm vs 365 Nm) makes a noticeable difference when pulling a caravan or heavy trailer.

To be clear: neither engine is a bad choice. The diesel is a proven workhorse for high‑mileage drivers; the petrol is the smarter all‑rounder for most people. The decision ultimately hinges on your annual mileage and typical driving patterns — and if you're buying used, always prioritise a full service history and (for diesel) evidence of regular long runs. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Ingenium petrol more reliable than diesel?

Yes, according to specialist feedback and our failure pattern data. The petrol (204PT) has fewer major known issues — its main weak point is cooling system leaks, whereas the diesel (204DTD) has timing chain tensioner wear, oil dilution, and DPF/EGR problems that are more costly to fix.

Which Ingenium engine is cheaper to replace?

The petrol is slightly cheaper, with reconditioned supply costs of £3,000–£5,000 versus £3,500–£5,800 for the diesel. Labour hours are the same (10–14 hours) for both.

Is the diesel better for towing?

Yes — the 204DTD produces more torque (up to 430 Nm) than the petrol (365 Nm), making it better suited for towing caravans or heavy trailers.

Which Ingenium engine has lower running costs?

For typical UK mileage (10,000–12,000 miles/year), the petrol often works out cheaper over five years due to lower maintenance costs and fewer emissions‑system failures. The diesel only wins on fuel for drivers covering 15,000+ motorway miles annually.
