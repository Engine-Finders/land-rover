The Verdict

The L460 is the better all-rounder for buyers who can stretch to its higher price. It's more modern, more efficient (especially with the petrol Ingenium), and has a significantly nicer interior with cutting-edge tech. The L405 is a brilliant, proven luxury SUV with a lower entry price and a strong reputation — but its 306DT diesel carries a known catastrophic crankshaft bearing risk, and its tech is beginning to feel dated. If you're buying an L405, budget for the possibility of a major engine repair; if you're buying an L460, you're paying a premium for peace of mind and modern refinement.

Range Rover L405 Deep Dive

The L405 is the fourth generation of the Range Rover, produced from 2012 to 2021. It was a revolutionary step forward, introducing aluminium construction to shed weight and improve efficiency. Its most common engine is the 306DT 3.0 V6 diesel (TDV6/SDV6), producing 258–280 bhp and excellent low-end torque. The main documented risk is catastrophic crankshaft bearing failure, typically between 80,000 and 140,000 miles [PATTERN DATA]. The 508PS 4.4 SDV8 V8 diesel is also available, offering effortless power but with higher running costs. Replacement costs for the 306DT are £6,500–£9,500 reconditioned [EM-QUOTE], and with vehicle values typically £14,000–£55,000, a major failure can be a significant financial hit — but it's often still viable given the L405's strong residual values. For full details, see our 306DT Knowledge Centre and the 306DT failure page.

Range Rover L460 Deep Dive

The L460 is the fifth-generation Range Rover, launched in 2022. It's a ground-up redesign with a new MLA platform, offering even more refinement, efficiency, and technology. Its most common engine is the 306PS 3.0 Ingenium petrol (with mild-hybrid), producing 400 bhp and offering excellent performance with lower emissions. Early data suggests potential cooling system issues [PATTERN DATA], but the engine is too new for long-term reliability data. At £4,500–£7,500 for a reconditioned replacement [EM-QUOTE], and with vehicle values starting at £55,000, the replacement cost is a much smaller percentage of the vehicle's value, making it financially safer. The L460 also offers a plug-in hybrid (P440e, P510e) and a V8 (P530), catering to a wide range of buyers. For full details, see our 306PS Knowledge Centre (coming soon).

Which Should You Buy?

🚗

You want a luxurious daily driver with proven reliability

L405 — it's a proven platform with a strong reputation and a lower entry price. Just budget for potential 306DT crankshaft issues.

💻

You want the latest tech and modern refinement

L460 — it's the most advanced Range Rover ever, with a stunning interior, cutting-edge infotainment, and a smoother ride.

💰

You're on a budget but want Range Rover luxury

A well-maintained L405 can be a bargain, but set aside a £3,000–£5,000 emergency fund for potential repairs. If you can stretch, a newer L460 will cost less over 5 years.

🔁

You plan to keep it for 5+ years

L460 — the 306PS's failure mode is less catastrophic, and parts are more readily available. Factor in the cost of a pre-2023 cooling system inspection as a preventative measure.

Both generations have their strengths. The L405 is a classic that still turns heads, and its value may hold well for enthusiasts. But for most families and daily drivers, the L460 is the more sensible, cost-effective choice. Always have any L405's 306DT inspected for signs of crankshaft wear before purchase, and prioritise a full service history.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is more reliable, Range Rover L405 or L460?

The L460 is likely to be more reliable overall, due to its newer design and the 306PS petrol engine's less catastrophic failure mode. However, the L405's 306DT diesel has a known crankshaft bearing risk that can be catastrophic. The L460 is still too new for long-term reliability data.

Is the L460 better than the L405?

It depends. The L460 is more modern, more efficient (especially with the petrol Ingenium), and has a significantly nicer interior. The L405 has a proven platform, a lower entry price, and a strong reputation. For most buyers, the L460 is the better all-rounder.

Which Range Rover is cheaper to run, L405 or L460?

The L460 (especially the petrol Ingenium) is generally cheaper to run, with lower VED, lower servicing costs, and a lower risk of major failure. The L405's older diesel engines are less efficient and more expensive to maintain.

Is the L405 a good used buy in 2026?

Yes — if you find a well-maintained example with full service history and a proven 306DT that's had its crankshaft bearing inspected, it can be a very capable, luxurious vehicle. Budget for potential major repairs, and buy below the 60% threshold of your budget.

Which Range Rover holds its value better, L405 or L460?

The L460 currently holds value better due to its newer design and modern interior. However, clean, low-mileage L405s, especially the last of the run (2020-2021), are also holding firm as enthusiasts value their proven reliability.
