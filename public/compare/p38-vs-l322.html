The Verdict

The L322 is the better all‑rounder for most buyers. It's more refined, more powerful, and its later engines – especially the 4.4 TDV8 – are more reliable than the P38's ageing units. The L322 also benefits from BMW's engineering influence, with better build quality and more modern electronics. However, the P38 is a true classic – simpler, cheaper to buy, and with a charm that the L322 can't quite replicate. If you're an enthusiast willing to get your hands dirty, the P38 is a rewarding project. For daily use and long‑distance comfort, the L322 is the smarter choice. Our data shows that while both generations are older, the L322's engine families (368DT, 448DT) still generate significant enquiry volumes [EM-VERIFIED], reflecting ongoing demand for parts and rebuilds.

P38 Deep Dive

The P38 (1994–2002) was the first Range Rover to feature air suspension and a fully independent chassis, but it retained the classic boxy styling. Engine options include the BMW M51 2.5‑litre six‑cylinder diesel and the Rover 4.0/4.6‑litre V8 petrol. The M51 diesel is known for injection pump failure and head gasket issues [PATTERN DATA], while the Rover V8 suffers from head gasket problems and liner slip. Replacement engines are relatively cheap – a used M51 can be found for £1,500–£3,000 – but the rest of the vehicle may require constant attention. Corrosion is the P38's biggest enemy, particularly on the rear chassis and body mounts. The P38's value band of £3,000–£10,000 means that a major engine replacement can easily approach the vehicle's value – the 60% threshold is a useful guide. For a detailed look at the Rover V8, see our Rover V8 Knowledge Centre.

L322 Deep Dive

The L322 (2002–2012) was developed under BMW ownership and represents a major step forward in refinement and technology. Its engine line‑up includes the 3.6 TDV8 (368DT), the 4.4 TDV8 (448DT), and petrol V8s (4.2 SC, 5.0 SC). The 3.6 TDV8 is known for timing chain tensioner failure on early (2006‑2009) examples [PATTERN DATA], with repair costs of £1,500–£3,000 for the chain and £6,000–£9,000 for a full engine. The 4.4 TDV8 is more robust, but its oil cooler leaks and coolant crossover pipe issues are well‑documented [PATTERN DATA]. Petrol V8s are generally reliable but thirsty. The L322's value band of £5,000–£18,000 means that replacement costs can be a significant proportion – but the 4.4 TDV8's lower failure rate makes it the engine of choice. Full engine details are on the 368DT Knowledge Centre and 448DT Knowledge Centre.

Ownership Costs Compared

The biggest cost difference is the purchase price – a P38 can be found for £3,000–£6,000, while an L322 typically costs £5,000–£15,000. Running costs are higher for the P38 in many ways: fuel economy is worse (the Rover V8 is notoriously thirsty, often under 18 mpg), while the L322's diesel V8s return 22–25 mpg. Insurance is similar, but the P38's age means more frequent repairs. Engine replacement costs are lower for the P38 (used units are cheaper), but the L322's 4.4 TDV8 is more reliable and may last longer. The ownership cost calculator can help you model your specific mileage and usage. Also, the L322's more advanced suspension and electronics can be expensive to fix, but the P38's air suspension and electrics are notorious for gremlins – a key consideration in the repair vs replace decision.

Which Should You Buy?

👑

Enthusiast / Classic Collector

Choose the P38. It's the last of the classic Range Rover shape, simpler to work on, and offers a more analogue driving experience. Just budget for corrosion repairs and be prepared to chase electrical gremlins.

🚙

Daily Driver / Modern Comfort

Pick the L322. It's more refined, more comfortable, and its later engines (especially the 4.4 TDV8) are more reliable. The L322 also offers better safety and a more modern interior.

💰

Value‑Conscious Buyer

The P38 offers lower entry cost, but running costs can be high. The L322 is more expensive upfront but may be cheaper in the long run if you choose a well‑sorted 4.4 TDV8. Do your sums carefully.

🔁

Long‑Term Ownership

The L322 is the better long‑term proposition, with better parts support, stronger resale values, and more modern engineering. The P38 is a classic – its value may rise, but it will also demand constant care.

Whichever you choose, the repair vs replace framework and the ownership cost calculator can help you decide whether to fix or move on when the bills arrive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is more reliable, P38 or L322?

The L322 is generally more reliable, especially the 4.4 TDV8 (448DT) which has no catastrophic bottom‑end issues. The P38's older engines and electronics are more prone to failure, but with proper maintenance both can be reliable. The L322's 3.6 TDV8 has timing chain risks, but these are well‑understood.

Is the P38 cheaper to run than the L322?

Not necessarily – the P38's Rover V8 is very thirsty (often under 18 mpg), while the L322's diesel V8s return 22–25 mpg. The P38 also has more frequent age‑related repairs, so total running costs may be similar or even higher for the P38.

Which is better off‑road?

Both are capable, with full‑time four‑wheel drive and low‑range gearing. The P38's shorter overhangs give it slightly better approach and departure angles, but the L322's more advanced traction control and Terrain Response (on later models) make it easier to drive off‑road. For serious off‑roading, both are excellent.

Which holds its value better?

The P38 is becoming a classic, and well‑preserved examples are appreciating. The L322 also holds value well, especially the later facelift models with the 4.4 TDV8. For long‑term investment, the P38 may have more upside, but it requires more maintenance to keep it in top condition.
