Two of Land Rover's most popular compact-to-mid-size SUVs, head to head. One is the stylish, premium choice with extra space; the other is the more affordable, urban‑friendly, and astonishingly popular choice. Here's which one you should actually buy.

The Evoque is the better buy for most buyers — it's cheaper to buy, cheaper to run, shares the same core engines, and is far more widely available on the used market. The Velar justifies its premium with more interior space, a more upscale cabin, and a larger range of engine options (including V6 diesels and petrols), but for the vast majority of use cases, the Evoque delivers 90% of the experience for significantly less money.

The Verdict

For the vast majority of buyers, the Evoque is the smarter choice. It dominates our enquiry data — combined L538 and L551 enquiries outnumber Velar L560 by nearly 4‑to‑1 [EM-VERIFIED] — and while that reflects sales volume as much as reliability, it also means the Evoque is easier to find, cheaper to buy, and has a far larger pool of independent specialists familiar with its quirks. The Velar is a genuinely nicer place to spend time, and if you regularly carry rear passengers or need the extra boot space, it's worth the premium. But for most single‑car households or second‑car buyers, the Evoque delivers the Land Rover experience at a lower entry price and with lower ongoing costs — and that's why we recommend it for most people.

Evoque Deep Dive

The Evoque is Land Rover's best‑selling model for good reason. It combines striking design with a compact footprint that makes it genuinely usable in cities and multi‑storey car parks, while still offering a commanding driving position and the brand's signature off‑road capability (albeit in a more road‑biased package).

The vast majority of UK‑spec Evoques are powered by the 2.0‑litre 204DTD diesel or 204PT petrol — the same Ingenium four‑cylinder units found in the Velar. The pre‑2019 timing chain tensioner risk is well documented and affects both models equally [PATTERN DATA]; see our 204PT Timing Chain Failure page for full detail. Post‑2019 builds are substantially revised and much more robust. The P300e plug‑in hybrid is a growing presence; our Evoque P300e case study covers real‑world owner experiences.

For the majority of buyers, the Evoque's combination of style, affordability, and proven mechanical package makes it the logical first choice. Full engine specs and reliability ratings are on the 204DTD Knowledge Centre and 204PT Knowledge Centre.

Velar Deep Dive

The Velar sits between the Evoque and the Range Rover Sport in size and price. It's the most design‑led vehicle in the range — a streamlined, coupé‑like SUV that prioritises aesthetics and interior refinement over outright off‑road ability. If you value a premium cabin, more rear legroom, and a larger boot, the Velar is a clear step up from the Evoque.

Engine choices are broader than the Evoque: alongside the 204DTD and 204PT, the Velar also offers the 306DT V6 diesel (badged D300) and V6 petrol units (P340, P380). The 306DT carries the well‑known crankshaft bearing failure risk [PATTERN DATA] — full detail on our 306DT Crankshaft Bearing page — but its replacement cost of £6,500–£9,500 [EM-QUOTE] is still ✅ Safe against typical Velar values. For those who need extra towing capacity or simply prefer the refinement of a six‑cylinder, the Velar is the only way to get it.

However, the Velar's additional size and complexity come at a price: purchase cost is higher, running costs are higher (particularly on the V6 models), and our data shows it is less commonly sought after — 155 enquiries in 2025 vs 593 for the combined Evoque line [EM-VERIFIED]. For most buyers, that lower demand translates to better value on the used market, which can make a Velar an excellent proposition if you specifically want the extra space and don't mind paying the premium to buy and run it.

Five‑Year Ownership Cost

Beyond the purchase price, the real cost difference between these two models over five years comes down to four factors: depreciation, fuel economy, servicing, and the probability‑weighted cost of a major repair. The Evoque typically depreciates less in percentage terms because it's more popular and has a lower entry price. Fuel economy is broadly similar on the four‑cylinder engines (both return 40‑45 mpg real‑world depending on spec), but the Velar's V6 options are significantly thirstier. Servicing costs are similar for the four‑cylinder units, but the Velar's larger engine bay does not increase labour times appreciably for the 204DTD/204PT; the V6 adds additional hours.

The probability‑weighted repair cost is the most interesting comparison. The Evoque and Velar share the same Ingenium diesel and petrol engines, so they share the same failure modes and replacement costs. The Velar's additional V6 options introduce a higher‑cost failure mode (the 306DT crank bearing), but these are a minority of Velar sales. On a like‑for‑like four‑cylinder basis, the running costs are close enough that the decision should be driven by space and comfort requirements, not running cost — but if you're on a budget, the Evoque's lower purchase price and greater availability of cheaper used examples make it the more cost‑effective choice overall.

Which Should You Buy?

🚙

Urban Commuter & Style‑Conscious Buyer

Evoque — it's shorter, narrower, and easier to park in town, while still offering the premium feel and commanding view that makes Land Rovers special.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Family Car or Regular Long Haul

Velar — the extra rear legroom and boot space make a real difference with kids or luggage, and the more refined ride on motorways is genuinely noticeable.

🏔️

Occasional Off‑Road or Towing

Velar with D300 (306DT) — the V6's torque and extra weight capacity make it the better tool for towing or green‑laning; the Evoque is capable but limited by its smaller engine and lighter construction.

💰

Budget‑Focused Used Buyer

Evoque — you'll find a much wider selection of used cars, more choice of engines and trims, and lower entry prices; the depreciation hit is also softer.

To be clear: neither model is a bad choice. The Evoque is the sensible, affordable, urban‑friendly option; the Velar is the spacious, luxurious, long‑distance cruiser. Both are built on the same core platform and share engines, so reliability prospects are similar (with the usual Ingenium caveats). The decision should be driven by how you actually use the car — and if you're still unsure, the repair vs replace framework and the ownership cost calculator can help you run the numbers on a specific example.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better, Velar or Evoque?

For most buyers, the Evoque is the better choice — it's cheaper to buy, cheaper to run, more widely available, and shares the same core engines. The Velar only makes sense if you genuinely need the extra interior space, towing capability (via V6 options), or the more premium interior.

Is the Velar more reliable than the Evoque?

No — they share the same Ingenium four‑cylinder engines, so reliability is functionally identical. The Velar also offers V6 options (306DT) which have a well‑documented crank bearing risk, so if anything the Velar's reliability profile is slightly worse if you opt for the V6. Both post‑2019 four‑cylinder units are robust.

Which has cheaper running costs, Velar or Evoque?

The Evoque is cheaper to run — it's lighter, uses less fuel (especially with four‑cylinder engines), and has lower servicing costs due to more widespread parts availability and specialist familiarity. The Velar's V6 options increase fuel, tax, and repair bills significantly.

Do Velar and Evoque have the same engines?

They share the 204DTD (2.0 diesel) and 204PT (2.0 petrol) Ingenium units. The Velar additionally offers the 306DT V6 diesel and V6 petrol engines, which are not available in the Evoque.
