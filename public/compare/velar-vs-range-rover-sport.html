If you want a stylish, road‑focused SUV with a premium feel, buy the Velar. If you need more space, practicality, and proven capability, the Range Rover Sport is the stronger choice. The Sport is larger, offers seven‑seat options (in later models), and shares its platform with the full‑size Range Rover, giving it a more rugged and capable character. For most buyers, the Sport wins on all‑round ability; for those prioritising style and urban appeal, the Velar is a compelling alternative.

The Verdict

The Range Rover Sport is the better all‑rounder for most buyers. It offers more interior space, a larger boot, and a more established reputation for capability and reliability (in its later iterations). The Sport's 308 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED] more than double the Velar's 155, reflecting its broader appeal. However, the Velar is a stunningly designed vehicle with a more premium, minimalist cabin and a lower, sleeker profile. It's cheaper to buy used and, with the 204DTD engine, cheaper to replace than the Sport's V6 diesel. For urban drivers who value aesthetics over space, the Velar is a compelling choice; for families and those needing real‑world practicality, the Sport wins decisively. Both share the 306DT's crankshaft bearing risk [PATTERN DATA] where fitted, so service history is crucial.

Velar Deep Dive

The Velar L560 (2017–present) is Land Rover's mid‑size SUV, positioned between the Evoque and the Range Rover Sport. It shares its platform with the Jaguar F‑Pace, giving it a more road‑focused, car‑like driving experience. Engine options include the 204DTD 2.0‑litre diesel (D180, D200), the 204PT 2.0‑litre petrol (P250, P300), and the 306DT 3.0‑litre V6 diesel (D300) in earlier models. The 204DTD has a known pre‑2019 timing chain tensioner risk [PATTERN DATA], while the 306DT carries the catastrophic crankshaft bearing failure risk. Replacement costs are £3,500–£5,800 for the 204DTD and £6,500–£9,500 for the 306DT [EM-QUOTE]. The Velar's coupe‑style roofline reduces rear headroom and boot space (up to 673 litres) compared to the Sport, but its design and interior quality are class‑leading. Our data shows 155 enquiries for the Velar [EM-VERIFIED], reflecting its niche appeal. Full engine details are on our 204DTD Knowledge Centre, 204PT Knowledge Centre, and 306DT Knowledge Centre.

Range Rover Sport Deep Dive

The Range Rover Sport L494 (2013–2022) is the second‑generation Sport, built on the same aluminium platform as the full‑size Range Rover. It's larger, more practical, and more capable than the Velar, with a more spacious cabin and a larger boot (up to 1,761 litres). Engine options are predominantly the 306DT 3.0 V6 diesel (TDV6/SDV6), the 276DT 2.7 V6 in earlier cars, and the 508PS 4.4 V8 in higher‑spec models. The 306DT's crankshaft bearing risk is well‑documented [PATTERN DATA], typically occurring between 80,000 and 140,000 miles. Replacement cost is £6,500–£9,500 [EM-QUOTE], and despite the cost, the Sport's £12,000–£55,000 value band means most examples are ✅ Safe to repair. The Sport also offers a seven‑seat option (on some facelift models), making it a genuine family SUV. Our data shows 308 enquiries for the L494 [EM-VERIFIED], making it one of the most‑enquired models on the site. Full engine specs are on the 306DT Knowledge Centre and 276DT Knowledge Centre. The Range Rover Sport Economics Guide breaks down the repair‑or‑replace decision.

Ownership Costs Compared

The biggest cost difference is the purchase price – a used Velar typically costs £2,000–£5,000 less than a comparable Range Rover Sport, reflecting its slightly less prestigious positioning. Running costs are similar for same‑engine examples, but the Velar's lighter weight and more aerodynamic design give it a fuel economy advantage (around 2‑3 mpg better for the 204DTD). Engine replacement costs are lower for the Velar if you choose the 204DTD (£3,500–£5,800) versus the Sport's 306DT (£6,500–£9,500) [EM-QUOTE]. However, the Sport's higher value means the replacement percentage is lower, making it a safer financial proposition if a 306DT failure occurs. The 60% threshold [EM-INTERVIEW] applies to both: for a Velar valued at £18,000, a £5,800 replacement is 32% – ✅ Safe; for a Sport valued at £22,000, a £9,500 replacement is 43% – ✅ Safe. Which Should You Buy?

🏙️

Urban Driver / Style Seeker

Choose the Velar. Its stunning design, premium interior, and lower running costs make it perfect for city living. The Velar's smaller dimensions also make it easier to park and manoeuvre in tight spaces.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Family Buyer / Needs Space

Pick the Range Rover Sport. It offers more rear legroom, a larger boot, and optional seven‑seat capability. The Sport is also better for towing and carries a more rugged, capable image.

💰

Value‑Conscious Buyer

The Velar offers better value for money – lower purchase price, cheaper engine options (204DTD), and similar running costs. If you don't need the extra space, the Velar is the more cost‑effective choice.

🔁

Long‑Term Ownership

Both can reach high mileages with care. The Sport's stronger resale values make it a better investment if you sell after 3‑5 years. For a decade of ownership, the Velar's lower purchase cost and cheaper engine replacements may give it an edge.

Whichever you choose, the repair vs replace framework and the ownership cost calculator can help you decide whether to fix or move on when the bills arrive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is more reliable, Velar or Range Rover Sport?

Reliability depends on the engine. The Velar with the 204DTD (post‑2019) is generally more reliable than the Sport's 306DT V6, which has a known crankshaft bearing risk. The Sport's later models (2018+) also improved, but the 306DT remains a high‑risk engine. Overall, the Velar's smaller four‑cylinder engines are more reliable and cheaper to replace.

Is the Velar cheaper to run than the Range Rover Sport?

Yes, typically. The Velar is lighter and more aerodynamic, giving better fuel economy (35–40 mpg for the 204DTD vs 28–32 mpg for the 306DT). Insurance and servicing are also marginally cheaper, and the Velar is generally less expensive to buy used. The gap in running costs can save £500–£1,000 per year.

Which is better off‑road?

The Range Rover Sport is more capable off‑road, with better ground clearance, wading depth, and approach/departure angles. The Velar is still capable, but its road‑focused design and lower ride height make it less suited to serious off‑roading. For green‑laning and light off‑road use, both are fine; for heavy terrain, the Sport is superior.

Which has more space inside?

The Range Rover Sport has significantly more space – a larger boot (up to 1,761 litres vs 673 litres in the Velar) and more rear legroom. The Sport also offers optional seven seats on some models. For families, the Sport is the clear winner on space.

Which holds its value better?

The Range Rover Sport traditionally holds its value better due to its stronger brand image and broader appeal. However, the Velar's design and lower entry price make it attractive on the used market, and its depreciation curve has been relatively stable. For the best resale, choose a desirable engine and spec and maintain a full service history.
