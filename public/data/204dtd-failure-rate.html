Failure Rate in Context

Where the 204DTD sits relative to other Land Rover engines.

In 2025, the 204DTD generated 1,150 enquiries [EM‑VERIFIED], making it the most‑enquired Land Rover engine – ahead of the 306DT (980) and the 276DT (700). It accounts for approximately 23% of all Land Rover engine enquiries, a significant share driven by its widespread fitment across the Discovery Sport (L550), Evoque (L551, L538), Defender (L663), and Velar (L560).

Importantly, enquiry volume does not equal failure rate [PATTERN DATA]. The 204DTD is the most common modern Land Rover diesel, and its absolute enquiry count reflects its large vehicle parc. However, the timing chain tensioner failure on pre‑2019 models is a well‑documented issue that can lead to catastrophic engine damage if not addressed early.

Compared to other Ingenium engines, the petrol 204PT is considered more reliable in our data (480 enquiries, 60k–120k typical failure), but the diesel 204DTD has a higher enquiry volume and a more serious failure profile due to the timing chain and oil dilution issues.

Mileage‑at‑Failure Distribution

When does the 204DTD typically fail?

Our data shows a typical failure window of 60,000–120,000 miles [PATTERN DATA]. This is derived from customer enquiry descriptions, supplier notes, and manufacturer TSBs. The distribution shows a peak around 80,000–100,000 miles, which aligns with the timing chain tensioner wear becoming critical.

Pre‑2019 models are at higher risk in the lower part of this range, while post‑2019 units (with revised tensioner) tend to push failures towards the higher end – though they are not immune. Oil dilution from DPF regeneration also contributes to earlier wear on engines used primarily for short urban trips.

The timing chain tensioner failure is the primary concern. It is often heralded by a cold‑start rattle – if you hear this on a pre‑2019 204DTD, it is an early warning sign. How Long Do They Actually Last?

Lifespan is not fixed – it depends on maintenance, model year, and driving patterns.

With diligent maintenance (6,000‑mile oil intervals using the correct 0W‑20 or 5W‑30 spec, avoiding short urban trips, and using premium fuel), many post‑2019 204DTD engines exceed 150,000 miles without major failure. We have seen examples in our data reaching 180,000+ miles on original engines.

However, the typical failure window of 60k–120k miles reflects the reality for most owners, especially those with pre‑2019 models. The key factors that push an engine towards the lower end of this range are:

  • Pre‑2019 model year – higher risk of tensioner failure
  • Short urban trips – causing DPF clogging and oil dilution
  • Extended oil change intervals – especially beyond 12,000 miles or using incorrect oil
  • Ignoring cold‑start rattle – the early warning sign of tensioner wear

Our Verdict – Is the 204DTD a Reliable Engine?

The answer depends on the model year and your ownership approach.

The 204DTD is a high‑volume, high‑stakes engine. Pre‑2019 models have a known timing chain tensioner weakness that can lead to catastrophic failure. Post‑2019 models are significantly improved and can be very reliable if maintained correctly. The engine also suffers from oil dilution and EGR issues, which are manageable with the right driving habits.

We rate the 204DTD as ⚠️ High Risk (pre‑2019) / ✅ Safe (post‑2019). The risk is real and well‑documented, but the post‑2019 revision has largely addressed the tensioner problem. If you own a pre‑2019 model, 6,000‑mile oil changes and listening for cold‑start rattle are non‑negotiable. Post‑2019 owners should still follow the service schedule and avoid short trips to prevent oil dilution.

On the repair‑to‑value front, the engine is ✅ Safe on typical host vehicles (Discovery Sport, Evoque, Defender), meaning replacement cost is well within the vehicle's value. This is why we see so many owners choosing to replace rather than scrap.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common failure on the 204DTD?

Timing chain tensioner failure is the primary issue, particularly on pre‑2019 models, typically occurring between 60,000 and 120,000 miles. Cold‑start rattle is the early warning sign.

How long does a 204DTD engine last?

With proper maintenance (6k oil intervals, correct oil spec), post‑2019 models often last beyond 150,000 miles. Pre‑2019 models are at higher risk in the 60k–120k window, but many still reach 120k+ with good care.

Is the 204DTD more reliable than the 204PT petrol?

Our data shows the 204PT (petrol Ingenium) has fewer enquiries (480 vs 1,150) and is generally considered more reliable, though it has its own cooling system issues. The 204DTD has a more serious failure profile due to the timing chain tensioner.

Is it worth replacing a 204DTD engine?

Yes – on typical host vehicles (Discovery Sport, Evoque, Defender), replacement is financially sound. The engine carries a ✅ Safe flag, meaning the cost is well within the vehicle's value.
