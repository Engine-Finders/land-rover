204PT Failure Rate in Context

How does the 204PT compare to other Land Rover engines in our 2025 enquiry data?

Our 2025 CRM data recorded 4,935 Land Rover‑specific engine enquiries [EM-VERIFIED]. The 204PT contributed 480 of those – about 9.7% of the total. That places it sixth in the overall Land Rover engine ranking, behind the 204DTD (1,150), 306DT (980), 276DT (700), 368DT (650), and 508PS (620) [EM-VERIFIED].

It is important to read this number in context: the 204PT is the petrol version of the Ingenium family, fitted to a smaller number of vehicles than its diesel sibling (the 204DTD/AJ20D6). Its enquiry volume is significantly lower than the diesel's 1,150, which is consistent with the diesel's higher market share in the UK and its known timing chain issues. The 204PT is generally considered more reliable than the diesel Ingenium, with fewer reports of catastrophic failures – but it still generates a steady stream of enquiries, primarily related to cooling system components.

Compared to the diesel Ingenium's timing chain tensioner failures, the 204PT's weak points are less dramatic but still costly. Water pump failure and cooling system leaks are the most frequent issues, and while they are not typically engine‑destroying, they can lead to overheating and secondary damage if left unaddressed. The engine carries a ✅ Safe flag on our R‑to‑V index: on a Velar or Evoque worth £18,000–£45,000, a £3,000–£5,000 replacement cost is well within the 60% threshold.

Mileage‑at‑Failure Distribution

When does the 204PT typically give up?

Our proprietary failure pattern data [PATTERN DATA] indicates that the 204PT most frequently shows failure signs in the 60,000–120,000 mile bracket. Within that band, we see two distinct clusters:

  • 60k–90k – often associated with water pump failure and cooling system leaks, particularly on vehicles that have not had regular coolant changes. This is the most common issue in this mileage range.
  • 90k–120k – more commonly linked to timing chain stretch (though less frequent than on the diesel) and occasional PCV valve or thermostat housing issues. These tend to occur on higher‑mileage units with irregular servicing.

These figures are based on customer enquiry descriptions and workshop notes from our specialist network. They are not exact failure rates against the total vehicle population – but they do represent real patterns observed across hundreds of 204PT replacement requests.

Owners who maintain their 204PT with regular coolant flushes (every 3 years), use the correct 0W‑20 or 5W‑30 oil, and replace the water pump proactively at around 60,000 miles often see the engine exceed 120,000 miles without major internal failures. However, the enquiry data shows that a significant proportion of failures occur before 90,000 miles, suggesting that preventative maintenance is frequently neglected.

The 204PT is a relatively reliable engine when maintained correctly, but its cooling system requires attention.

How Long Does a 204PT Actually Last?

Real‑world lifespan based on owner data and workshop feedback.

There is no single answer, but our data and interviews with specialists point to a typical service life of 100,000–150,000 miles for a well‑maintained 204PT [PATTERN DATA]. Engines that receive regular oil changes (using the correct 0W‑20 or 5W‑30 specification) and have had the water pump and cooling system inspected proactively tend to exceed 120,000 miles without major internal failures.

Unlike the diesel Ingenium, the 204PT does not have a widespread timing chain tensioner issue – the petrol engine's timing chain is more robust, and failures are rare. The primary determinant of longevity is the cooling system: water pump and thermostat housing failures can lead to overheating and head gasket damage if ignored. With regular coolant flushes and early replacement of the water pump (at around 60,000 miles as a preventative measure), many 204PT units reach 150,000 miles with only routine maintenance.

Conversely, vehicles that have had irregular servicing – particularly with extended oil change intervals or neglected coolant maintenance – are far more likely to experience cooling system issues and, in some cases, timing chain stretch before 100,000 miles. The 204PT is a modern, efficient petrol engine, but it does not forgive neglect of its cooling and oil systems.

One interesting data point: among the 480 enquiries we logged in 2025, the average declared mileage at the time of enquiry was approximately 85,000 miles – though this is self‑reported and may skew towards higher‑mileage vehicles. For a more rigorous look, we track this in our Engine Lifespan analysis.

If your 204PT is approaching 60,000 miles, we recommend a pre‑emptive water pump inspection – the cost of this check is modest compared to a full engine replacement if the pump fails and causes overheating.

Our Verdict – Repair or Replace?

Applying the 60% threshold to the 204PT.

The Land Rover ownership guide uses a 60% replacement‑to‑value (R‑to‑V) threshold: if the cost of a reconditioned engine supply and fit exceeds 60% of the vehicle’s current market value, we consider scrapping or selling as a serious alternative.

For the 204PT, the typical host vehicle values are:

  • Range Rover Velar L560 (2017–present): £18,000–£50,000 [THIRD-PARTY]
  • Evoque L551 (2019–present): £16,000–£38,000 [THIRD-PARTY]
  • Discovery Sport L550 (2014–2019): £8,000–£22,000 [THIRD-PARTY]

A reconditioned 204PT supply costs £3,000–£5,000 [EM-QUOTE]. Adding labour (10–14 hours at ~£65/hr indie) gives a fitted cost of £3,650–£5,910 for a reconditioned unit.

Worked example: A 2018 Velar P250 worth £22,000. Fitted recon cost (mid‑range) = £4,800. That's 22% of the vehicle's value – well below 60%. In this scenario, the engine replacement is financially sensible.

Even on a lower‑value example (e.g., a 2015 Discovery Sport worth £10,000), the same £4,800 fitted cost represents 48% – still comfortably within the 60% threshold, so replacement is still justified.

Our ✅ Safe flag reflects this: the 204PT is a good economic choice on most vehicles, and replacement is almost always justified. Always use our Repair vs Replace Calculator for your specific vehicle and engine combination.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the typical mileage for 204PT failure?

Most failures occur between 60,000 and 120,000 miles [PATTERN DATA], with water pump and cooling system issues being the most common at the lower end of that range.

How much does a 204PT reconditioned engine cost?

Supply‑only prices range from £3,000 to £5,000 [EM-QUOTE]. With labour (10–14 hours), fitted costs are typically £3,650–£5,910 at independent rates.

Is the 204PT a reliable engine?

Yes, it is considered more reliable than the diesel Ingenium. Its main weak points are the cooling system (water pump and thermostat housing) rather than catastrophic engine failures. With proper maintenance, it can exceed 120,000 miles.

Should I replace a 204PT in a Discovery Sport?

Almost always yes. Even on a lower‑value Discovery Sport, the replacement cost is well within the 60% threshold. Replacement is financially sound and will extend the vehicle’s life significantly.

How can I prevent 204PT failure?

Regular coolant flushes (every 3 years), pre‑emptive water pump replacement at around 60,000 miles, and using correct‑spec oil (0W‑20 or 5W‑30) can prevent the most common failures. Also, address any coolant leaks promptly.
