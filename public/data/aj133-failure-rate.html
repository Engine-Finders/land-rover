AJ133 Failure Rate in Context

How does the AJ133 compare to other Land Rover engines in our 2025 enquiry data?

Our 2025 CRM data recorded 4,935 Land Rover‑specific engine enquiries [EM-VERIFIED]. The AJ133 contributed 330 of those – about 6.7% of the total. That places it ninth in the overall Land Rover engine ranking, behind the 204DTD (1,150), 306DT (980), 276DT (700), 368DT (650), 508PS (620), 204PT (480), 224DT (350) and 448DT (320) [EM-VERIFIED].

It is important to read this number in context: the AJ133 is a 5.0-litre supercharged V8 used primarily in the Range Rover L405, Range Rover Sport L494, and some Discovery models. Its enquiry volume is relatively modest compared to the more common diesel engines, but it remains a significant source of replacement enquiries – particularly for pre-2013 examples where the timing chain tensioner is a known weakness.

Unlike the 306DT's catastrophic bottom‑end failure, the AJ133's issues are generally related to ancillaries and timing chain components, which are expensive but not terminal. The post-2013 revision is substantially more robust, which is reflected in the lower enquiry volume for newer vehicles. The engine carries a ✅ Safe R‑to‑V flag: on a vehicle worth £8,000–£35,000, a £5,500–£9,000 replacement is well within the 60% threshold.

Mileage‑at‑Failure Distribution

When does the AJ133 typically give up?

Our proprietary failure pattern data [PATTERN DATA] indicates that the AJ133 most frequently shows failure signs between 60,000 and 120,000 miles. Within that band, we see two distinct clusters:

  • 60k–90k – often associated with early timing chain tensioner wear on pre-2013 units, particularly on vehicles with extended oil change intervals. Supercharger coupler wear also appears in this band.
  • 90k–120k – more commonly linked to coolant crossover pipe leaks, which can lead to sudden coolant loss and potential overheating if not addressed promptly.

These figures are based on customer enquiry descriptions and workshop notes from our specialist network. They are not exact failure rates against the total vehicle population – but they do represent real patterns observed across hundreds of AJ133 replacement requests.

Owners who maintain their AJ133 with regular oil changes (every 10,000 miles or annually) and use the correct specification oil often see the engine exceed 120,000 miles without major internal failures. However, the enquiry data shows that a significant proportion of failures occur before 100,000 miles, suggesting that preventative maintenance is frequently neglected – particularly the timing chain tensioner inspection on pre-2013 units.

The timing chain tensioner issue is the most critical and expensive failure mode. Pre-2013 AJ133 engines are known to suffer from tensioner wear, which can cause the timing chain to jump – a catastrophic failure. The coolant crossover pipes are also a known weakness – they are made of plastic and become brittle with age, often failing with little warning. The supercharger coupler is a less serious but common annoyance, causing a characteristic rattle at idle.

How Long Does an AJ133 Actually Last?

Real‑world lifespan based on owner data and workshop feedback.

There is no single answer, but our data and interviews with specialists point to a typical service life of 120,000–180,000 miles for a well‑maintained AJ133 [PATTERN DATA]. Engines that receive regular oil changes (using the correct 5W‑30 or 0W‑20 specification) and have had the timing chain tensioner inspected (particularly on pre-2013 units) tend to exceed 150,000 miles without major internal failures.

The critical distinction is pre-2013 vs post-2013. The timing chain tensioner was revised in 2013, and post-2013 engines are significantly more robust. Post-2013 AJ133 units frequently exceed 150,000 miles with only routine maintenance, whereas pre-2013 units are at risk of tensioner failure from around 60,000 miles.

Conversely, vehicles that have had irregular servicing – particularly with extended oil change intervals or neglected cooling system maintenance – are far more likely to experience timing chain issues and coolant leaks before 100,000 miles. The AJ133 is a robust engine, but it does not forgive neglect of its timing chain and cooling systems.

One interesting data point: among the 330 enquiries we logged in 2025, the average declared mileage at the time of enquiry was approximately 95,000 miles – though this is self‑reported and may skew towards higher‑mileage vehicles. For a more rigorous look, we track this in our Engine Lifespan analysis.

If your AJ133 is approaching 60,000 miles and is a pre-2013 unit, we recommend a pre‑emptive timing chain tensioner inspection – the cost of this check is modest compared to a full engine replacement if the chain jumps.

Our Verdict – Repair or Replace?

Applying the 60% threshold to the AJ133.

The Land Rover ownership guide uses a 60% replacement‑to‑value (R‑to‑V) threshold: if the cost of a reconditioned engine supply and fit exceeds 60% of the vehicle’s current market value, we consider scrapping or selling as a serious alternative.

For the AJ133, the typical host vehicle values are:

  • Range Rover L405 (2012–2021): £14,000–£55,000 [THIRD-PARTY]
  • Range Rover Sport L494 (2013–2022): £12,000–£55,000 [THIRD-PARTY]
  • Discovery 4/L319 (2009–2016): £6,000–£18,000 [THIRD-PARTY]

A reconditioned AJ133 supply costs £5,500–£9,000 [EM-QUOTE]. Adding labour (14–18 hours at ~£65/hr indie) gives a fitted cost of £6,410–£10,170 for a reconditioned unit.

Worked example: A 2015 Range Rover L405 worth £25,000. Fitted recon cost (mid‑range) = £8,000. That's 32% of the vehicle's value – well below 60%. In this scenario, the engine replacement is financially sensible.

Even on a lower‑value example (e.g., a 2013 Discovery 4 worth £8,000), the same £8,000 fitted cost represents 100% – well above 60%. In this scenario, replacement is not financially sensible; we would advise scrapping or selling for parts.

Our ✅ Safe flag reflects the typical Range Rover host values, but the calculation changes significantly on Discovery 4 examples. Always use our Repair vs Replace Calculator for your specific vehicle and engine combination.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the typical mileage for AJ133 failure?

Most failures occur between 60,000 and 120,000 miles [PATTERN DATA], with timing chain tensioner wear on pre-2013 units being the most common issue at the lower end of that range.

How much does an AJ133 reconditioned engine cost?

Supply‑only prices range from £5,500 to £9,000 [EM-QUOTE]. With labour (14–18 hours), fitted costs are typically £6,410–£10,170 at independent rates.

Is the AJ133 a reliable engine?

It is reliable when maintained correctly, particularly post-2013 units. Pre-2013 engines have a known timing chain tensioner weakness that requires proactive inspection. With proper maintenance, it can exceed 150,000 miles.

Should I replace an AJ133 in a Discovery 4?

It depends on the vehicle's value. A Discovery 4 worth under £8,000 is often not worth replacing – the cost of a recon engine (fitted) frequently exceeds 60% of the vehicle's value. Use our calculator for a personalised answer.

How can I prevent AJ133 failure?

Regular oil changes (10,000 miles or annually), using correct‑spec oil, pre‑emptive timing chain tensioner inspection on pre-2013 units, and early replacement of coolant crossover pipes can prevent the most common failures. Also, address any coolant leaks promptly.
