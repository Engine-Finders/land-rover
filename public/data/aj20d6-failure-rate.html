AJ20D6 Failure Rate in Context

How does the AJ20D6 compare to other Land Rover engines in our 2025 enquiry data?

Our 2025 CRM data recorded 4,935 Land Rover‑specific engine enquiries [EM-VERIFIED]. The AJ20D6 – which is the same engine as the Land Rover 204DTD – contributed 1,150 of those – about 23% of the total. That places it first in the overall Land Rover engine ranking, ahead of the 306DT (980), 276DT (700), 368DT (650), 508PS (620), 204PT (480), 224DT (350) and 448DT (320) [EM-VERIFIED].

It is important to read this number in context: the AJ20D6/204DTD is a high‑volume engine fitted to some of Land Rover's most popular models – Discovery Sport, Evoque, and Freelander 2 – across a wide range of years. Its enquiry volume reflects both the size of that vehicle parc and the fact that these vehicles are now reaching the age and mileage where replacement becomes a realistic consideration. The AJ20D6 does not necessarily fail at a higher rate proportionally than other engines – it simply has the largest absolute number of vehicles on the road.

Compared to the 306DT's catastrophic crankshaft bearing failure, the AJ20D6's failure modes are generally less dramatic – but they still lead to expensive repairs if not caught early. The pattern of enquiries we see for the AJ20D6 is consistent with an engine that is high‑volume and reaching the age where timing chain and oil dilution issues become prevalent. The engine carries a ✅ Safe flag on our R‑to‑V index: on a Discovery Sport or Evoque worth £12,000–£30,000, a £3,500–£5,800 replacement cost is well within the 60% threshold.

Mileage‑at‑Failure Distribution

When does the AJ20D6 typically give up?

Our proprietary failure pattern data [PATTERN DATA] indicates that the AJ20D6 most frequently shows failure signs in the 60,000–120,000 mile bracket. Within that band, we see two distinct clusters:

  • 60k–90k – often associated with early timing chain tensioner wear on pre-2019 units, particularly on vehicles with extended oil change intervals. Oil dilution from DPF regeneration is also common in this band, especially on vehicles used predominantly for short urban trips.
  • 90k–120k – more commonly linked to EGR cooler leaks and secondary timing chain issues, often on vehicles that have had irregular servicing or have been used in stop‑start traffic.

These figures are based on customer enquiry descriptions and workshop notes from our specialist network. They are not exact failure rates against the total vehicle population – but they do represent real patterns observed across hundreds of AJ20D6 replacement requests.

Owners who maintain their AJ20D6 with regular oil changes (every 10,000 miles or annually), use the correct oil specification, and take occasional longer journeys (to allow DPF regeneration) often see the engine exceed 120,000 miles without major internal failures. However, the enquiry data shows that a significant proportion of failures occur before 90,000 miles, suggesting that preventative maintenance is frequently neglected.

The timing chain tensioner is the most critical and expensive failure mode. Pre-2019 AJ20D6 engines are known to suffer from tensioner wear, which can cause the timing chain to jump – a catastrophic failure. The 2019 revision improved the tensioner design, and post-2019 units are substantially more reliable. Oil dilution is a common but less catastrophic issue – it occurs when diesel fuel mixes with engine oil during DPF regeneration, reducing the oil's lubricating properties. Regular oil analysis can detect this early.

If you experience any of these symptoms, our 204DTD cold start rattle or white smoke pages can help you narrow it down. (The AJ20D6 shares the same symptoms as the 204DTD.)

How Long Does an AJ20D6 Actually Last?

Real‑world lifespan based on owner data and workshop feedback.

There is no single answer, but our data and interviews with specialists point to a typical service life of 100,000–150,000 miles for a well‑maintained AJ20D6 [PATTERN DATA]. Engines that receive regular oil changes (using the correct 0W‑20 or 5W‑30 specification) and have had the timing chain tensioner inspected (particularly on pre-2019 units) tend to exceed 120,000 miles without major internal failures.

The critical distinction is pre-2019 vs post-2019. The timing chain tensioner was revised in 2019, and post-2019 engines are significantly more robust. Post-2019 AJ20D6 units frequently exceed 140,000 miles with only routine maintenance, whereas pre-2019 units are at risk of tensioner failure from around 60,000 miles.

Conversely, vehicles that have had irregular servicing – particularly with extended oil change intervals or neglected DPF maintenance – are far more likely to experience timing chain issues and oil dilution before 90,000 miles. The AJ20D6 is a modern, efficient engine, but it does not forgive neglect of its timing chain and oil systems.

One interesting data point: among the 1,150 enquiries we logged in 2025, the average declared mileage at the time of enquiry was approximately 85,000 miles – though this is self‑reported and may skew towards higher‑mileage vehicles. For a more rigorous look, we track this in our Engine Lifespan analysis.

If your AJ20D6 is approaching 60,000 miles and is a pre-2019 unit, we recommend a pre‑emptive timing chain tensioner inspection – the cost of this check is modest compared to a full engine replacement if the chain jumps.

Our Verdict – Repair or Replace?

Applying the 60% threshold to the AJ20D6.

The Land Rover ownership guide uses a 60% replacement‑to‑value (R‑to‑V) threshold: if the cost of a reconditioned engine supply and fit exceeds 60% of the vehicle’s current market value, we consider scrapping or selling as a serious alternative.

For the AJ20D6, the typical host vehicle values are:

  • Discovery Sport L550 (2014–2019): £8,000–£22,000 [THIRD-PARTY]
  • Evoque L538 (2011–2018): £5,000–£14,000 [THIRD-PARTY]
  • Freelander 2 L359 (2006–2014): £2,500–£7,000 [THIRD-PARTY]

A reconditioned AJ20D6 supply costs £3,500–£5,800 [EM-QUOTE]. Adding labour (10–14 hours at ~£65/hr indie) gives a fitted cost of £4,150–£6,710 for a reconditioned unit.

Worked example: A 2016 Discovery Sport L550 worth £15,000. Fitted recon cost (mid‑range) = £5,500. That's 37% of the vehicle's value – well below 60%. In this scenario, the engine replacement is financially sensible.

On a lower‑value example (e.g., a 2012 Freelander 2 worth £4,000), the same £5,500 fitted cost represents 137% – well above 60%. In this scenario, replacement is not financially sensible; we would advise scrapping or selling for parts.

Our ✅ Safe flag reflects the typical Discovery Sport and Evoque host values, but the calculation changes significantly on Freelander 2 examples. Always use our Repair vs Replace Calculator for your specific vehicle and engine combination.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the typical mileage for AJ20D6 failure?

Most failures occur between 60,000 and 120,000 miles [PATTERN DATA], with timing chain tensioner wear on pre-2019 units being the most common issue at the lower end of that range.

How much does an AJ20D6 reconditioned engine cost?

Supply‑only prices range from £3,500 to £5,800 [EM-QUOTE]. With labour (10–14 hours), fitted costs are typically £4,150–£6,710 at independent rates.

Is the AJ20D6 a reliable engine?

It is reliable when maintained correctly, particularly post-2019 units. Pre-2019 engines have a known timing chain tensioner weakness that requires proactive inspection. With proper maintenance, it can exceed 120,000 miles.

Should I replace an AJ20D6 in a Freelander 2?

It depends on the vehicle's value. A Freelander 2 worth under £5,000 is often not worth replacing – the cost of a recon engine (fitted) frequently exceeds 60% of the vehicle's value. Use our calculator for a personalised answer.

How can I prevent AJ20D6 failure?

Regular oil changes (10,000 miles or annually) using correct‑spec oil, pre‑emptive timing chain tensioner inspection on pre-2019 units, and occasional longer journeys to allow DPF regeneration can prevent the most common failures. Also, address any oil dilution issues promptly.
