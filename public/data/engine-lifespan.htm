Methodology

How we built this lifespan report from real UK failure data.

Source: Internal CRM quote‑request data from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, combined. Full calendar year 2025. 24,650 unique quote requests across all brands; 4,935 of these relate specifically to Land Rover and Range Rover engines.

Lifespan definition: we define "lifespan" as the typical mileage range at which the engine is reported to have suffered a major failure requiring replacement or significant repair. This is derived from the Known Failure Patterns table (cross‑referenced with customer enquiry descriptions, supplier notes, and manufacturer TSBs).

Limitations: our data reflects engines that have failed and been enquired about – it is not a random sample of all Land Rover engines. Engines that never fail are not represented. Therefore, the mileage ranges are indicative of failure points, not a guarantee that all engines of that type will fail in that window. Many engines exceed these mileages with proper care.

Refresh cycle: annually, each February, covering the prior full calendar year. Figures are locked at publication and not silently revised.

What Affects Land Rover Engine Lifespan

The mileage at which an engine fails is not fixed – several factors can push it earlier or later:

  • Service history: Regular oil changes with the correct specification are critical. Extended intervals or incorrect oil can dramatically shorten lifespan – particularly for the Ingenium diesel (oil dilution) and the 306DT (crankshaft bearing).
  • Driving pattern: Short urban trips cause DPF clogging and oil dilution, especially in diesels. Frequent motorway running at operating temperature helps longevity.
  • Model year and revisions: Post‑2019 Ingenium engines have revised timing chain tensioners, improving durability. Earlier 306DT units (pre‑2012) have a higher crankshaft bearing failure risk.
  • Maintenance of cooling system: Overheating is a common cause of early failure – ensuring coolant is flushed and the system is leak‑free extends life.

What This Means For Owners

If you own a Land Rover with an Ingenium engine (204DTD or 204PT), you should be particularly vigilant from 60,000 miles – watch for cold‑start rattle (timing chain) or coolant loss. The V6 diesel units (306DT, 276DT) tend to fail between 80,000 and 140,000, with oil pump and crankshaft bearing issues being the primary culprits. For the 4.4 V8 diesels (448DT, 508PS), they are the most robust – many exceed 150,000 miles without major issues, though oil cooler leaks and DPF regeneration problems can occur.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Land Rover engine lasts the longest?

The 448DT (4.4 TDV8) and 508PS (4.4 SDV8) are the most durable, with typical failure mileage between 100,000 and 200,000 miles – many owners report exceeding 150,000 with good maintenance.

At what mileage do Ingenium diesel engines fail?

The 204DTD Ingenium diesel often shows timing chain and oil dilution issues from 60,000 miles, with many failures occurring between 60,000 and 120,000 miles. Regular oil changes and avoiding short trips can extend this.

Are the 306DT and 276DT V6 diesels reliable?

They have a typical failure window of 80,000–140,000 miles, with crankshaft bearing and oil pump failures being the main issues. They are not the most durable, but many reach over 100,000 miles with diligent servicing.

How can I extend my Land Rover engine's lifespan?

Stick to the recommended service intervals, use the correct oil specification, avoid excessive idling and short trips (especially for diesels), and address any warning lights or unusual noises promptly. Our repair‑or‑scrap framework has more detail.
