Methodology

What this data is, what it isn't, and how to read it correctly.

Source: Internal CRM quote-request data from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, combined. Full calendar year 2025. 24,650 unique quote requests across all brands we cover; 4,935 of these relate specifically to Land Rover and Range Rover engines.

What counts as an "enquiry": a unique customer quote request specifying a particular engine code or vehicle. Repeat requests from the same customer for the same engine are not double-counted.

What this data cannot tell you: our dataset reflects people who contacted us about a replacement engine — it is not a random sample of every Land Rover on UK roads. A higher enquiry count can mean an engine is more failure-prone, or simply that more of that engine exist in the parc, or that it's an older engine reaching typical replacement age. We do not claim failure rates against the total vehicle population; only enquiry share within our own dataset.

Refresh cycle: annually, each February, covering the prior full calendar year. Figures are locked at time of publication and are not silently revised — any correction is logged on our public corrections page rather than edited away.

For a full breakdown of how we tag and verify every number on this page, see our Data Verification and Editorial Standards pages.

What This Data Means

The ranked table above shows which Land Rover engines generated the most replacement enquiries in our 2025 UK data. It is not a reliability ranking in the way a survey of every Land Rover on the road would be. Enquiry volume reflects vehicle parc size, age profile, and owner behaviour as much as engineering quality.

What the data does show clearly is which engines are reaching replacement mileage most frequently in the UK right now. The 204DTD and 306DT are the two most common diesel engines across the modern Land Rover range — fitted to Discovery Sport, Evoque, Range Rover Sport, and full-size Range Rover. Their high enquiry count is consistent with their large installed base, not necessarily a sign that they are uniquely problematic.

The R-to-V (Repair-to-Value) flag is arguably more useful than the raw enquiry count. It tells you whether the cost of a reconditioned replacement sits comfortably within the typical value of the vehicle it's fitted to. Almost every engine on this list clears our 60% safety threshold, meaning replacement is financially sound on the typical host vehicle. The two flagged ⚠️ Watch — 276DT and 224DT — are engines most often fitted to older, lower-value hosts (Discovery 3, Freelander 2), where the calculation is closer and worth checking individually.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Land Rover engine generates the most replacement enquiries?

The 204DTD (Ingenium diesel) leads with 1,150 enquiries in our 2025 data, followed by the 306DT (TDV6/SDV6) at 980. This reflects both engines' popularity across the range, not a claim that either is unreliable by design.

Does a high enquiry count mean an engine is unreliable?

Not on its own. Enquiry volume reflects how many vehicles are on the road with that engine as much as it reflects failure frequency. We pair enquiry data with our separate Known Failure Patterns research to distinguish popularity from reliability.

Is this data representative of all Land Rovers on UK roads?

No, and we want to be upfront about that. Our dataset reflects people who contacted us about a replacement engine, which naturally over-represents engines that fail and under-represents ones that don't. Full methodology and limitations are set out in the Methodology section above.

How often is this data updated?

Annually, each February, covering the prior full calendar year. This data covers 2025 and is the baseline for our 2026 report.

What is the 60% rule and how does it apply here?

The 60% rule is our framework for deciding whether an engine replacement is financially sound. If the lowest viable replacement cost exceeds 60% of the vehicle's current market value, we recommend evaluating the scrap or part-exchange value before committing. This threshold is explained in detail on our Repair, Replace, Scrap Framework page.

Where can I find failure rate data by model rather than engine?

Our Most Enquired Models page ranks chassis codes by enquiry volume. It's the same underlying dataset, filtered and sorted by vehicle rather than engine code.
