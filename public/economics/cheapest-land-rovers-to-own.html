The Cheapest Land Rovers to Own by Engine Cost

These are the models where a major engine failure is least likely to write the car off — either because the replacement is genuinely affordable, or because the vehicle value is high enough to absorb the cost comfortably.

1

Freelander 2 (224DT 2.2 Duratorq)

Recon supply cost: £2,000–£3,500 [EM-QUOTE]

The cheapest engine in the Land Rover range to replace. Ford-sourced, widely available, and supported by every independent diesel specialist in the country. The catch: Freelander 2 values are low, so a £3,500 fitted repair can still exceed 60% of the vehicle's value on older, higher-mileage examples. Our Freelander 2 economics page covers this in detail.

Enquiry volume: 350 requests [EM-VERIFIED]

Typical failure: EGR cooler leaks, turbo actuator failure, injector seal leaks [PATTERN DATA]

2

Discovery Sport (204DTD, post-2019)

Recon supply cost: £3,500–£5,800 [EM-QUOTE]

The most-enquired engine in our data at 1,150 requests [EM-VERIFIED], but post-2019 revisions to the timing chain tensioner have substantially reduced catastrophic failure rates. The key is buying a facelift car or a late pre-facelift with documented service history. Earlier units (2015–2018) are much riskier — see our Ingenium Timing Chain Failure page.

Strong point: Discovery Sport values remain strong enough that a £4,500–£6,000 fitted repair is usually within the 60% threshold.

3

Range Rover L405 (448DT 4.4 SDV8)

Recon supply cost: £6,000–£9,000 [EM-QUOTE]

The most expensive engine on this list, but also the one that is least likely to fail catastrophically. The 448DT has no known bottom-end design flaw, which is rare for a Land Rover diesel. Oil cooler leaks and coolant crossover pipe failures are the main issues, but these are manageable repairs rather than engine-killers. For a vehicle worth £25,000–£55,000, a £7,500–£10,000 fitted replacement is still within the 60% threshold.

Enquiry volume: 320 [EM-VERIFIED]. Full detail in our 448DT Knowledge Centre.

4

Defender L663 (D200 / D240)

Recon supply cost: £3,500–£5,800 [EM-QUOTE]

The Defender's extraordinary residual values make engine replacement a non-event financially. A £5,000–£7,000 fitted repair on a £35,000–£90,000 vehicle is a very different proposition from the same cost on a £4,000 Freelander 2. The engine itself is the same 204DTD Ingenium diesel found in the Discovery Sport, with the same timing chain caveats — but the vehicle value provides a buffer the Discovery Sport doesn't have.

Enquiry volume: 292 [EM-VERIFIED]

The Most Expensive Land Rovers to Own by Engine Cost

These are the models where a major engine failure is most likely to exceed the 60% repair-to-value threshold — the cars that risk becoming financial write-offs when something significant goes wrong.

⚠

Discovery 4 (306DT SDV6 / 276DT TDV6)

Recon supply cost: £6,500–£9,500 [EM-QUOTE]

The most notorious engine in the Land Rover range. Crankshaft bearing failure is catastrophic, often leaving little rebuildable material. With Discovery 4 values typically £6,000–£15,000, a £7,500–£11,000 fitted replacement frequently exceeds 60% of the vehicle's value. This is the engine that has probably written off more Discoverys than any other single fault.

Enquiry volume: 700 (276DT) + 980 (306DT) [EM-VERIFIED]

Key resource: 306DT Crankshaft Bearing Failure and Is It Worth Replacing a Discovery Engine?

⚠

Range Rover Sport L320 (368DT 3.6 TDV8)

Recon supply cost: £5,500–£8,000 [EM-QUOTE]

Early L320s with the 368DT TDV8 (2006–2009) are at significant risk of crankshaft fracture and timing chain tensioner failure. The 3.6 TDV8 is not the reliable 4.4 TDV8 — it shares none of that engine's durability. With L320 values typically £4,000–£15,000, many 368DT failures are effectively write-offs.

Enquiry volume: 650 [EM-VERIFIED]

Key resource: 368DT Turbo & Injector Failure

⚠

Freelander 2 (any major engine failure)

Recon supply cost: £2,000–£5,800 depending on engine [EM-QUOTE]

Paradoxically, the cheapest engine to replace is also one of the highest-risk write-off candidates. Freelander 2 values are now £2,500–£7,000 for most examples. A £3,500 fitted repair on a £4,000 car is 87% of the vehicle's value — a clear scrap verdict. The same repair on a £7,000 example is 50% — a safe repair. This is the model where the 60% threshold produces the widest range of outcomes.

Key resource: Freelander 2 Scrap vs Repair Case Study

⚠

Evoque L538 (pre-2019 204DTD)

Recon supply cost: £3,500–£5,800 [EM-QUOTE]

Early 204DTD Ingenium diesel units with the original timing chain tensioner are the same risk as Discovery Sport pre-facelift cars, but Evoque values are lower than Discovery Sport values for comparable years. A £5,000 fitted replacement on a £7,000–£10,000 Evoque L538 often fails the 60% threshold.

Enquiry volume: 244 (L538) + 349 (L551) [EM-VERIFIED]

How the 60% Rule Determines "Cheapest to Own"

An engine can be cheap to replace but still be expensive to own if a failure writes the car off. This is the key insight behind our ranking, and why "cheapest to own" is not the same as "cheapest engine to replace."

The paradox of the Freelander 2: the 224DT engine is the cheapest in the Land Rover range to replace (£2,000–£3,500 supply-only, fitted cost £2,500–£4,200). Yet a Freelander 2 with a failed 224DT is often a write-off because the vehicle itself is worth only £2,500–£7,000. A £4,000 fitted repair on a £4,500 car is 89% of the vehicle's value — the repair makes no financial sense.

The safety of the Defender L663: the same 204DTD engine that writes off Evoques and Discovery Sports costs £3,500–£5,800 supply-only. Fitted cost of £4,500–£7,000 on a £35,000–£90,000 Defender is only 5–20% of the vehicle's value. The car is cheap to own relative to its value even though the engine itself costs exactly the same as in a cheaper car.

What this means for buyers: the cheapest Land Rovers to own are not the cheapest to buy. A £4,000 Freelander 2 and a £15,000 Discovery Sport both have the potential for expensive engine failures, but the Defender and L405 have enough value to absorb that cost. The sweet spot is a model with reasonable engine replacement costs and strong enough residual values to keep the R-to-V ratio well below 60%.

The models that pass this test most consistently: L405 Range Rover (448DT), Defender L663 (all engines), post-2019 Discovery Sport (higher values + improved engine), and L494 Range Rover Sport on higher-value examples. The models that fail it most often: Freelander 2 (any major failure), pre-2013 Discovery 4 (306DT), and L538 Evoque with early Ingenium diesel.

Our Verdict: Which Land Rover Is Actually Cheapest to Own?

Based on the full 2025 dataset — 9,000+ real enquiries, 24,650 quote requests, and hundreds of specialist interviews — here is our definitive ranking of the cheapest Land Rovers to own by engine cost.

🏆

Winners: Best Value Ownership

  • 1. Land Rover Defender L663 (D200/D240) — strong values + reasonable recon cost = 5–17% R-to-V
  • 2. Range Rover L405 (448DT 4.4 SDV8) — durable V8, high values, no catastrophic design flaw
  • 3. Discovery Sport (post-2019, 204DTD revised) — improved engine + values high enough to absorb a failure
  • 4. Range Rover Sport L494 (high-value examples, 306DT) — risk is real, but high values often keep R-to-V below 60%
🚫

Losers: Highest Ownership Cost Risk

  • 1. Freelander 2 (any engine) — cheapest engine to replace, but lowest vehicle values = highest write-off risk
  • 2. Discovery 4 (306DT / 276DT) — catastrophic crank failure + falling values = frequent write-offs
  • 3. Range Rover Sport L320 (368DT 3.6 TDV8) — early TDV8 risk + ageing vehicle values = high write-off rate
  • 4. Evoque L538 (pre-2019 204DTD) — lower values than Discovery Sport with same engine risk

The honest conclusion: the cheapest Land Rover to own is a well-maintained post-2019 Discovery Sport with the revised 204DTD — it has a relatively affordable engine to replace, strong enough residual values to keep the R-to-V ratio safe, and the Ingenium timing chain issue was substantially improved in the facelift. The most expensive is a Freelander 2 or Discovery 4 with any major engine failure, where the repair often costs more than the car is worth.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest Land Rover to own?

The cheapest Land Rover to own by engine cost is the post-2019 Discovery Sport with the revised 204DTD diesel, followed by the Defender L663 (D200/D240) and the L405 Range Rover with the 448DT 4.4 SDV8. The Freelander 2 has the cheapest engine to replace, but its low vehicle values mean a major failure often writes the car off.

Is a Land Rover expensive to maintain?

It depends entirely on the model and engine. A well-maintained Defender L663 or L405 Range Rover with the 448DT V8 has relatively predictable running costs. A Discovery 4 with the 306DT SDV6, by contrast, is a very expensive car to keep on the road — one major failure can cost £8,000–£12,000 to fix, which often exceeds the vehicle's value.

Which Land Rover engine is most reliable?

The 448DT 4.4 SDV8 is the most reliable large diesel in the Land Rover range, with no catastrophic design flaw. The 224DT 2.2 Duratorq (Ford-sourced) is robust and cheap to repair. The post-2019 204DTD Ingenium diesel is significantly improved over the pre-2019 units. Our 448DT Knowledge Centre and 224DT Knowledge Centre have full details.

How much does it cost to replace a Land Rover engine?

Supply-only costs range from £1,800–£3,500 for a 224DT Freelander engine to £6,500–£9,500 for a 306DT SDV6 and up to £12,000 for a 508PS SDV8. Fitted costs add 8–20 hours labour at £65–£75/hr (or significantly more in London/SE). Full data is in our Replacement Cost by Engine Code table.

Is a Freelander 2 worth repairing?

If the repair cost is less than 60% of the vehicle's current value, yes. The problem is that Freelander 2 values are often £2,500–£5,000, and even a relatively cheap 224DT repair (£3,000–£4,000 fitted) can exceed that threshold. Our Freelander 2 Scrap vs Repair Case Study covers this in detail.

Which Land Rover should I avoid?

High-risk models include the Discovery 4 with the 306DT SDV6 (catastrophic crank failure), the Range Rover Sport L320 with the 368DT TDV8 (early TDV8 issues), and any pre-2019 Ingenium diesel without documented timing chain work. Our Failure Database and Engine Failure Index 2026 provide the full picture.
