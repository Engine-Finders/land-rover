The short answer: for most Velars built after 2019 and valued above £22,000, a reconditioned engine replacement is financially viable. For earlier D180 and D240 diesels near the bottom of their value curve, the same repair can tip into scrap territory. The number that matters is your car's current trade value, not its original list price.

  • D180 / D240 (2.0 Ingenium diesel) — recon supply-and-fit typically £3,500–£5,800 [EM-QUOTE]; safe on vehicles above £12,000, watch below
  • D300 (3.0 V6 diesel) — recon supply-and-fit typically £6,500–£9,500 [EM-QUOTE]; requires £14,000+ vehicle value to clear the 60% threshold
  • P250 (2.0 Ingenium petrol) — recon supply-and-fit typically £3,000–£5,000 [EM-QUOTE]; safe on almost all Velars, as petrol Ingenium is more reliable and cheaper to replace
  • Our 60% rule means a £5,000 repair on a £10,000 Velar is a scrap verdict; the same repair on a £18,000 Velar is a safe repair

When the Honest Answer Is No: Velar Scrap Scenarios

A site that always recommends replacement is a sales funnel wearing an editorial costume. Here are the Velar scenarios where our data says the honest answer is to walk away.

🚫

Pre-2019 D180 / D240 at 100k+

Where a timing chain failure coincides with a tired gearbox and worn suspension, the combined repair bill often exceeds 70% of the vehicle's value. The honest advice is usually to fund a different car rather than stack repairs on this one — see our scrap threshold guide for the full reasoning.

🚫

D300 with Crankshaft Bearing Failure

At £6,500–£9,500 supply-and-fit [EM-QUOTE], a D300 recon engine on a sub-£12,000 Velar fails the 60% threshold. If the vehicle has other issues, selling for parts or to a specialist breaker is the financially honest outcome. See our 306DT crankshaft bearing failure page for the full mechanism.

🚫

Any Velar Below £8,000 Trade Value

Below this line, almost no engine fault clears the 60% threshold. This includes early, high-mileage D180s and D240s with history gaps. Our Most Expensive Land Rover Repairs page shows where this threshold gets tested hardest across the entire range.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a Velar engine replacement worth it in 2025?

For most Velars valued above £14,000, yes — a reconditioned engine replacement is financially viable. The only exception is the D300's more expensive replacement cost on sub-£12,000 examples, where the 60% rule tips into scrap territory. Use our matrix above to check your specific case.

How much does a Velar D180 engine replacement cost?

A reconditioned D180 (204DTD) engine typically costs £3,500–£5,800 supply-and-fit [EM-QUOTE], while a used unit is £2,000–£3,500. The revised post-2019 engines are more reliable than pre-2019 examples, so the reconditioned route is often the better value.

Is the Velar P250 petrol engine more reliable than the diesel?

Yes — the Ingenium petrol (204PT) is more reliable than the diesel (204DTD), with fewer timing chain and oil dilution issues. It is also cheaper to replace, at £3,000–£5,000 recon vs the diesel's £3,500–£5,800. See our Ingenium Diesel vs Petrol comparison for a full breakdown.

What's the scrap value of a Velar with a failed engine?

A Velar with a seized or catastrophic engine failure typically sells for £1,500–£4,000 as a parts donor or to a specialist breaker, depending on the model year and overall condition. This is rarely enough to fund a replacement engine. Our scrap value guide explains how this figure is reached.

Should I replace a Velar engine or sell the car?

If your Velar's repair-to-value ratio is below 60%, replace it. If it's above 60%, sell it as a non-runner or for parts. The worked example above shows how this is calculated — always use your vehicle's current trade value, not what you paid for it.
