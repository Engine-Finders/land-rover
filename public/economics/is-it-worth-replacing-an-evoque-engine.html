The short answer: for most Evoques built after 2019 and valued above £14,000, a reconditioned engine replacement is financially viable. For earlier 2.2 diesel (224DT) and pre-2019 Ingenium diesel (204DTD) models near the bottom of their value curve, the same repair can tip into scrap territory. The number that matters is your car's current trade value, not its original list price.

  • 2.2 Diesel (224DT, L538 pre-2015) — recon supply-and-fit typically £2,000–£3,500 [EM-QUOTE]; safe on vehicles above £6,000, watch below
  • 2.0 Ingenium Diesel (204DTD, L538 2015–2018 / L551 pre-2019) — recon supply-and-fit typically £3,500–£5,800 [EM-QUOTE]; requires £10,000+ vehicle value to clear the 60% threshold; post-2019 revised units safer
  • 2.0 Ingenium Petrol (204PT, P250/P300) — recon supply-and-fit typically £3,000–£5,000 [EM-QUOTE]; safe on almost all Evoques, as petrol Ingenium is more reliable and cheaper to replace
  • Our 60% rule means a £4,500 repair on a £9,000 Evoque is a scrap verdict; the same repair on a £16,000 Evoque is a safe repair

When the Honest Answer Is No: Evoque Scrap Scenarios

A site that always recommends replacement is a sales funnel wearing an editorial costume. Here are the Evoque scenarios where our data says the honest answer is to walk away.

🚫

Pre-2015 2.2 Diesel with High Mileage

Where a 224DT failure coincides with a tired gearbox and worn suspension, the combined repair bill often exceeds 70% of the vehicle's value. The honest advice is usually to fund a different car rather than stack repairs on this one — see our scrap threshold guide for the full reasoning.

🚫

Pre-2019 Ingenium Diesel at 100k+

A timing chain replacement on a 204DTD can cost £4,500+ supply-and-fit [EM-QUOTE], and if the chain has already failed catastrophically, a full engine replacement may be required. On an L538 valued at £7,000 or less, this fails the 60% threshold. See our Ingenium diesel timing chain failure page for the full mechanism.

🚫

Any Evoque Below £6,000 Trade Value

Below this line, almost no engine fault clears the 60% threshold. This includes early high-mileage L538 diesels with history gaps. Our Most Expensive Land Rover Repairs page shows where this threshold gets tested hardest across the entire range.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an Evoque engine replacement worth it in 2025?

For most Evoques valued above £10,000, yes — a reconditioned engine replacement is financially viable. The only exception is the early 2.2 diesel and pre-2019 Ingenium diesel on sub-£8,000 examples, where the 60% rule tips into scrap territory. Use our matrix above to check your specific case.

How much does an Evoque 2.0 diesel engine replacement cost?

A reconditioned 204DTD (Ingenium diesel) engine typically costs £3,500–£5,800 supply-and-fit [EM-QUOTE], while a used unit is £2,000–£3,500. The revised post-2019 engines are more reliable than pre-2019 examples, so the reconditioned route is often the better value.

Is the Evoque petrol engine more reliable than the diesel?

Yes — the Ingenium petrol (204PT) is more reliable than the diesel (204DTD), with fewer timing chain and oil dilution issues. It is also cheaper to replace, at £3,000–£5,000 recon vs the diesel's £3,500–£5,800. See our Ingenium Diesel vs Petrol comparison for a full breakdown.

What's the scrap value of an Evoque with a failed engine?

An Evoque with a seized or catastrophic engine failure typically sells for £1,000–£3,000 as a parts donor or to a specialist breaker, depending on the model year and overall condition. This is rarely enough to fund a replacement engine. Our scrap value guide explains how this figure is reached.

Should I replace an Evoque engine or sell the car?

If your Evoque's repair-to-value ratio is below 60%, replace it. If it's above 60%, sell it as a non-runner or for parts. The worked example above shows how this is calculated — always use your vehicle's current trade value, not what you paid for it.
