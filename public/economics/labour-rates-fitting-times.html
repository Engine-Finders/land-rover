Factors That Affect Fitting Times and Labour Costs

Not all engine replacements are equal. Here's what can increase or decrease the labour time — and the final bill.

🔧

Engine Accessibility

4‑cylinder engines like the 204DTD and 224DT are relatively accessible — most work is done from the top and bottom with standard tools. V6 and V8 engines require subframe removal, exhaust disconnection and more complex disassembly, adding hours.

⚙️

Ancillary Stripping & Reassembly

If ancillaries like turbos, injectors or the air conditioning system need to be removed and refitted, labour hours increase. Sometimes these parts fail during removal, adding both parts and labour cost.

🛠️

Vehicle Condition & Corrosion

Older vehicles, especially those used off-road, can have seized bolts, corroded subframes or damaged wiring. These add significant time — sometimes 2–4 extra hours — to a job that would otherwise be straightforward.

📍

Regional Labour Rates

As shown above, London and South East rates are significantly higher. If you're outside these regions, you may save 20–40% on labour alone — even factoring in transport costs, it can be worth travelling to a specialist in the Midlands or North.

How to Save on Labour Costs

Labour is often the biggest variable in an engine replacement quote. Here are practical ways to reduce it without compromising quality.

  1. Get multiple quotes – independent specialists can vary by 20–30% for the same job. Always compare at least three.
  2. Consider travelling to a lower-cost region – if you're in London/SE, a specialist in the Midlands might save you £500–£1,000 on labour alone, even after transport.
  3. Ask for a fixed-price labour quote – this protects you if the job takes longer than estimated due to seized bolts or unexpected issues.
  4. Choose an engine with lower labour hours – if you're replacing an engine and have a choice, 4‑cylinder units cost significantly less to fit than V6 or V8.
  5. Prepare the vehicle – cleaning the engine bay and removing non-essential items can reduce labour time; ask your specialist what you can do to help.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do main dealers charge more for labour?

Main dealers have higher overheads (premises, equipment, training, manufacturer fees) and use genuine parts at premium prices. They also often include a comprehensive warranty. For older Land Rovers outside warranty, independent specialists offer comparable quality at significantly lower cost.

Can I supply my own engine and just pay for fitting?

Yes — many specialists will fit a customer-supplied engine, but they may not offer a warranty on the engine itself (only on the fitting work). Always check whether the specialist is willing to do this and what their warranty terms are. See our reconditioned vs used vs rebuilt guide for help sourcing an engine.

How do I know if a labour quote is fair?

Compare it against the typical labour hours in our table above. Multiply the hours by the hourly rate quoted. If the total is significantly above our range, ask why. Also check whether diagnostics, ancillaries and delivery are included — these are often charged separately.

Does the 60% rule apply to labour costs?

Yes — the 60% repair-to-value rule applies to the total supply-and-fit cost, including labour and ancillaries. If the total exceeds 60% of your vehicle's current value, replacement may not be financially sensible, regardless of the labour cost breakdown.

What's included in a "fitted premium"?

The fitted premium is the total labour cost for the job, typically quoted as a single figure by specialists. It includes the removal of the old engine, fitting the new one, reconnecting systems, refilling fluids and testing. It does not include ancillaries like gaskets, seals, oil or filters — those are often extra. See our Supply & Fit Explained page for a full breakdown.
