The short answer: if a repair or replacement engine costs more than 60% of your Land Rover's current market value, the honest verdict is usually to scrap it or sell it for parts rather than repair it — but the number that matters is your car's value, not its original price.

  • Our threshold is 60% for Land Rover and Range Rover, versus 70% for BMW and Mercedes-Benz, reflecting a lower typical parts and labour baseline [PATTERN DATA]
  • Across our 9,000+ 2025 UK enquiries [EM-VERIFIED], the models most likely to fail this threshold are higher-mileage Freelander 2s and early Discovery Sports
  • The models least likely to fail it are Defenders and post-2019 Discovery 5s, whose residual values comfortably absorb even a full engine replacement
  • A reconditioned engine typically costs 30–45% more than a used one, but carries a warranty a used unit doesn't [PATTERN DATA]

How We Calculate the 60% Threshold

This is the part most sites don't show you. Here is exactly how the rule is built, so you can judge whether it's fair rather than take our word for it.

1

Repair-to-Value Ratio

We divide the full supply-and-fit quote by the vehicle's current trade value, not its original list price or what you paid for it. A ten-year-old Discovery is not worth what you paid — the ratio only works against today's figure.

2

The 60% Line

Below 60%, our enquiry data [EM-VERIFIED] shows owners who repair are substantially more likely to report the decision as worthwhile a year later. Above it, the split shifts sharply toward regret or early re-sale.

3

Why Not a Fixed Number

A flat "£5,000 and under" rule ignores that a £5,000 repair on a £6,000 car and a £5,000 repair on a £20,000 car are completely different decisions. Ratio, not absolute cost, is what actually predicts regret.

One honest limitation: our data is enquiry-based, meaning it reflects people who contacted us about a failure, not a random sample of every Land Rover on the road. That means our failure-rate figures should be read as share of enquiries, not share of the fleet. Full detail on this is on our data methodology page. It's also worth noting the threshold is deliberately conservative — set slightly below the point our data suggests most owners actually break even, to account for the uncertainty in any single vehicle valuation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 60% rule for Land Rover engine replacement?

The 60% rule is our repair-to-value threshold: if a repair or replacement engine costs more than 60% of your Land Rover's current market value, the honest verdict is usually to scrap or sell for parts rather than repair. It is a starting guide, not an absolute cut-off, and we always show the full working so you can judge your own case.

Why is the threshold 60% for Land Rover and not a different figure?

60% reflects the typical parts and labour baseline for Land Rover and Jaguar engines in our enquiry data. BMW and Mercedes-Benz sit at 70% in our data, reflecting a higher typical cost baseline for those brands. The figure is reviewed against real enquiry outcomes rather than fixed arbitrarily.

Is a reconditioned engine always cheaper than a used one?

No. A used engine is usually the cheapest supply-and-fit option but carries more uncertainty over remaining life and history. A reconditioned engine costs more but comes with a warranty and a known rebuild standard. Which is worth it depends on how long you plan to keep the vehicle.

Does the 60% rule apply the same way to every Land Rover model?

The rule itself is constant, but the answer it produces varies enormously by model because vehicle values vary so much across the range. A Defender's strong residual values mean the same repair cost clears the threshold comfortably; a higher-mileage Freelander 2 near the bottom of its value curve fails the same threshold at a far lower repair cost.

Should I get an independent valuation before deciding?

Yes. The value side of this calculation matters as much as the cost side, and a stale valuation in your head is the most common reason people get the decision wrong in either direction.
