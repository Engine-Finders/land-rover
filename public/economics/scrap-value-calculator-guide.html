The 60% Rule — The Foundation of Every Scrap Decision

If you take one thing away from this guide, it's this: the 60% rule is the single most reliable predictor of whether you'll regret repairing your Land Rover or be glad you did.

The calculation is simple: divide the full supply-and-fit cost of the repair or replacement engine by your Land Rover's current market value. If the result is below 60%, the repair is financially sensible. Above 60%, the honest verdict is usually to scrap the vehicle or sell it for parts.

Why 60%? Our enquiry data [EM-VERIFIED] shows that owners who spend less than 60% of their vehicle's value on an engine replacement are substantially more likely to report the decision as worthwhile a year later. Above that line, the split shifts sharply toward regret or early re-sale. The threshold is set deliberately conservative — slightly below the point our data suggests most owners actually break even — to account for the uncertainty in any single vehicle valuation.

Why not a fixed number? A flat "£5,000 and under" rule ignores that a £5,000 repair on a £6,000 vehicle and a £5,000 repair on a £20,000 vehicle are completely different decisions. Ratio, not absolute cost, is what actually predicts regret.

The most common mistake:

Using the original purchase price or the car's value five years ago. The calculation only works against today's trade or private sale value. A 2010 Freelander 2 bought for £10,000 in 2015 may now be worth £3,000 — the ratio changes dramatically.

How to Calculate Your Land Rover's Scrap Value Decision

Follow these four steps to determine whether your Land Rover is a repair or a scrap decision. The process takes less than five minutes and uses real data from our 24,650 quote requests [EM-VERIFIED].

1

Step 1 — Get Your Vehicle's Current Value

Check your Land Rover's current trade and private sale value using AutoTrader, Motors.co.uk or Parkers. Use the lower end of the range — this is a conservative calculation. A £8,000 car with a £5,000 repair is 62.5% — a scrap decision. The same car valued at £10,000 is 50% — a safe repair. The difference is £2,000 of valuation.

Tip: The value side of this calculation matters as much as the cost side. A stale valuation in your head is the most common reason people get the decision wrong.

2

Step 2 — Get a Qualified Repair or Replacement Quote

Get a quote from a specialist, not a general garage. The quote should be for the full fitted cost — supply of the engine, labour, ancillaries (fluids, gaskets, belts) and any additional work identified. Independent specialist rates are typically £65–£75/hr in the North/Midlands, higher in London/SE.

Tip: Always get at least two quotes. If the first quote is £6,000 and the second is £7,500, the lower quote changes the calculation significantly. Our model-specific economics pages show typical costs for each engine.

3

Step 3 — Apply the 60% Rule

Divide the fitted cost by the vehicle value. If the result is below 60%, the repair is financially sensible. Above 60%, it's usually a scrap decision. Between 50% and 70%, the answer depends on the vehicle's overall condition — a well-maintained example with no other faults may justify a higher spend.

Example: £5,500 fitted repair ÷ £10,000 vehicle value = 55% — ✅ Safe. £5,500 ÷ £8,000 = 69% — ⚠️ Watch. £5,500 ÷ £6,000 = 92% — 🔴 Scrap.

4

Step 4 — Consider the Whole Vehicle

The 60% rule is a starting guide, not an absolute cut-off. A low-mileage, rot-free Defender with full service history may justify a repair above the threshold. A tired Freelander 2 with a failing gearbox, corroded subframe and worn suspension probably won't clear it even below. Always assess the whole vehicle.

Tip: If the chassis needs £1,500 of welding and the subframe needs replacing, add those costs to the repair total before applying the 60% rule.

Our Verdict: How to Use This Scrap Value Guide

Based on the full 2025 dataset — 24,650 quote requests and 9,000+ real enquiries — here is our definitive advice on when to scrap your Land Rover.

The short verdict:

Use the 60% rule as your starting point, then assess the whole vehicle.

Scrap if: The repair cost exceeds 60% of the vehicle's value — OR — the vehicle has significant corrosion, a failing gearbox, or multiple other faults that add to the total repair bill.

Repair if: The repair cost is below 60% of the vehicle's value — AND — the vehicle is in good overall condition with no significant other faults.

Watch zone: If the repair cost is 40–70% of the vehicle's value, the decision is marginal. Use our Repair vs Replace Calculator with your own valuation and quote.

✅

Models That Usually Clear the Threshold

  • Defender L663 — high values mean repair cost is a fraction of vehicle value
  • Range Rover L405 (448DT) — durable engine + strong residual values
  • Defender Classic (300Tdi, Td5) — strong collector values
  • Discovery 5 (2017+) — strong residual values
🚫

Models That Usually Fail the Threshold

  • Freelander 2 (any major failure) — low values + any repair cost = high R-to-V
  • Discovery 4 (306DT, sub-£10k) — catastrophic failure + low values = write-off
  • Range Rover Sport L320 (276DT/368DT) — low values + expensive repairs
  • Discovery 3 (any major failure) — values too low to justify most repairs

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate the scrap value of my Land Rover?

Get a current trade valuation (AutoTrader, Parkers) and a full fitted repair quote. Divide the repair cost by the vehicle value. If the result is above 60%, the honest verdict is usually to scrap or sell for parts. Use our calculator to do this automatically.

What is the 60% rule for scrapping a Land Rover?

The 60% rule is our repair-to-value threshold: if the fitted repair cost is more than 60% of your Land Rover's current market value, the honest verdict is usually to scrap or sell for parts. Full explanation is on our Repair, Replace or Scrap Framework page.

What Land Rover models are most likely to be scrapped?

Freelander 2s (any major engine failure), Discovery 4s with 306DT failure, and Range Rover Sport L320s with 276DT or 368DT failure are the models most likely to exceed the 60% threshold. See the By Model table above for full detail.

What is the scrap value of a Land Rover with a failed engine?

The scrap value depends on the model, condition and location. A Freelander 2 with a failed engine might be worth £300–£800 as scrap, while a Discovery 4 might be £500–£1,500. Selling for parts is often more profitable — check When Should You Scrap a Land Rover? for guidance.

When is it worth repairing a Land Rover instead of scrapping it?

It's worth repairing if the fitted repair cost is below 60% of the vehicle's value, and the vehicle is in good overall condition. Defender L663, L405 Range Rovers and well-maintained Classic Defenders are the models most likely to clear the threshold.

Can I get a quote for scrapping my Land Rover?

We don't offer scrapping services directly, but our Repair vs Replace Calculator will show you whether a repair is financially sensible. If it isn't, your next step is to contact a specialist breaker or scrap yard for a valuation.
