The short answer: a supply and fit quote for a Land Rover engine typically includes the engine unit itself, labour to remove and install it, and the necessary replacement gaskets, seals and fluids. But not every quote is the same — some suppliers exclude ancillaries, delivery charges, or diagnostic time, adding hundreds to the final bill.

  • Supply — the engine unit (used, reconditioned or rebuilt) plus delivery charge, often excluded from the headline price
  • Fit — labour for removal and installation, usually 10–20+ hours depending on engine complexity, at £65–£90/hr indie rates [PATTERN DATA]
  • Ancillaries — gaskets, seals, oil, coolant, filters and sundries, typically £200–£500 extra
  • Hidden extras — diagnostics, warranty, delivery, or unexpected parts like timing chains or turbos can push the final bill up by 20–50%

What Is "Fit" in a Supply and Fit Quote?

The "fit" part covers the labour to remove your old engine and install the replacement. But labour hours vary significantly by engine type and workshop.

⏱️

Straightforward Engines

10–14 hours — 204DTD, 204PT, 224DT. These are relatively accessible and well-documented, with good parts availability and clear workshop guides. Indie labour at £65/hr gives a fitted premium of £650–£910.

⏱️

Complex V6 & V8 Engines

14–24 hours — 276DT, 306DT, 368DT, 448DT, 508PS. These require subframe work, more disassembly and specialist knowledge. Indie labour at £65/hr gives a fitted premium of £910–£1,560.

⏱️

What Labour Actually Includes

Removal of old engine, stripping ancillaries, fitting new engine, reconnecting all systems, refilling fluids, and testing. It does not include diagnostic time to confirm the fault, which is often charged separately.

For a full breakdown of labour hours by engine code, see our Labour Rates & Fitting Times guide. London and South East workshops typically charge 40–60% more than the national average.

Hidden Extras: What Could Catch You Out

The gap between a headline "supply and fit" price and the final invoice can be significant. Here are the most common hidden extras to watch for.

⚠️

Diagnostic Time

Confirming the fault before the job starts is rarely included. Typical diagnostic fees are £80–£150, and if the fault is intermittent, it can take longer. Always ask if diagnostics are included.

⚠️

Ancillary Parts Failures

During removal, parts like turbos, injectors or sensors can be found to be failing. Replacing them adds cost and time — and is almost never included in the headline quote. Our failure database shows which engines are most prone to this.

⚠️

Engine Warranty & Registration

Some suppliers charge extra for registration of the warranty or to "activate" it. This is often a nominal fee (£25–£50) but it's another line item to check.

The safest way to avoid surprises is to get a fixed‑price supply and fit quote that includes all ancillaries and diagnostics. If a supplier won't give you a fixed price, ask for a detailed breakdown of every potential extra before you commit.

The Verdict: What You Should Actually Ask For

When getting quotes, ask for a fixed‑price supply and fit quote that includes all the items above — engine unit, labour, gaskets, fluids, filters, diagnostics and sundries.

If a supplier quotes you a headline price that seems too low, it almost certainly excludes some of the ancillaries or diagnostics. Use the breakdown above to ask the right questions:

  • Is this a fixed price or an estimate?
  • What ancillaries are included? — gaskets, seals, oil, coolant, filters
  • Is diagnostic time included?
  • Is delivery included?
  • What is the warranty and what does it cover?

Frequently Asked Questions

What is included in a "supply and fit" quote?

A full supply and fit quote should include the engine unit, labour to remove and install it, plus gaskets, seals, oil, coolant and filters. Some quotes also include diagnostics and delivery, but not all — always check.

Why do supply and fit quotes vary so much?

Variation comes from the type of engine (used vs recon vs rebuilt), labour time (10–24+ hours depending on complexity), and what ancillaries are included. Some quotes exclude delivery, diagnostics or sundries, making the headline price seem lower.

How do I know if a quote is fair?

Compare it against the typical costs in this guide. For your engine code, check the recon supply‑only cost in our replacement cost table, add the labour hours from our labour guide, and add £200–£500 for ancillaries. If the quote is significantly above or below, ask why.

Are main dealer supply and fit costs higher?

Yes — main dealer labour rates are typically 30–50% higher than independent specialists, and their parts pricing is also above independent supply. For older Land Rovers, the value proposition often favours independents.

What should I do if my quote excludes something?

Ask for a revised quote that includes the missing items. If the supplier won't give you a fixed price for everything, consider getting quotes from other specialists through our network.
