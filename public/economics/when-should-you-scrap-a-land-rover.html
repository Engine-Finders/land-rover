The short answer: you should scrap a Land Rover when the repair or replacement cost exceeds 60% of the vehicle's current market value. That threshold is the single best predictor of whether owners regret repairing. Below it, most are glad they fixed it; above it, most wish they had walked away.

  • Our threshold is 60% for Land Rover and Range Rover, versus 70% for BMW and Mercedes-Benz, reflecting a lower typical parts and labour baseline [PATTERN DATA]
  • Across our 9,000+ 2025 UK enquiries [EM‑VERIFIED], the models most likely to fail this threshold are higher‑mileage Freelander 2s, early Discovery Sports, and Discovery 4s with 306DT failures
  • The models least likely to fail it are Defenders (all generations) and post‑2019 Discovery 5s, whose residual values comfortably absorb even a full engine replacement
  • A reconditioned engine typically costs 30–45% more than a used one, but carries a warranty a used unit doesn't [PATTERN DATA]

When the Honest Answer Is "Scrap It"

Sometimes the best financial decision is to walk away. These are the Land Rover scenarios where our data suggests scrapping or parting out is the better choice.

🚫

Freelander 2 with 224DT Failure

Late in its value curve, a 224DT diesel replacement frequently exceeds 70% of trade value. With typical Freelander 2 values under £5,000, a recon at £2,000–£3,500 often pushes the ratio above 60%. Our full analysis is in Is It Worth Replacing a Freelander Engine?

🚫

Discovery 4 SDV6 Worth Under £10,000

The 306DT crankshaft failure is catastrophic and expensive. If your Discovery 4 is worth less than £10,000, a recon at £6,500–£9,500 will almost certainly exceed the 60% threshold. We cover this in detail in our Discovery SDV6 case study and our dedicated 306DT write‑off guide.

🚫

Early Discovery Sport with 204DTD Failure

Pre‑2019 Ingenium diesel tensioner failures are common, and with early Discovery Sport values now sitting below £10,000 on many examples, a recon at £3,500–£5,800 can push the ratio to 60% or higher – especially if the vehicle also needs suspension or gearbox work. See Discovery Sport economics for the full picture.

🚫

Any Land Rover Below £4,000 Trade Value

Below this line, almost no engine fault clears the 60% threshold. Selling for parts or to a specialist breaker is usually the financially honest outcome – our scrap value guide explains how that figure is reached.

🚫

Vehicles with Multiple Secondary Faults

If your Land Rover also needs a gearbox, air suspension or a new infotainment system, the cumulative repair cost can push the total well beyond the value of the vehicle – even if the engine alone would have cleared the threshold. Use our Repair vs Replace Calculator to factor in all costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 60% rule for scrapping a Land Rover?

The 60% rule is our repair‑to‑value threshold: if a repair or replacement engine costs more than 60% of your Land Rover's current market value, the honest verdict is usually to scrap or sell for parts rather than repair. It is a starting guide, not an absolute cut‑off, and we always show the full working so you can judge your own case.

How do I find out my Land Rover's current value?

Use a combination of Auto Trader, Parkers, and your local trade guide – but the most accurate figure is what a dealer would offer you part‑ex, or what similar vehicles are actually selling for. Our Engine Value Checker can help you get a ballpark figure based on your model, year and mileage.

Is a used engine ever a better option than scrapping?

Yes – a used engine is usually cheaper (£1,500–£4,500) and can sometimes bring the repair‑to‑value ratio below 60% even on a lower‑value vehicle. However, a used engine carries no warranty and unknown history. A reconditioned engine costs more but includes a rebuild warranty. Our Reconditioned vs Used vs Rebuilt guide explains the trade‑offs.

Should I scrap a Land Rover with a 306DT failure?

It depends on the vehicle's value. If your Discovery 4 or Range Rover Sport is worth less than about £11,000–£16,000, a 306DT failure is almost always a write‑off. If it's worth more than that, repair may be viable. Our 306DT write‑off guide has a full breakdown by model.

Does the 60% rule apply the same way to every Land Rover model?

The rule itself is constant, but the answer it produces varies enormously by model because vehicle values vary so much across the range. A Defender's strong residual values mean the same repair cost clears the threshold comfortably; a higher‑mileage Freelander 2 near the bottom of its value curve fails the same threshold at a far lower repair cost.

What should I do with my Land Rover if I decide to scrap it?

Contact a specialist breaker – many will collect the vehicle and offer a cash price based on the condition of the body, interior and remaining mechanical parts. We recommend getting at least three quotes before committing. Our scrap value guide explains how to get the best price.
