306DT Crankshaft Bearing Failure — Symptoms, Cost & Diagnosis

The 306DT (3.0 SDV6/TDV6) is one of Land Rover's most powerful diesel engines — but it has a well-documented and potentially catastrophic crankshaft bearing failure. The Short Answer

The 306DT crankshaft bearing failure is a catastrophic engine failure that typically occurs between 80,000 and 140,000 miles. It is caused by fatigue in the tin-aluminium bearing coating, which can lead to a snapped crankshaft and complete engine destruction. Early warning signs include a cold-start knock and low oil pressure warnings.

  • Typical mileage at failure: 80,000–140,000 miles [PATTERN DATA]

Symptom Progression — What Owners Notice, In Order

The 306DT crankshaft bearing failure doesn't happen overnight. It gives progressive warnings that, if caught early, can save your engine. Here's what owners typically notice, in the order it appears over weeks or months.

  1. Stage 1 – Cold-start knock. A distinct knocking or rumbling noise from the engine when started from cold, which often fades as the engine warms up. This is the single most important early warning sign.
  2. Stage 2 – Low oil pressure warning. The oil pressure warning light may flicker or illuminate, especially at idle when the engine is hot. This indicates that bearing wear is affecting oil pressure.
  3. Stage 3 – Metallic debris in oil. A mechanic may find fine metal particles (bearing material) in the oil filter or sump during a routine service. This confirms bearing wear is occurring.
  4. Stage 4 – Engine knocking under load. The knocking becomes audible even when the engine is warm, particularly under acceleration or uphill. The engine may feel down on power.
  5. Stage 5 – Catastrophic failure. The crankshaft bearing seizes or the crankshaft snaps, often resulting in a loud bang, complete loss of power, and oil pouring from the engine. At this point, a full engine replacement is almost always required.

If you experience any of these symptoms, do not continue driving. A simple oil pressure test and oil analysis can often confirm the issue before it becomes catastrophic. For a broader view of Land Rover engine noises, see our engine knocking noise guide.

Root Cause — Why 306DT Crankshaft Bearings Fail

The 306DT (also known as the 3.0 SDV6 or TDV6) is a Ford-sourced "Lion" V6 diesel engine used extensively in Land Rover products. Its crankshaft bearing failure is a well-documented design weakness that stems from the tin-aluminium bearing coating used in the main bearings and big-end bearings. Over time, this coating fatigues, particularly under high-load conditions, leading to bearing material flaking away. This creates a chain reaction: the bearing clearance increases, oil pressure drops, and the crankshaft begins to move excessively. Eventually, the bearing can seize, or the crankshaft can fracture — often catastrophically. The issue is exacerbated by the engine's high torque output (up to 700 Nm) and the heavy-duty use typical of Land Rover vehicles, such as towing and off-roading.

Many specialists note that the 306DT is less tolerant of extended oil change intervals than other engines. A 5,000-mile oil change interval is often recommended for high-mileage or hard-used vehicles. For detailed failure data and common mileage patterns, see our 306DT failure rate data page.

What Makes Failure More or Less Likely

🛢️

Missed Oil Changes

The 306DT is sensitive to oil quality. Extended oil change intervals (beyond 5,000–6,000 miles) increase bearing wear significantly. Many specialists recommend 5,000-mile intervals for high-mileage or hard-used vehicles.

🚀

Heavy Towing & Off-Road Use

Frequent towing, off-roading, or track use generates higher loads on the crankshaft bearings, accelerating the fatigue process. Vehicles used primarily on motorways tend to have longer lifespans.

⚡

Engine Tuning & Power Upgrades

Remapping the engine to increase power and torque places significantly more stress on the crankshaft bearings. Many specialists advise against tuning a 306DT unless the engine is fully rebuilt with upgraded bearings.

The best protection is a strict oil change regime and avoiding heavy loads until the engine is fully warmed up. For a broader view of Land Rover engine reliability, see our Engine Failure Index 2026.

How a Specialist Confirms 306DT Crankshaft Bearing Failure

1

Oil Pressure Test

A specialist will connect a mechanical oil pressure gauge to the engine. Low oil pressure, especially at idle when hot, is a strong indicator of bearing wear.

2

Oil Analysis

An oil sample is sent to a laboratory for analysis. High levels of aluminium, tin, or copper indicate bearing material wear. This is a highly reliable diagnostic method.

3

Inspection of Oil Filter & Sump

The oil filter and sump are removed and inspected for metal particles. Bearing material appears as a metallic "glitter" in the oil. This is a definitive sign of failure.

4

Endoscope Inspection

In advanced cases, an endoscope can be used through the oil drain plug to inspect the crankshaft bearing surfaces. This is less common but can confirm the diagnosis without engine disassembly.

Prevention & Early Detection — What You Can Actually Do

The 306DT is a powerful engine, but it demands respect and maintenance. Here's what you can do to minimise the risk of crankshaft bearing failure.

  • Stick to a 5,000-mile oil change interval. This is the single most important thing you can do. Use high-quality 5W-30 or 5W-40 oil that meets the correct Land Rover specification.
  • Use genuine or high-quality oil filters. A poor-quality filter can allow debris to circulate, accelerating bearing wear.
  • Consider oil analysis. Sending an oil sample for analysis at every service can detect bearing wear early, long before symptoms appear.
  • Warm the engine before heavy load. Avoid hard acceleration, towing, or off-roading until the engine oil has reached operating temperature.
  • Listen for cold-start knock. If you hear a knock when starting from cold, have it checked immediately. Do not ignore it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the first signs of 306DT crankshaft bearing failure?

The earliest sign is a cold-start knock — a distinct knocking or rumbling noise from the engine when started from cold. This may fade as the engine warms up. Other signs include low oil pressure warnings, metallic debris in the oil, and engine knocking under load.

Can I repair a 306DT crankshaft bearing, or does it need full replacement?

If caught very early (Stage 1–2), it may be possible to replace the bearings without replacing the entire engine. However, this is a labour-intensive job that requires engine removal and disassembly. In most cases, a full engine replacement or rebuild is the more reliable option.

How much does a 306DT engine replacement cost in the UK in 2025?

For a reconditioned engine supply-only, expect £6,500–£9,500 [EM-QUOTE]. With labour (15–20 hours), the total cost at an independent specialist is typically £7,500–£10,800. Main dealer prices are significantly higher, often £10,000–£14,000.

Is it worth fixing a 306DT engine on an older Discovery 4?

It depends on the vehicle's value and overall condition. On a Discovery 4 worth £6,000–£10,000, a full engine replacement may approach or exceed 60% of the car's value. However, on a higher-value L405 or L494, the investment is often worthwhile. Always get a current valuation and a firm quote before deciding.

Is there a recall for 306DT crankshaft bearing failure?

Land Rover has not issued a widespread recall for this issue. However, some models have had recalls related to the engine management software or oil pump. Check with your dealer using your VIN to see if any recalls apply to your vehicle.

What is the typical mileage for 306DT crankshaft bearing failure?

Based on our data [PATTERN DATA], the typical mileage range is 80,000–140,000 miles. Early failures can occur below 60,000 miles, and some examples have exceeded 150,000 miles without issue — usually those with very strict oil change regimes.
