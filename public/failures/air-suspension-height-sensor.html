Air Suspension Height Sensor Faults — Symptoms, Cost & Repair Verdict

Height sensors are the eyes of your Land Rover's air suspension. When they fail, the system loses its reference, leading to uneven ride heights, warning lights, and a constantly running compressor. The Short Answer

Air suspension height sensors are vulnerable to corrosion, physical damage, and electrical failure, causing the system to lose track of ride height. This results in a lopsided vehicle, warning lights, and often a compressor that runs continuously to try to compensate. Replacing a faulty sensor is usually a straightforward fix costing a few hundred pounds – and it's almost always worth doing.

Symptom Progression — What Owners Notice, In Order

Height sensor failure often starts subtly and worsens over time. Here's what owners typically notice, in the order it appears.

  1. Stage 1 – Vehicle sits unevenly. One corner of the car may be lower than the others, especially after parking overnight. The system fails to level the vehicle.
  2. Stage 2 – Suspension warning light. The amber suspension fault warning appears on the dashboard, often accompanied by a message like "Suspension Fault" or "Air Suspension Inactive".
  3. Stage 3 – Compressor runs more often. The compressor cycles frequently or runs continuously in an attempt to adjust ride height, as the sensor data is erratic.
  4. Stage 4 – Harsh ride and poor handling. The vehicle may lean in corners, feel unstable, or ride harshly due to incorrect height settings.
  5. Stage 5 – Complete system failure. The system may enter a "limp" mode, locking the suspension at a fixed height, or the compressor may burn out due to overwork.

If you notice any of these signs, act quickly. A faulty sensor can damage the compressor and cause uneven tyre wear. For a broader view of suspension issues, see our suspension fault warning guide.

Root Cause — Why Height Sensors Fail

Air suspension height sensors are small potentiometers or Hall-effect sensors mounted on the suspension arms. They convert the arm's angular position into a voltage signal that the ECU uses to determine ride height. Failure occurs due to:

  • Corrosion and water ingress. The sensors are exposed to road spray, salt, and moisture. Over time, the internal contacts corrode, leading to erratic signals or open circuits.
  • Worn linkage or bushes. The plastic ball joints connecting the sensor to the suspension arm wear out, causing play and inaccurate readings.
  • Electrical faults. Damaged wiring, broken connectors, or a failing sensor supply voltage can cause the ECU to lose the signal.
  • Physical damage. Off‑roading or debris can knock the sensor or its linkage, causing it to break or jam.

The most common failure is internal corrosion of the sensor's track. Many specialists recommend replacing the sensor as a set (all four) if one fails, as the others are likely to fail soon. For a detailed breakdown of the air suspension system, see our air suspension compressor failure guide.

What Makes Failure More or Less Likely

🌧️

Damp Environment

UK roads are salted in winter, which accelerates corrosion. Sensors exposed to salt spray and moisture fail much sooner than those in drier climates.

🚵

Off‑Road Use

Mud, water, and stones can damage the sensor linkage or the sensor itself. Regular cleaning after off‑roading can prevent this.

🔧

Age and Mileage

Like most electronic components, height sensors wear out with time and use. Higher‑mileage vehicles are more likely to experience sensor failure.

How a Specialist Confirms Height Sensor Faults

1

Diagnostic Scan

A specialist will connect a diagnostic tool to read fault codes. Common codes include C1A13 (sensor signal out of range), C1A14 (sensor supply voltage), and specific corner codes like C1A15–C1A18. These pinpoint the faulty sensor.

2

Live Data Check

With the engine running, the technician will monitor the live height values from each sensor while moving the suspension. A stuck value or erratic reading indicates a faulty sensor.

3

Visual Inspection

The sensor and its linkage are inspected for physical damage, corrosion, or broken wires. The ball joints on the linkage are checked for play.

4

Resistance/Voltage Check

Using a multimeter, the sensor's resistance or output voltage can be measured and compared to specifications. A short or open circuit confirms a bad sensor.

Prevention & Early Detection — What You Can Actually Do

Height sensors are exposed and vulnerable, but you can extend their life with a few simple habits.

  • Inspect the sensors and linkages annually. Look for corrosion, frayed wires, and play in the ball joints. Replace any worn linkages before they cause sensor failure.
  • Keep the sensors clean. After off‑roading or during winter, rinse the suspension components with a hose to remove salt and mud.
  • Apply dielectric grease to connectors. This prevents moisture ingress and corrosion in the electrical plugs.
  • Check suspension warning lights promptly. Do not ignore a fault – a diagnostic scan can identify a bad sensor before it causes compressor damage.
  • Consider replacing all four sensors if one fails. The remaining sensors are often of similar age and may fail soon; replacing them as a set saves labour in the long run.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do air suspension height sensors last?

Typically 60,000–100,000 miles, depending on exposure to moisture and salt. In the UK, they often fail sooner due to the damp climate. Regular cleaning and inspection can extend their life.

Can a height sensor be repaired, or must it be replaced?

In most cases, replacement is the only reliable option. Internal corrosion cannot be repaired, and the sensor is a sealed unit. Aftermarket sensors are available at a fraction of the dealer cost.

What are the symptoms of a faulty height sensor?

Common symptoms include an uneven ride height, a suspension warning light, the compressor running constantly, and a harsh or unstable ride. Diagnostic trouble codes will pinpoint the affected corner.

How much does it cost to replace a height sensor in the UK in 2025?

At an independent specialist, expect £200–£500 [EM-QUOTE] for a reconditioned sensor including fitting. Main dealer prices are significantly higher, often £400–£800. Additional costs may apply if the linkage or wiring is also damaged.

Is it worth replacing a height sensor on an older Land Rover?

Yes – a new sensor is a relatively cheap fix that restores ride quality and prevents compressor damage. Even on older L322 models, the repair is usually worthwhile if the rest of the vehicle is in good condition.

Is there a recall for air suspension height sensor issues?

Land Rover has issued recalls for certain air suspension issues, but they are typically for software updates or wiring harness faults, not for the sensors themselves. Check with your dealer using your VIN to see if any recalls apply.
