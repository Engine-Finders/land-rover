The Short Answer

Infotainment software glitches in Land Rovers are usually caused by corrupted firmware, a failing display module, or a faulty control unit. Symptoms range from a slow, unresponsive screen to complete blackouts, intermittent reboots, and loss of features like navigation or phone connectivity. In most cases, a software update or a replacement of the control unit resolves the issue at a reasonable cost.

  • Typical mileage at first glitch: 20,000–60,000 miles [PATTERN DATA]
  • Software update (dealer or specialist): £150–£300 [EM-QUOTE]
  • Control unit replacement (independent): £400–£1,200 [EM-QUOTE]

Symptom Progression — What Owners Notice, In Order

Infotainment glitches often start subtly and worsen over time. Here's what owners typically notice, in the order it appears.

  1. Stage 1 – System lags or freezes. The screen responds slowly to touch inputs, or the system hangs when switching between functions like navigation, media, and phone.
  2. Stage 2 – Random reboots. The infotainment system restarts unexpectedly, sometimes multiple times during a single journey.
  3. Stage 3 – Loss of features. Navigation maps may fail to load, Bluetooth pairing drops out, or the climate controls become unresponsive.
  4. Stage 4 – Black screen. The display remains blank, but the system may still be alive (you can hear audio or feel feedback).
  5. Stage 5 – Complete system failure. The infotainment unit fails to boot at all, and the vehicle may throw unrelated warning codes due to CANbus communication errors.

If you notice any of these signs, act promptly. A software update often fixes the issue, but hardware failure requires replacement. For a broader view of electrical issues, see our infotainment software glitches symptom hub.

Root Cause — Why Infotainment Systems Glitch

Land Rover's InControl Touch Pro and Touch Pro Duo systems are complex, integrating navigation, audio, vehicle settings, and connectivity. Glitches can be caused by:

  • Corrupted firmware. Over‑the‑air updates (or failed dealer updates) can corrupt the system software, causing instability.
  • Display module failure. The touchscreen itself can develop dead zones, delamination, or internal shorts, leading to erratic behaviour.
  • Control unit failure. The main infotainment ECU (often called the 'head unit') can fail due to overheating, voltage spikes, or manufacturing defects.
  • CANbus communication errors. A fault on the vehicle's CAN network can cause the infotainment system to lose communication with other ECUs, leading to glitches and warning lights.
  • Battery or alternator issues. A weak battery or failing alternator can cause voltage drops that make the infotainment system behave erratically.

The most common cause is a software bug that can be resolved with a simple update. However, if the hardware has failed, replacement is usually the only option. For a detailed breakdown of electrical diagnostics, see our infotainment diagnostic technical reference.

What Makes Failure More or Less Likely

📱

Software Update History

Vehicles that haven't had regular dealer or OTA updates are more likely to suffer from glitches. Land Rover releases updates to fix bugs and improve stability.

🔋

Battery Health

A weak battery can cause voltage drops that corrupt the infotainment system's memory. This is especially common on vehicles that sit unused for long periods.

🌡️

Temperature Extremes

Heat and cold can cause the display module to fail. In the UK, cold, damp weather is a common trigger for touchscreen issues.

How a Specialist Confirms Infotainment Glitches

1

Diagnostic Scan

A specialist will connect a diagnostic tool to read fault codes from the infotainment system and the vehicle's CANbus. Common codes include U1A00 (lost communication), B1A00 (display module failure), and various software-related codes.

2

Software Version Check

The technician will check the current software version and compare it to the latest available. If an update is available, it may be installed as part of the diagnosis.

3

Display and Touch Test

The screen is tested for dead zones, unresponsive areas, or flickering. This helps distinguish between a software bug and a hardware failure.

4

Battery and Charging System Check

Battery voltage and alternator output are checked to ensure the infotainment system is receiving stable power.

Prevention & Early Detection — What You Can Actually Do

Infotainment glitches can often be prevented with a few simple habits.

  • Keep the software updated. Check with your dealer or use the Land Rover InControl app to ensure you have the latest firmware. Over‑the‑air updates often fix known bugs.
  • Maintain battery health. A weak battery can cause voltage drops that corrupt the system. If your vehicle sits for long periods, use a battery maintainer.
  • Restart the system periodically. A hard reset (holding the power button for 10 seconds) can clear temporary glitches and prevent them from escalating.
  • Avoid extreme temperatures. If possible, park in a garage or use a windscreen shade to protect the touchscreen from direct sunlight.
  • Address glitches promptly. If you notice lag or reboots, have the system checked before the issue worsens.

Prevention is cheaper than repair. For a full guide to infotainment maintenance, see our infotainment diagnostic technical reference.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the most common infotainment glitches in Land Rovers?

Common issues include a frozen touchscreen, random reboots, loss of navigation or Bluetooth, and a black screen. These are often caused by software bugs or a failing control unit.

Can a software update fix infotainment glitches?

Yes – many glitches are resolved by a simple firmware update. Land Rover releases updates regularly to fix bugs and improve stability. A dealer or specialist can perform the update.

How much does it cost to fix infotainment glitches in the UK in 2025?

A software update is typically £150–£300 [EM-QUOTE]. If the control unit needs replacing, expect £400–£1,200 [EM-QUOTE] at an independent specialist. Main dealer prices are significantly higher.

Is it worth repairing infotainment glitches on an older Land Rover?

It depends. If the issue is software, a cheap update is worthwhile. If the hardware is failing, the cost may approach 20% of the vehicle's value. On most older models, a replacement control unit is still cost-effective.

Can I reset the infotainment system myself?

Yes – a hard reset (holding the power button for 10 seconds) can clear temporary glitches. If the issue persists, a software update or component replacement is needed.

Is there a recall for infotainment software glitches?

Land Rover has issued recalls for certain infotainment issues, usually for software updates or wiring harness faults. Check with your dealer using your VIN to see if any recalls apply.
