The Short Answer

The 204DTD (2.0 Ingenium Diesel) and 224DT (2.2 Duratorq) engines are prone to timing chain tensioner wear, particularly in pre-2019 models. This typically occurs between 60,000 and 120,000 miles and, if left unchecked, can cause the timing chain to jump or break, resulting in bent valves and a destroyed engine. Early warning signs include a cold‑start rattle and a check engine light.

Symptom Progression — What Owners Notice, In Order

Timing chain tensioner wear doesn't happen overnight. It gives progressive warnings that, if caught early, can save your engine. Here's what owners typically notice, in the order it appears over weeks or months.

  1. Stage 1 – Cold‑start rattle. A distinct rattling or clattering noise from the front of the engine when started from cold, which often fades as the engine warms up. This is the single most important early warning sign.
  2. Stage 2 – Check engine light. The engine management light may illuminate, often with fault codes related to camshaft/crankshaft correlation (e.g., P0016, P0017).
  3. Stage 3 – Hesitation or poor performance. The engine may feel down on power, hesitate under acceleration, or exhibit rough idling as the timing becomes increasingly inaccurate.
  4. Stage 4 – Timing chain skip. The chain jumps one or more teeth, leading to a more persistent rattle, loss of power, and potential valve/piston contact.
  5. Stage 5 – Catastrophic failure. The chain breaks or the tensioner fails completely, causing severe engine damage — bent valves, damaged pistons, and a destroyed cylinder head. At this point, a full engine replacement is almost always required.

If you experience any of these symptoms, do not continue driving. A simple diagnostic scan and a visual inspection of the timing chain can often confirm the issue before it becomes catastrophic. For a broader view of Land Rover engine noises, see our engine knocking noise guide.

Root Cause — Why Timing Chain Tensioners Fail in 204DTD & 224DT

The 204DTD (2.0 Ingenium Diesel) and 224DT (2.2 Duratorq) engines share a similar timing chain design that relies on a hydraulic tensioner to keep the chain tight. In pre-2019 models, the tensioner design was prone to premature wear, often due to a combination of factors:

  • Oil pressure issues. The tensioner relies on oil pressure to maintain tension. If oil changes are neglected, sludge and debris can clog the oil feed, reducing pressure and accelerating wear.
  • Tensioner material fatigue. The tensioner's internal spring and ratchet mechanism can weaken over time, especially on engines that are frequently cold-started or driven short distances.
  • Chain stretch. Over time, the timing chain itself stretches, placing additional load on the tensioner and causing it to extend beyond its operating range.
  • DPF regeneration cycles. On the 204DTD, frequent DPF regenerations can lead to oil dilution, which reduces the oil's lubricity and accelerates wear on the tensioner and chain guides.

Many specialists recommend replacing the timing chain and tensioner as a preventative measure at around 70,000–80,000 miles, especially on vehicles used primarily for short urban trips. What Makes Failure More or Less Likely

🛢️

Missed Oil Changes

Both engines are sensitive to oil quality and sludge build-up. Extended intervals beyond 6,000–8,000 miles increase the risk of tensioner failure. Many specialists recommend 5,000‑mile oil changes for high‑mileage vehicles.

🚀

Short Urban Trips

Frequent cold starts and short journeys prevent the engine from reaching full operating temperature, leading to increased oil contamination from DPF regeneration on the 204DTD and accelerated tensioner wear.

⚡

Engine Tuning

Remapping the engine to increase power and torque puts additional stress on the timing chain and tensioner, significantly increasing the risk of failure. Many specialists advise against tuning unless the chain and tensioner have been upgraded.

How a Specialist Confirms Timing Chain Tensioner Wear

1

Diagnostic Scan

A specialist will connect a diagnostic tool to read fault codes. Codes such as P0016 (crankshaft/camshaft correlation) or P0340/0341 (camshaft position sensor) are strong indicators of timing chain issues.

2

Visual Inspection

The timing chain cover may be removed to inspect the tensioner and chain for wear. Excessive slack or visible wear on the tensioner ratchet confirms the problem.

3

Oil Pressure Test

A mechanical oil pressure gauge is connected to check if oil pressure is within specification. Low pressure at idle can indicate a blocked oil feed to the tensioner.

4

Oil Analysis

An oil sample can reveal elevated levels of wear metals (iron, aluminium) that indicate chain guide wear. This is a less invasive but reliable diagnostic step.

Prevention & Early Detection — What You Can Actually Do

The 204DTD and 224DT are reliable engines when properly maintained. Here's what you can do to minimise the risk of timing chain tensioner failure.

  • Stick to a 5,000-mile oil change interval. Use high-quality 5W-30 oil that meets the correct Land Rover specification (WSS-M2C913-D or later). This is the single most important preventative measure.
  • Use genuine or high-quality oil filters. A poor-quality filter can restrict oil flow to the tensioner, accelerating wear.
  • Consider a preventative timing chain replacement. Many specialists recommend replacing the chain and tensioner at 70,000–80,000 miles on pre-2019 vehicles, especially if you plan to keep the car long-term.
  • Warm the engine before heavy load. Avoid hard acceleration, towing, or high-revving until the engine has reached operating temperature.
  • Listen for cold‑start rattle. If you hear a rattle when starting from cold, have it checked immediately. Do not ignore it — early detection can save you thousands.

Prevention is always cheaper than repair. For a more detailed discussion of repair-vs-replace economics, see our Repair vs Replace vs Scrap Framework.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the first signs of timing chain tensioner wear on a 204DTD or 224DT?

The earliest sign is a cold‑start rattle from the front of the engine — a metallic clatter that fades as the engine warms up. Other signs include a check engine light, hesitation, and reduced performance.

Can I repair the timing chain tensioner, or does it need full replacement?

If caught very early (Stage 1–2), it may be possible to replace just the chain, tensioner, and guides without replacing the entire engine. However, this is a labour-intensive job that requires removing the front cover. In most cases, a full engine replacement or rebuild is the more reliable option once damage has occurred.

How much does a 204DTD engine replacement cost in the UK in 2025?

For a reconditioned 204DTD engine supply-only, expect £4,500–£6,500 [EM-QUOTE]. With labour (10–14 hours), the total cost at an independent specialist is typically £5,200–£7,400. Main dealer prices are significantly higher, often £7,000–£10,000.

Is it worth fixing a 204DTD engine on an older Discovery Sport?

It depends on the vehicle's value and overall condition. On a Discovery Sport worth £8,000–£14,000, a full engine replacement may be borderline but often worthwhile if the rest of the car is in good shape. Always get a current valuation and a firm quote before deciding.

Is there a recall for 204DTD or 224DT timing chain issues?

Land Rover has not issued a widespread recall for this issue. However, some models have had recalls related to the engine management software or timing chain tensioner revisions. Check with your dealer using your VIN to see if any recalls apply to your vehicle.

What is the typical mileage for timing chain tensioner failure on these engines?

Based on our data [PATTERN DATA], the typical mileage range is 60,000–120,000 miles. Early failures can occur below 50,000 miles, especially with poor maintenance, and some examples have exceeded 150,000 miles without issue — usually those with very strict oil change regimes.
