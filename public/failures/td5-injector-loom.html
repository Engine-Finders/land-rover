TD5 Injector Harness Oil Migration — Symptoms, Cost & Diagnosis

The TD5 2.5-litre five-cylinder diesel is one of Land Rover's most legendary engines — but a well-known flaw in the injector harness can cause oil to migrate to the ECU, leading to rough running, misfires, and even engine shutdown. The Short Answer

The TD5 injector harness oil migration is a well‑documented issue that typically occurs between 80,000 and 150,000 miles. Oil travels through the wiring harness from the injector loom into the ECU, causing misfires, rough running, and eventually engine shutdown. Early warning signs include a misfire on cylinder 5, a check engine light, and intermittent stalling.

Symptom Progression — What Owners Notice, In Order

Injector harness oil migration on the TD5 doesn't happen overnight. It gives progressive warnings that, if caught early, can save your ECU from expensive damage. Here's what owners typically notice, in the order it appears over weeks or months.

  1. Stage 1 – Intermittent misfire. The engine feels lumpy or hesitates, particularly on cylinder 5 (the one nearest the bulkhead). This is often the first sign of oil contamination in the harness.
  2. Stage 2 – Check engine light. The engine management light may illuminate, often with fault codes related to injector function (e.g., injector open circuit or short circuit).
  3. Stage 3 – Rough running and hunting. The engine may idle unevenly, hunt between revs, or stall unexpectedly, especially when hot or under load.
  4. Stage 4 – Hard starting. The engine becomes difficult to start, particularly when warm, as the contaminated injector signals disrupt the ECU's fuel timing.
  5. Stage 5 – Complete ECU failure. Oil reaches the ECU connector pins, causing short circuits and potentially destroying the ECU. At this point, the engine may refuse to start entirely, requiring ECU repair or replacement.

If you experience any of these symptoms, do not ignore them. A simple inspection of the injector harness connector and ECU plug can often confirm the issue before it becomes expensive. For a broader view of Land Rover engine running issues, see our won't start guide.

Root Cause — Why TD5 Injector Harnesses Suffer Oil Migration

The TD5 is a 2.5-litre five-cylinder diesel engine produced by Land Rover from 1999 to 2006. It is one of the most robust and reliable engines Land Rover ever made — but it has one well‑documented weakness: the injector wiring harness.

The injector harness runs inside the cylinder head, directly above the injectors. Over time, oil from the valve train can seep past the injector seals and into the harness. The oil then travels through the wiring harness by capillary action, eventually reaching the ECU connector plug. This oil contamination causes short circuits, erratic injector signals, and — in severe cases — ECU failure.

The issue is often exacerbated by:

  • High mileage. As the engine ages, the injector seals harden and become less effective at keeping oil out of the harness.
  • Oil quality. Using the wrong oil or extending oil change intervals can accelerate seal degradation.
  • Short trips. Frequent cold starts and short journeys prevent the engine from reaching full operating temperature, which can accelerate seal wear.

How a Specialist Confirms TD5 Injector Harness Oil Migration

1

Visual Inspection of ECU Connector

A specialist will remove the ECU connector and inspect the pins for oil contamination. If oil is visible on the connector pins, the harness has migrated oil to the ECU.

2

Diagnostic Scan

A diagnostic tool can read fault codes from the ECU. Common codes include injector open circuit or short circuit, which are strong indicators of oil contamination.

3

Injector Harness Inspection

The injector harness is removed and inspected for oil ingress. The presence of oil in the harness confirms the issue and indicates that replacement is required.

4

ECU Testing

If oil has reached the ECU, the unit may need to be tested on a dedicated bench to check for internal damage. In many cases, the ECU can be repaired by cleaning and replacing damaged components.

Prevention & Early Detection — What You Can Actually Do

The TD5 is a legendary engine, but it needs proper maintenance to avoid injector harness oil migration. Here's what you can do to minimise the risk.

  • Stick to a 5,000-mile oil change interval. Use high-quality 5W-30 or 5W-40 oil that meets the correct Land Rover specification. This is the single most important preventative measure.
  • Use genuine or high-quality injector seals. When replacing injectors or doing a cylinder head service, always use genuine or high-quality seals to prevent oil ingress.
  • Consider a preventative injector harness replacement. Many specialists recommend replacing the harness at 100,000 miles as a preventative measure on high-mileage vehicles.
  • Inspect the ECU connector periodically. If you're doing a service, ask your mechanic to inspect the ECU connector for signs of oil contamination. Early detection can save you thousands.
  • Listen for misfires. If you notice a lumpy idle or hesitation, have the injector harness checked immediately.

Prevention is always cheaper than repair. For a more detailed discussion of repair-vs-replace economics, see our Repair vs Replace vs Scrap Framework.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the first signs of injector harness oil migration on a TD5?

The earliest sign is a misfire, particularly on cylinder 5 (the one nearest the bulkhead). You may also notice a check engine light, rough idling, or the engine stalling unexpectedly.

Can I repair the injector harness, or does it need full replacement?

If caught early, the injector harness can be replaced without needing to replace the ECU. However, if oil has already reached the ECU, the unit may need to be cleaned or repaired. In most cases, replacing the harness and cleaning the ECU is sufficient.

How much does a TD5 injector harness replacement cost in the UK in 2025?

For a supply‑and‑fit of a new injector harness, expect £150–£300 at an independent specialist [EM-QUOTE]. If the ECU needs cleaning or repair, the total cost can rise to £400–£1,100.

Is it worth fixing a TD5 injector harness issue on an older Defender?

Yes. The TD5 is a robust engine, and the repair is relatively inexpensive. On a high‑value Defender, the repair cost is a small fraction of the vehicle's value. Ignoring the issue can lead to ECU failure and a much more expensive repair.

Is there a recall for TD5 injector harness oil migration?

Land Rover has not issued a widespread recall for this issue. However, some models have had recalls related to the injector harness or ECU software updates. Check with your dealer using your VIN to see if any recalls apply to your vehicle.

What is the typical mileage for injector harness oil migration on the TD5?

Based on our data [PATTERN DATA], the typical mileage range is 80,000–150,000 miles. Early failures can occur below 60,000 miles, especially with poor maintenance, and some examples have exceeded 200,000 miles without issues — usually those with very strict oil change regimes.
