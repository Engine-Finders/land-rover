P0011 Land Rover — Camshaft Position Timing Over-Advanced

This code means the ECU has detected that the intake camshaft is advanced beyond the commanded position – often a sign of a failing variable valve timing (VVT) actuator, a stretched timing chain, or low oil pressure. We rank the causes cheapest-first so you can avoid an expensive misdiagnosis.

P0011 means the intake camshaft timing is more advanced than the ECU expects. On Land Rover engines – especially the 204DTD Ingenium and 306DT V6 – this is most often caused by a sticking VVT solenoid, low oil pressure, or a stretched timing chain.

  • Most common and cheapest fix: a faulty VVT solenoid or oil control valve – typically £100–£250 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: timing chain stretch or tensioner failure, costing £1,200–£2,500 [PATTERN DATA]
  • Often appears alongside P0016 – if both are present, the chain is the prime suspect
  • Accompanied by engine management light, rough idle, and limp mode

Do not replace the timing chain before ruling out the solenoid and oil pressure. Follow the cheapest-first diagnostic order below.

What P0011 Actually Means

In a variable valve timing (VVT) system, the ECU adjusts the position of the intake camshaft relative to the crankshaft to optimise power, fuel economy, and emissions. It does this by commanding the oil control valve (OCV) to direct pressurised oil to the VVT actuator (phaser) mounted on the camshaft. P0011 is set when the actual camshaft position, as reported by the camshaft position sensor, is more advanced than the commanded position by more than a few degrees for a set period. This can happen because the actuator is stuck in the advanced position, the OCV is stuck open, oil pressure is too low to retract the phaser, or the timing chain has stretched, causing a permanent offset. On Land Rover diesels, the 204DTD and 306DT engines are the most common culprits – both have well-documented issues with timing chain tensioners and VVT components.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated – usually amber
  • Rough idle or misfire, especially when cold or under load
  • Limp mode or noticeable loss of power – the ECU limits performance to protect the engine
  • Hard starting or extended cranking – over-advanced timing can make starting difficult
  • Increased fuel consumption – the engine is not running at its most efficient timing
  • In severe cases, a rattling noise from the timing chain area on cold start (a classic symptom of chain stretch or tensioner wear)

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check oil level and pressure

Low oil or poor pressure can cause the VVT system to malfunction. Topping up or an oil change may clear the code.

2

Inspect and clean the oil control valve (OCV)

Remove the solenoid, check the screen for debris, clean or replace. Very often resolves the issue.

3

Test the OCV electrical circuit

Check wiring, connector, and resistance – a failed solenoid or wiring fault is common.

4

Live‑data the camshaft position

Compare commanded vs actual cam timing. If the phaser is stuck advanced, the actuator is the issue.

5

Only then consider timing chain and tensioner

If all the above check out and the code persists, a stretched chain or worn tensioner is the likely cause.

Can You Still Drive It?

Short distances only, and with caution. P0011 indicates a timing control problem that can lead to engine damage if ignored. If the phaser is stuck advanced, the engine may run poorly, but more critically, if the cause is a stretched chain, the chain could jump teeth and cause catastrophic piston‑valve contact. If you hear a rattle on cold start or the engine is misfiring badly, stop driving immediately and call for recovery. Otherwise, drive gently to a specialist and get it looked at within a day or two – the risk of a much larger bill is real.

Related Fault Codes

P0011 is a timing code, and it rarely travels alone. Which codes accompany it tells you a lot.

P0016 – Crankshaft / Camshaft Position Correlation

If both P0011 and P0016 appear, the timing chain is almost certainly stretched or the tensioner has failed – the physical timing is off.

P0012 – Camshaft Position Timing Over‑Retarded (Bank 1)

The mirror code – if you see both P0011 and P0012, the VVT system is likely erratic, pointing to oil pressure or solenoid issues.

P0087 – Fuel Rail Pressure Too Low

May co‑occur if the engine has entered limp mode due to timing issues – see our timing chain diagnosis guide.

P0300 – Random/Multiple Cylinder Misfire

Often accompanies P0011 when the over‑advanced timing causes poor combustion – can damage the catalytic converter if ignored.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0011 on a Land Rover?

For a very short journey to a garage, maybe – but if you hear a rattle or the engine is misfiring, stop immediately. Over-advanced timing can cause detonation and engine damage, and a stretched chain can jump teeth. Get it diagnosed as soon as possible.

Does P0011 always mean the timing chain has stretched?

No – in many cases it's a faulty VVT solenoid or low oil pressure. The diagnostic order above is designed to rule out the cheap fixes first. Only consider chain work after the simpler causes are eliminated.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU re-detects the timing error. Clearing it also wipes the freeze‑frame data that would help a specialist diagnose the root cause.

How much should P0011 diagnosis cost?

Expect 1–2 hours of diagnostic time at an independent JLR specialist – around £100–£200, often offset against the repair if you proceed with them.

What's the most common fix for P0011 on a Discovery Sport or Evoque?

For the 204DTD Ingenium engine, replacing the oil control valve (VVT solenoid) is the most common and cheapest fix – it resolves the code in over 50% of cases [PATTERN DATA]. Always check oil level first, though.
