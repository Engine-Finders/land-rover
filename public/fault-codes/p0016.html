P0016 means the engine control unit (ECU) has detected that the crankshaft and camshaft are not synchronised as expected – the timing is off. On Land Rover diesel engines, this often points to a stretched timing chain or a failing sensor, but it can also indicate a worn timing tensioner or a faulty variable valve timing (VVT) actuator.

  • Most common (and cheapest) fix: a faulty camshaft or crankshaft position sensor – typically £100–£250 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: timing chain replacement on engines like the 204DTD Ingenium or 306DT V6, costing £1,200–£2,500 [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly accompanied by engine management light, limp mode, and engine knocking noise
  • Frequently appears with P0011 – the combination strongly suggests a timing chain or tensioner issue

Do not ignore this code – a stretched chain can jump teeth and cause catastrophic engine damage. Follow the cheapest-first diagnostic order below.

What P0016 Actually Means

In modern engine management, the crankshaft position sensor and camshaft position sensors work together to tell the ECU exactly where each piston is and which stroke it's on. The ECU uses this information to control fuel injection and ignition timing. P0016 is set when the signal from the crankshaft sensor and the signal from the camshaft sensor (bank 1, intake cam) disagree by more than a few degrees for a set duration. This mismatch can be caused by a mechanical timing shift (e.g., a stretched timing chain or a worn tensioner), an electrical fault (a failing sensor or wiring), or a problem with the variable valve timing (VVT) system. On Land Rover engines, the most notorious culprit is timing chain stretch – particularly on early 204DTD Ingenium units and the 306DT V6 family, where chain tensioner wear is a well-known issue.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated – often amber, but may flash if misfires occur
  • Rough idle or misfire, especially when cold
  • Noticeable loss of power or limp mode – acceleration feels flat
  • Hard starting, extended cranking, or cranks but won't start
  • Engine knocking or rattling – often most audible on cold start (classic timing chain symptom)
  • Increased fuel consumption due to sub‑optimal timing

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Scan live data – compare crank and cam signals

A specialist will read the position sensor values while cranking. If one sensor reads implausibly, it's likely faulty.

2

Inspect sensor wiring and connectors

Check for damage, corrosion, or loose pins – a common and cheap fix.

3

Swap sensors (if identical) to test

If the code moves to the other bank or changes, the sensor is the culprit.

4

Check timing chain tension and stretch

Measure actual cam/crank correlation with a timing tool. A stretched chain is a mechanical reality that requires replacement.

5

Inspect VVT actuator and oil pressure

If everything else checks out, the phaser may be sticking – often due to low oil pressure or sludge.

Can You Still Drive It?

Not for long – and not without risk. A P0016 code indicates the engine's timing is out of sync. If the chain is stretched or a tensioner has failed, the chain can jump a tooth, leading to piston‑valve contact – a catastrophic failure that typically writes off the engine. Even if the cause is a sensor, ignoring the code means the ECU may run the engine in a degraded mode, increasing fuel consumption and potentially damaging the catalytic converter or DPF. If you hear a loud rattle on cold start, stop driving immediately. Otherwise, drive gently to a specialist and have it diagnosed within a day or two – the risk of a much larger bill is real.

Related Fault Codes

P0016 is often accompanied by other timing-related codes. The combination can pinpoint the exact problem.

P0011 – "A" Camshaft Position Timing Over-Advanced

If both P0011 and P0016 appear, the timing chain is almost certainly stretched or the VVT system is malfunctioning.

P0017 – Crankshaft / Camshaft Position Correlation Bank 1 Sensor B

The mirror code for the exhaust cam – rarely seen together with P0016, but if both appear, suspect a sensor or wiring issue.

P0087 – Fuel Rail Pressure Too Low

May co‑occur if the engine has entered limp mode due to timing issues – see timing chain diagnosis guide.

P0300 – Random/Multiple Cylinder Misfire

Often accompanies P0016 when timing is off enough to cause misfires. This can quickly damage the catalytic converter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0016 on a Land Rover?

For a very short journey to a garage, you might get away with it – but if you hear a rattle or the engine is misfiring, stop immediately. Driving with a stretched chain risks a timing jump and a destroyed engine. Get it diagnosed as soon as possible.

Does P0016 always mean the timing chain has stretched?

No – a faulty sensor or wiring issue is actually more common. The diagnostic order above is designed to rule out the cheaper causes before condemning the chain. However, on high‑mileage Land Rover diesels, chain stretch is a very real possibility.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU re-detects the correlation error. Clearing it also wipes the freeze‑frame data that would help a specialist diagnose the root cause.

How much should P0016 diagnosis cost?

Expect 1–2 hours of diagnostic time at an independent JLR specialist – typically around £100–£200, often offset against the repair if you proceed with them.

What’s the most common fix for P0016 on a Discovery Sport or Evoque?

For the 204DTD Ingenium engine, replacing the camshaft position sensor is the most common and cheapest fix – it resolves the code in over 40% of cases [PATTERN DATA]. If that doesn't help, timing chain replacement is the next step.
