What P0303 Actually Means

The engine control unit (ECU) monitors crankshaft speed fluctuations to detect misfires. P0303 is logged when Cylinder 3 fails to produce sufficient power during its firing event — either completely (a dead misfire) or intermittently. Crucially, the code identifies the symptom, not the cause. The misfire could be caused by a faulty spark plug, a failing ignition coil (on petrol engines), a faulty fuel injector (on both petrol and diesel), a vacuum leak, low compression, or even a timing issue. The diagnostic approach differs between petrol and diesel engines: on petrol engines, the ignition system is the primary suspect; on diesels, the fuel injector and compression are the main focus. This code appears across the full range of JLR engines — from the 204PT Ingenium petrol to the 204DTD Ingenium diesel and the 306DT V6 — and the diagnostic approach varies by fuel type.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated (amber) — if flashing, the misfire is severe
  • Noticeable rough idle or engine vibration, especially when cold
  • Engine hesitation or power loss under acceleration
  • Poor fuel economy — the ECU enriches the mixture to compensate
  • On petrols, you may notice a strong fuel smell from the exhaust (unburned fuel)
  • On diesels, you may notice black smoke from the exhaust
  • In severe cases, the engine may enter limp mode

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check for basic issues

Inspect the spark plug (petrol) or glow plug (diesel) on Cylinder 3. Check the ignition coil connector (petrol) and injector connector (both). Look for corrosion or damage.

2

Swap components between cylinders

On petrol: swap the ignition coil from Cylinder 3 to another cylinder. If the misfire moves, the coil is faulty. On diesel: swap the injector from Cylinder 3 to another cylinder — if the misfire moves, the injector is faulty.

3

Live-data the misfire counters

A specialist can monitor the misfire counters for each cylinder. If Cylinder 3 shows a high count, the fault is confirmed. If multiple cylinders show misfires, suspect a fuel or vacuum issue.

4

Check fuel pressure and injector wiring

If the injector swap doesn't move the fault, check the wiring to the Cylinder 3 injector. Check fuel rail pressure to ensure it's within spec.

5

Perform a compression test

If all electrical and fuel checks are clear, perform a compression test on Cylinder 3. Compare to other cylinders. Low compression indicates a mechanical issue.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes — but not for long. A single-cylinder misfire is not an immediate engine-destroying fault, but prolonged driving can cause serious damage. On petrol engines, unburned fuel can overheat and destroy the catalytic converter. On diesels, misfiring can cause DPF blockages. If the engine management light is flashing, the misfire is severe enough to damage the catalytic converter — stop driving immediately. If the light is steady, you can drive a short distance to a garage, but avoid heavy acceleration and get it checked within days.

Related Fault Codes

P0303 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P0300 — Random/Multiple Cylinder Misfire

If both P0303 and P0300 appear, suspect a fuel system, vacuum leak, or timing issue affecting all cylinders.

P0301 — Cylinder 1 Misfire

If multiple cylinder misfire codes appear, suspect a fuel or vacuum issue.

P0302 — Cylinder 2 Misfire

Multiple misfire codes often point to a fuel supply or ignition system issue.

P0304 — Cylinder 4 Misfire

If all four cylinders have misfire codes, suspect a fuel pressure, vacuum leak, or timing issue.

P0263 — Cylinder 1 Contribution/Balance Fault

Often appears alongside P0303 if the injector is failing — injector swap test is the key diagnostic step.

P0200 — Injector Circuit Malfunction

If present, the issue is likely electrical — check wiring and connectors.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0303 on a Land Rover?

Yes, for short journeys to get it diagnosed — but not for extended periods. If the check engine light is flashing, stop driving immediately to avoid catalytic converter damage. If the light is steady, drive gently and get it checked within days.

Does P0303 always mean the injector has failed?

No. On petrol engines, the spark plug or ignition coil is the most common cause. On diesels, the injector is the primary suspect, but wiring and compression issues are also possible. The component swap test is the quickest way to confirm.

Can a vacuum leak cause P0303?

Yes, a vacuum leak can cause a lean condition that leads to misfires. However, it usually affects all cylinders and would likely trigger P0300 as well, not just a single-cylinder misfire.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one to two hours of diagnostic time at an independent JLR specialist — around £80–£160. Many will offset this against the repair if you proceed.

Can low fuel cause P0303?

Yes, if the fuel level is very low, the fuel pump may suck air, causing misfires. However, this would usually affect all cylinders. Top up the fuel and clear the code to see if it returns.

What is the difference between P0303 and P0300?

P0300 is a random or multiple-cylinder misfire, meaning the ECU detected misfires on more than one cylinder. P0303 is a specific cylinder misfire (Cylinder 3). P0303 is easier to diagnose because you know exactly which cylinder is affected.
