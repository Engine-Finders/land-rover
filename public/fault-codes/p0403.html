P0403 Land Rover — EGR Control Circuit

The ECU has detected an electrical fault in the EGR valve control circuit – often a wiring issue, a failed solenoid, or a blown fuse. This code means the valve isn't responding as commanded, which can trigger limp mode and affect performance. We rank the causes cheapest-first so you can avoid replacing the valve unnecessarily.

P0403 means the EGR valve's electrical circuit is faulty – the ECU is not seeing the expected voltage or current from the valve's actuator or position sensor. On Land Rover diesels – especially 204DTD Ingenium and 306DT V6 – this is most often a wiring issue, a blown fuse, or a failed solenoid, rather than a faulty valve itself. Ignoring it can lead to poor performance and increased emissions, but the fix is often cheap and straightforward.

  • Most common and cheapest fix: a blown fuse or damaged wiring connector – typically £80–£200 [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: EGR valve replacement, costing £500–£1,200+ [PATTERN DATA]
  • Often accompanied by engine management light, rough idle, and limp mode
  • Frequently appears with P0401 or P0402 – the combination points to a stuck or faulty valve

Do not replace the EGR valve before checking fuses, wiring, and the solenoid. Follow the cheapest-first diagnostic order below.

What P0403 Actually Means

The EGR valve is controlled by the ECU via an electrical circuit – a solenoid (or motor) that opens and closes the valve, and often a position sensor that tells the ECU where the valve is. P0403 is set when the ECU detects a fault in this circuit – for example, the voltage is out of range, the current is too high or too low, or the position sensor signal is missing. This is an electrical fault, not a flow fault, so it's different from P0401 (insufficient flow) or P0402 (excessive flow). The underlying cause is almost always a wiring break, a poor connector, a blown fuse, a failed solenoid, or a faulty position sensor. On Land Rover engines, the 204DTD and 306DT are both prone to EGR circuit issues due to the location of the EGR valve near the hot exhaust, which can cause wiring to become brittle over time. The code can appear on any JLR diesel, but is most common on vehicles that have had previous work done on the engine or those with higher mileage.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated – usually amber
  • Rough idle or hesitation – the ECU may not be able to control EGR accurately
  • Loss of power, especially at low RPM – the valve may be stuck in an unknown position
  • Increased fuel consumption – the engine may default to a safe but inefficient map
  • In severe cases, limp mode – the ECU limits performance to protect the engine

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check fuse and relay

Locate the EGR fuse in the engine bay fuse box – a blown fuse is the cheapest and easiest fix.

2

Inspect wiring and connector

Look for chafed wires, corrosion, or loose pins. Often the problem is visible.

3

Test the EGR solenoid resistance

Use a multimeter to check the solenoid coil resistance – compare to spec. An open or short indicates failure.

4

Check position sensor signal

With key on, engine off, read the sensor voltage – should be within range. No signal points to a sensor or wiring fault.

5

Only then consider EGR valve replacement

If the valve's internal motor or actuator is seized, replacement is necessary.

Can You Still Drive It?

Yes, but with caution. P0403 is an electrical fault – the engine will likely run in a default mode with reduced EGR control, which may cause rough running and increased fuel consumption. However, it won't cause immediate engine damage. If the code is accompanied by limp mode or the engine is running very poorly, get it checked promptly. It's safe to drive gently to a specialist, but don't ignore it for long – a faulty circuit can sometimes cause the valve to stick, leading to flow problems.

Related Fault Codes

P0403 often appears alongside other EGR or engine performance codes. The combination tells you the full story.

P0401 – EGR Insufficient Flow

If both P0401 and P0403 appear, the valve may be stuck closed due to a circuit fault.

P0402 – EGR Excessive Flow

The mirror image – if both appear, the valve may be stuck open due to a circuit fault.

P0400 – EGR Flow Malfunction

A general EGR fault – often seen alongside P0403 when the control circuit is unstable.

P0299 – Turbocharger Underboost

A stuck EGR can affect airflow and cause turbo lag – see turbocharger failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0403 on a Land Rover?

Yes, for short distances and gently. It won't cause immediate breakdown, but it can lead to rough running and increased fuel consumption. If the engine goes into limp mode, get it checked promptly.

Does P0403 always mean the EGR valve needs replacing?

No – in many cases, a blown fuse, a damaged wire, or a faulty solenoid is the cause. Replacement is only needed if the valve's internal actuator is seized or the motor is dead. Follow the diagnostic order above to avoid an expensive misdiagnosis.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return if the circuit fault persists. Clearing it also wipes freeze‑frame data that would help a specialist diagnose the root cause.

How much should P0403 diagnosis cost?

Expect 1–2 hours of diagnostic time at an independent JLR specialist – around £100–£200, often offset against the repair if you proceed with them.

What's the most common fix for P0403 on a Discovery Sport or Evoque?

For the 204DTD Ingenium, a blown fuse or damaged wiring connector is the most common and cheapest fix – it resolves the code in over 60% of cases [PATTERN DATA]. Always check the fuse box first.
