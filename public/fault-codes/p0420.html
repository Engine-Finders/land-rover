P0420 means the ECU has detected that the catalytic converter is not reducing emissions as effectively as it should. On most Land Rover diesels and petrols this is a sensor or exhaust leak issue long before it's a failed catalyst — and it should be diagnosed cheapest-first.

  • Most common real cause: a failing downstream O2 sensor or an exhaust leak — typically £150–£450 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: catalytic converter replacement, £800–£2,200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside engine management light and limp mode
  • Frequently appears with P0300 (misfire) or P0130 (O2 sensor circuit) — the combination narrows the diagnosis considerably

What P0420 Actually Means

In a modern petrol or diesel with a catalytic converter, the ECU uses two oxygen sensors — one before (upstream) and one after (downstream) the catalyst — to monitor how effectively the converter is storing and releasing oxygen. P0420 is logged when the downstream sensor's signal mirrors the upstream sensor's signal too closely, indicating that the catalyst is not doing its job. Crucially, the code describes a performance issue, not a component failure. The efficiency could be genuinely low due to a failing catalyst, or it could be misreported because of a faulty downstream O2 sensor, an exhaust leak, or even a misfire that has contaminated the catalyst. That distinction is the entire difference between a £150 sensor and a £2,000 catalyst replacement, and it's why the diagnostic order below matters more than the code itself. This code appears across the full range of JLR petrol engines — from the 204PT Ingenium to the AJ126 V6 and AJ133 V8 — and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, usually amber
  • Possible black smoke from exhaust or a strong sulphur/rotten egg smell
  • Noticeable power loss or limp mode, especially if accompanied by misfire codes
  • Poor fuel economy — the ECU may enrich the mixture to protect the catalyst
  • Occasional rough idle or misfire if the underlying cause is ignition or fuel-related

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check for exhaust leaks

Inspect the exhaust system from the manifold to the catalyst for cracks or failed gaskets. A leak before the catalyst introduces extra oxygen, skewing the sensor readings.

2

Live-data the O2 sensors

A specialist compares upstream vs downstream sensor voltages at warm idle and under load. If the downstream sensor mimics the upstream closely, the catalyst isn't storing oxygen — but if the sensor's response is slow, the sensor itself is the problem.

3

Check for misfire codes

Codes like P0300 or P0303 indicate a misfire that can damage the catalyst. Fix the misfire first; the P0420 may clear after a drive cycle.

4

Inspect fuel trims

Extreme fuel trim values point to a vacuum leak, fuel pressure issue, or MAF sensor problem that's causing a rich or lean condition — this can contaminate the catalyst.

5

Only then consider the catalyst

If steps 1–4 are clear and the downstream sensor still reads poorly, the catalytic converter itself is the likely answer.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes — short journeys to get it diagnosed. P0420 on its own is not an engine-destroying fault. However, if the underlying cause is a misfire or a fuel mixture issue, prolonged driving can overheat and permanently damage the catalytic converter — turning a £150 sensor into a £2,000 catalyst. Also, the vehicle may fail its MOT emissions test if the light is on. Get it looked at within a week, and avoid heavy acceleration or towing until the cause is confirmed.

Related Fault Codes

P0420 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P0300 — Random/Multiple Cylinder Misfire

If present, the misfire is likely the root cause — fix it first, then re-evaluate the catalyst efficiency.

P0303 — Cylinder 3 Misfire

Specific misfire codes point to ignition or fuel delivery issues on that cylinder.

P0130 — O2 Sensor Circuit (Bank 1 Sensor 1)

Points toward the upstream sensor, which affects fuel trims and can cause P0420 indirectly.

P0299 — Turbocharger Underboost

Often co-occurs with restricted performance; can also affect catalyst efficiency.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0420 on a Land Rover?

For short journeys to reach a garage, generally yes — but not for extended periods or under heavy load, as you risk damaging the catalytic converter. The vehicle will likely still pass a local drive but will fail an MOT emissions test.

Does P0420 always mean the catalytic converter has failed?

No. Across the five ranked causes, catalyst degradation is the least common. A faulty downstream O2 sensor or an exhaust leak are both far more likely and cost a fraction as much.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU sees the efficiency drop again, usually within a drive cycle or two. Clearing it also destroys the freeze-frame data a specialist would have used to narrow the cause.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one to two hours of diagnostic time at an independent JLR specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.
