P0562 Land Rover — System Voltage Low

One of the most overlooked codes on modern JLR vehicles, and one of the cheapest to fix. What it actually means, the causes ranked cheapest-first, and why ignoring it can lead to an expensive breakdown.

P0562 means the ECU is seeing sustained low system voltage — usually below 11.5 volts while the engine is running. On most Land Rover diesels this is a worn battery, a loose connection, or a failing alternator long before it's a serious electrical fault. It should be diagnosed cheapest-first.

  • Most common real cause: an ageing battery or poor earth connection — typically £80–£200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: alternator failure, £350–£650 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside battery warning light and limp mode
  • Frequently appears with P0563 or P0620 — the combination narrows the diagnosis considerably

What P0562 Actually Means

In modern common-rail diesels, the ECU continuously monitors system voltage to ensure fuel injectors, glow plugs, and other critical components operate correctly. P0562 is logged when the ECU detects sustained low voltage — typically below 11.5 volts — while the engine is running. Crucially, the code describes a measurement, not a diagnosis. The voltage could be genuinely low (a failing battery or alternator), or it could be the result of a poor earth connection causing an inaccurate reading. That distinction is the entire difference between an £80 fix and a £650 one, and it's why the diagnostic order below matters more than the code itself. This code appears across the full range of JLR vehicles, from the 204DTD Ingenium to the 306DT V6, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Battery warning light illuminated, usually red
  • Restricted performance message on the dash — JLR's specific wording
  • Noticeable power loss or limp mode, especially under acceleration or load
  • Hard starting, or extended cranking before the engine catches — see cranks but won't start
  • Occasional rough idle or misfire when voltage drops below 10.5 volts
  • Electrical glitches — flickering dash lights, slow wipers, or erratic infotainment behaviour

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check battery age and condition

If it's over 4–5 years old or showing signs of weakness, replace it. A failing battery triggers P0562 more often than anything else.

2

Inspect earth connections and terminals

Clean and tighten the main battery earth strap and the engine-to-chassis earth point. This alone resolves a meaningful share of cases.

3

Test battery voltage and cranking

Load-test the battery to confirm it can hold voltage under cranking load.

4

Check BMS (battery management system) data

If the battery is healthy, the sensor itself may be the issue.

5

Only then consider the alternator

If steps 1–4 are clear and voltage remains low with the engine running, the alternator becomes the likely answer.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes — short journeys to get it diagnosed. P0562 on its own is not an engine-destroying fault the way an oil pressure warning is. But if the voltage drops below 10.5 volts while driving, the ECU may shut down non-essential systems, and eventually the engine could stall. Prolonged running with low voltage can also shorten the life of the battery and alternator — turning a cheap fix into an expensive one. Get it looked at within days, not weeks.

Related Fault Codes

P0562 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P0563 — System Voltage High

The mirror image. If both are logged intermittently, suspect the regulator or sensor rather than the battery or alternator.

P0620 — Generator Control Circuit

Strong supporting evidence that the alternator control circuit is at fault.

P0700 — Transmission Control System Malfunction

Often co-occurs where low voltage has affected the gearbox module. See ZF 8-speed gearbox.

U0100 — Lost Communication with ECM

Voltage drops can cause communication faults between modules.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0562 on a Land Rover?

For short journeys to reach a garage, generally yes — but not for normal use, and not on motorways if the vehicle has entered limp mode. Prolonged running with low voltage can shorten battery and alternator life, and in severe cases the engine may stall.

Does P0562 always mean the alternator has failed?

No. Across the five ranked causes, alternator failure is the least common. An ageing battery or a poor earth connection are both far more likely and cost a fraction as much.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return once the ECU sees the voltage discrepancy again, usually within a drive cycle or two. Clearing it also destroys the freeze-frame data a specialist would have used to narrow the cause.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one to two hours of diagnostic time at an independent JLR specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.
