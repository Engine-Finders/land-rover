P164B Land Rover — Reductant Quality Performance

A code that appears when the AdBlue® (DEF) system detects that the fluid quality is below the required standard. Often caused by contaminated or degraded fluid, a faulty quality sensor, or software glitches. Left unchecked, it can lead to reduced power and eventually a no-start condition.

P164B means the ECU has detected that the reductant (AdBlue) quality is not meeting the required performance standard. On most Land Rover diesels this is a fluid quality issue or a sensor problem long before it's a major hardware failure — and it should be diagnosed in the correct order.

  • Most common real cause: degraded or contaminated AdBlue — typically £100–£250 to drain and refill [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: a failed reductant quality sensor or control module, £500–£1,200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside AdBlue warning light and limp mode
  • Frequently appears with P204F or P20E8 — the combination points to system performance rather than quality alone

Do not let anyone sell you a new SCR catalyst or tank before the fluid and sensor have been checked. Full diagnostic order below.

What P164B Actually Means

In modern diesel Land Rovers equipped with selective catalytic reduction (SCR), the AdBlue system injects a urea-based fluid into the exhaust to break down nitrogen oxides. The ECU constantly monitors the quality of the fluid using a sensor (often a sonic or conductivity-based sensor) inside the tank. P164B is set when the measured quality falls below a calibrated threshold — either because the fluid itself is poor (old, contaminated, or diluted) or because the sensor is reporting incorrectly. It's important to understand that the code describes a quality issue, not a quantity issue (that would be a different code). The distinction matters: a fluid change often fixes it, but a sensor fault requires replacement. This code appears across the full range of JLR diesel models, from the 204DTD Ingenium to the 306DT V6, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • AdBlue warning light illuminated, often amber
  • Restricted performance message on the dash — the most common symptom
  • Noticeable power loss or limp mode, especially when the system is under load
  • Reduced fuel economy as the ECU adjusts injection timing to compensate
  • In severe cases, a countdown timer may appear, limiting speed or preventing restart after a certain distance

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check AdBlue fluid quality and age

Verify the fluid is within its expiry date and has not been contaminated. A simple drain and refill with fresh, ISO-22241-compliant fluid often resolves the code.

2

Inspect sensor wiring and connector

Look for corrosion, broken wires, or loose connections at the reductant quality sensor — a common and cheap fix.

3

Perform sensor calibration / reset

Using diagnostic equipment, the sensor can be recalibrated or the learned values reset, which may clear the fault if it was a temporary glitch.

4

Replace the reductant quality sensor

If steps 1–3 don't fix it, the sensor is the next most likely culprit — relatively straightforward on most models.

5

Inspect tank and SCR system

Only after ruling out fluid and sensor, consider tank internal issues or SCR catalyst efficiency problems.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes — but for a limited time. P164B on its own is not an immediate engine-damaging fault, but the vehicle may enter a derated state (reduced power) and eventually a countdown timer will appear. Once the countdown reaches zero, the engine will not start again until the fault is repaired. For short journeys to a specialist, it is generally safe, but prolonged driving can lead to forced limp mode and possibly clog the SCR system if the quality issue is severe. Get it diagnosed as soon as possible.

Related Fault Codes

P164B rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P204F — Reductant System Performance

Indicates the entire SCR system is underperforming, not just quality. Often seen with P164B if the fluid is very poor.

P20E8 — Reductant Pressure Too Low

Suggests a supply issue (blocked lines or pump) rather than quality — a different branch of diagnosis.

P244A — Diesel Particulate Filter Differential Pressure Too Low

Can co-occur if the SCR and DPF systems share logic; not directly related but often flagged together.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P164B on a Land Rover?

For short journeys to a garage, generally yes — but not for extended periods. The vehicle may enter limp mode and eventually a countdown will prevent restarting. Get it sorted as soon as possible.

Does P164B always mean the AdBlue is bad?

Not always. While degraded fluid is the most common cause, a faulty quality sensor or wiring can trigger the same code even with good fluid. Diagnostic steps will differentiate.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return if the underlying quality or sensor issue persists. It may also mask the problem and prevent accurate diagnosis.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one to two hours of diagnostic time at an independent JLR specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.
