What P2002 Actually Means

The diesel particulate filter (DPF) is designed to trap soot particles from the exhaust. The ECU monitors its efficiency using differential pressure sensors (before and after the DPF) and calculates how much soot is being trapped. P2002 is set when the ECU determines that the DPF is not trapping enough soot – meaning its efficiency has dropped below a threshold, typically around 60-70%. This can happen because the DPF is physically damaged (cracked or melted), there is an exhaust leak affecting pressure readings, the pressure sensor is faulty, or the engine is producing excessive soot due to injector or combustion issues. On Land Rover engines, the 204DTD and 306DT are both prone to DPF-related codes, and P2002 is often the first sign that something is wrong with the DPF or its monitoring system.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • DPF warning light illuminated – often amber, sometimes flashing
  • Engine management light on – usually amber
  • Limp mode or noticeably reduced power – the ECU limits performance
  • Black smoke from the exhaust – soot is passing through the DPF
  • Increased fuel consumption – the engine may over-fuel to try and compensate
  • In severe cases, a strong smell of diesel or burning – from incomplete combustion or overheating DPF

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check DPF pressure sensor and hoses

Inspect the sensor hoses for blockages, kinks, or damage. A blocked hose is a common and cheap fix.

2

Read live data from the pressure sensor

Compare the sensor readings to expected values. An implausible reading points to the sensor itself.

3

Inspect for exhaust leaks

Check the entire exhaust system from turbo to tailpipe – leaks affect pressure differentials.

4

Perform a visual inspection of the DPF

Look for cracks, damage, or signs of melting. This may require removing the DPF for a thorough check.

5

Only then consider DPF replacement

If the DPF is physically damaged, it cannot be repaired – replacement is the only option.

Can You Still Drive It?

Only short distances, and with extreme caution. P2002 indicates the DPF is not working correctly – soot is passing through into the atmosphere, which is an MOT failure and may also indicate the DPF is damaged. If the DPF is cracked or melted, driving can allow hot exhaust gases to cause a fire risk or damage the turbocharger. If you notice black smoke or a strong smell of diesel, stop driving immediately. It's generally safe to drive gently to a specialist, but avoid heavy acceleration and long journeys until the cause is diagnosed.

Related Fault Codes

P2002 often appears alongside other DPF or engine performance codes. The combination helps narrow the diagnosis.

P2463 – DPF Soot Accumulation

If both P2002 and P2463 appear, the DPF is likely blocked and also damaged – replacement is usually required.

P0299 – Turbocharger Underboost

A damaged or blocked DPF restricts exhaust flow, causing underboost – see DPF blockage.

P244A – DPF Differential Pressure Too Low

The mirror image – if both appear, suspect a faulty pressure sensor or a leak in the exhaust system.

P0087 – Fuel Rail Pressure Too Low

May co‑occur if injector issues are causing excessive soot – address the fuel system first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P2002 on a Land Rover?

Only short distances and with caution. If the DPF is damaged, driving can be dangerous – hot exhaust gases may escape and cause a fire risk. If you see black smoke or smell diesel, stop immediately. Drive gently to a specialist for diagnosis.

Does P2002 always mean the DPF needs replacing?

No – in many cases, a faulty pressure sensor or a blocked sensor hose is the cause. DPF replacement is the last resort. Follow the diagnostic order above to avoid an expensive misdiagnosis.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return if the efficiency issue persists. Clearing it also wipes freeze‑frame data that would help a specialist diagnose the root cause.

How much should P2002 diagnosis cost?

Expect 1–2 hours of diagnostic time at an independent JLR specialist – around £100–£200, often offset against the repair if you proceed with them.

What's the most common fix for P2002 on a Discovery Sport or Evoque?

For the 204DTD Ingenium, replacing the DPF pressure sensor or cleaning the sensor hoses is the most common and cheapest fix – it resolves the code in over 40% of cases [PATTERN DATA]. Always inspect the hoses first.
