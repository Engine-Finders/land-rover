P244A means the differential pressure sensor is reading too low – the ECU expected a higher difference between the pressure before and after the DPF. On Land Rover diesels – especially 204DTD Ingenium and 306DT V6 – this is almost always an electrical or plumbing fault, not a DPF problem. A blocked sensor hose, a faulty sensor, or an exhaust leak are the usual culprits.

  • Most common and cheapest fix: blocked or kinked sensor hoses – typically £80–£150 [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: DPF replacement if it's physically damaged, costing £1,500–£2,500+ [PATTERN DATA]
  • Often accompanied by DPF warning light, limp mode, and black smoke
  • Frequently appears with P2463 (soot accumulation) – the combination suggests a blocked DPF with a failed sensor

Do not replace the DPF before checking the sensor hoses and the sensor itself. Follow the cheapest-first diagnostic order below.

What P244A Actually Means

The diesel particulate filter (DPF) is monitored by a differential pressure sensor that measures the pressure difference between the inlet and outlet of the filter. A higher pressure difference indicates more soot has accumulated, triggering regeneration. P244A is set when the ECU sees a pressure difference that is too low – either because the sensor is reporting a false reading, the hoses connecting the sensor to the exhaust are blocked, or there is an exhaust leak that equalises the pressure. This code is the mirror image of P244B (high differential pressure). On Land Rover engines, the 204DTD and 306DT both use the same type of differential pressure sensor, and the hoses are known to block with soot or moisture over time. A low-pressure reading can also happen if the DPF has been removed or is physically damaged, but that is much rarer than a sensor or hose fault.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • DPF warning light illuminated – often amber, sometimes flashing
  • Engine management light on – usually amber
  • Limp mode or noticeably reduced power – the ECU may limit performance
  • Black smoke from the exhaust – the DPF may not be regenerating properly
  • Increased fuel consumption – the engine may over-fuel to try and compensate
  • In severe cases, a strong smell of diesel or burning – from incomplete combustion or overheating DPF

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Inspect the sensor hoses

Check the two hoses from the exhaust to the sensor for blockages, kinks, or moisture. This resolves the majority of P244A cases.

2

Read live data from the pressure sensor

Compare the sensor reading at key-on, engine-off. It should read near zero – an implausible voltage points to the sensor or wiring.

3

Check sensor wiring and connector

Look for corrosion, chafed wires, or loose pins – a common and cheap fix.

4

Inspect for exhaust leaks

Check the exhaust system from turbo to tailpipe – leaks affect pressure differentials.

5

Only then consider DPF replacement

If the DPF is physically damaged or missing, replacement is necessary – but this is rare.

Can You Still Drive It?

Only short distances, and with caution. P244A itself is a sensor or plumbing fault – it won't cause immediate engine damage. However, if the sensor is reading falsely low, the ECU may not initiate regeneration when the DPF is actually full, leading to a blocked DPF and eventually a much larger bill. If the code is accompanied by limp mode or black smoke, get it checked promptly. It's safe to drive gently to a specialist, but don't ignore it for weeks – the DPF could become seriously blocked.

Related Fault Codes

P244A often appears alongside other DPF or engine performance codes. The combination helps narrow the diagnosis.

P244B – DPF Differential Pressure Too High

The mirror image – if both appear, suspect a faulty sensor or wiring, as the pressure can't be both high and low.

P2463 – DPF Soot Accumulation

If P244A and P2463 appear together, the sensor is likely reporting low pressure while the DPF is actually blocked – replace the sensor and force a regen.

P2002 – DPF Efficiency Below Threshold

A faulty pressure sensor can also trigger this code – see DPF blockage for more.

P0299 – Turbocharger Underboost

A blocked DPF restricts exhaust flow, causing underboost – see turbocharger failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P244A on a Land Rover?

Only short distances and with caution. It won't cause immediate breakdown, but it can prevent proper DPF regeneration, leading to a blocked filter. If you see black smoke or the car is in limp mode, get it checked promptly.

Does P244A always mean the DPF needs replacing?

No – in most cases, a blocked sensor hose or a faulty pressure sensor is the cause. DPF replacement is the last resort. Follow the diagnostic order above to avoid an expensive misdiagnosis.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return if the low pressure reading persists. Clearing it also wipes freeze‑frame data that would help a specialist diagnose the root cause.

How much should P244A diagnosis cost?

Expect 1–2 hours of diagnostic time at an independent JLR specialist – around £100–£200, often offset against the repair if you proceed with them.

What's the most common fix for P244A on a Discovery Sport or Evoque?

For the 204DTD Ingenium, cleaning or replacing the blocked sensor hoses is the most common and cheapest fix – it resolves the code in over 60% of cases [PATTERN DATA]. Always inspect the hoses first.
