P244B means the differential pressure sensor across the DPF is seeing a higher-than-expected pressure difference. On most Land Rover diesels this is a sensor or pipe blockage long before it's a blocked DPF — and proper diagnosis saves hundreds of pounds.

  • Most common real cause: blocked pressure sensor hoses or a failed sensor — typically £80–£200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: a full DPF that needs forced regeneration or replacement — £400–£1,200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside limp mode and engine management light
  • Frequently appears with P2002 or P2463 — the combination confirms a DPF blockage rather than a sensor glitch

What P244B Actually Means

In a modern diesel, the ECU uses a differential pressure sensor to measure the pressure drop across the DPF. P244B is logged when the ECU sees a pressure difference higher than the threshold it expects for the current driving conditions — typically at idle or low load. Crucially, this code describes a reading, not a diagnosis. The pressure difference could be genuinely high (a blocked DPF), or it could be a measurement error caused by a sensor fault, blocked reference pipes, or even a software glitch. That distinction can be the difference between a £80 sensor clean and a £1,200 DPF replacement. This code appears across the full range of JLR common-rail diesels, from the 204DTD Ingenium to the 306DT V6, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, usually amber
  • DPF warning light on the dashboard
  • Restricted performance message or limp mode
  • Noticeable loss of power, especially under acceleration
  • Poor fuel economy — the engine may be running richer to try and regenerate the DPF
  • Occasional white smoke from the exhaust if the DPF is attempting a failed regeneration

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Inspect pressure sensor hoses

Check the two reference pipes from the DPF to the sensor for blockages, splits, or condensation. A blocked pipe is a very common cause and costs almost nothing to clear.

2

Check live data at idle

A specialist compares differential pressure sensor readings against known-good values at idle. A reading above 0.1 bar at idle often points to a sensor or pipe issue, not a blocked filter.

3

Compare to P2002 and P2463

If P244B appears alone, suspect sensor or hoses. If it appears with P2002 or P2463, the DPF is genuinely blocked.

4

Test DPF soot load

Live data or forced regeneration test shows actual soot load and whether the DPF can regenerate normally.

5

Only then consider replacement

If steps 1–4 show the filter is genuinely blocked with ash and won't regenerate, replacement becomes the correct answer.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes — but get it addressed quickly. P244B on its own is not an engine-destroying fault. However, ignoring it risks DPF damage, failed regenerations, and eventually a blocked filter that will force limp mode and can lead to expensive turbocharger or injector problems. If the vehicle has entered limp mode, motorway driving becomes genuinely unsafe because you cannot accelerate normally to merge or overtake. Get it looked at within a week, sooner if you see the DPF warning light flashing or solid.

Related Fault Codes

P244B often appears alongside other DPF-related codes. Which codes accompany it changes the diagnosis.

P2002 — DPF Efficiency Below Threshold

If P244B and P2002 appear together, the DPF is genuinely blocked and not regenerating. If P244B appears alone, suspect sensor issues first.

P2463 — DPF Soot Accumulation

Indicates high soot loading. Together with P244B, confirms the filter needs an active forced regeneration or professional cleaning.

P246C — DPF Pressure Sensor Circuit High

Points toward a sensor electrical fault rather than a block. Strong supporting evidence for a failing sensor.

P0299 — Turbo Underboost

Can appear alongside DPF codes where exhaust back pressure is increased enough to affect turbo response. See turbocharger failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P244B on a Land Rover?

For short, non-motorway journeys to reach a garage, generally yes — but not for normal use, and not if you can see the DPF warning light flashing or if the vehicle has entered limp mode. Prolonged driving with this code risks damaging the DPF, turbocharger, and injectors.

Does P244B always mean the DPF is blocked?

No. Across the five ranked causes, a genuinely blocked DPF is only middle of the pack. A blocked sensor hose or a failing differential pressure sensor are both far more likely and cost a fraction as much.

Will a forced regeneration fix it?

That depends on the cause. If the DPF is blocked with soot but still physically intact, a forced regeneration often fixes it. If the DPF is blocked with ash, regeneration will not work — the filter needs cleaning or replacement. If the sensor or hoses are at fault, regeneration won't change the reading.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one to two hours of diagnostic time at an independent JLR specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.
