P246C Land Rover — DPF Restriction – Ash Accumulation

The DPF differential pressure sensor is reporting a pressure difference that indicates the filter is becoming blocked with ash. This is a gradual process, but once the code appears, the filter is approaching the end of its service life. What it means, how to confirm it, and what it costs to fix.

P246C means the ECU is seeing a high differential pressure across the DPF, consistent with ash accumulation that cannot be cleared by regeneration. Unlike soot, ash is a byproduct of normal combustion that accumulates over time and eventually fills the filter.

  • Most common cause: end-of-life ash fill – the filter has reached its designed capacity, typically after 100k–150k miles. Cost: £400–£900 for professional cleaning or replacement [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: full DPF replacement if cleaning fails – £900–£1,200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside DPF warning light and limp mode
  • Frequently appears with P244B or P2002 — confirming a genuine restriction rather than a sensor fault

What P246C Actually Means

In a diesel engine, the DPF traps soot (particulate matter) and periodically burns it off during regeneration. However, the process also leaves behind ash – metallic compounds from engine oil and fuel additives – which cannot be burned. Over time, ash accumulates and reduces the available volume inside the filter. P246C is the ECU's way of saying the pressure differential across the DPF has exceeded the threshold that indicates a high level of ash accumulation. This is a gradual, mileage-related failure, not a sudden component breakdown. Unlike soot, ash cannot be cleared by driving at motorway speeds or using a forced regeneration – the filter must be physically cleaned or replaced. This code is common on higher-mileage 204DTD Ingenium, 306DT, and 448DT engines, especially those used extensively in urban environments where the DPF never gets hot enough for effective passive regeneration.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • DPF warning light illuminated – often amber, sometimes flashing
  • Engine management light may also appear
  • Restricted performance message or limp mode when the filter is heavily blocked
  • Noticeable loss of power, especially under acceleration – the engine is strangled by exhaust backpressure
  • Poor fuel economy – the engine may run richer to try to manage the restriction
  • Occasional white smoke from the exhaust if the DPF is overheating

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check pressure sensor & hoses

Inspect the differential pressure sensor and its reference pipes for blockages or leaks. This is the cheapest test and rules out a false reading.

2

Live data – compare readings

A specialist will compare the sensor reading at idle with known-good values. A reading above 0.1 bar at idle indicates a real restriction, not a sensor glitch.

3

Check soot load via diagnostic tool

Live data shows the calculated soot mass. If it's high but the car has recently been driven, the DPF may have failed to regenerate.

4

Attempt a forced regeneration

If the soot load is high but ash is not the primary issue, a forced regen may clear it. However, if P246C persists after regen, ash is the culprit.

5

Professional cleaning or replacement

If all else fails, the DPF must be removed and cleaned using a specialist machine (e.g., a thermal or ultrasonic cleaner). If cleaning fails, replacement is the only option.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes – but not for long. If the DPF is only partially blocked, you may be able to drive for a short time, but the car will eventually enter limp mode to protect the engine. Continued driving with a blocked DPF can cause overheating, turbocharger damage, and even engine failure. If the DPF warning light is flashing, stop immediately and seek professional help. Do not attempt a long journey – the risk of secondary damage is high. Get it diagnosed and addressed within a few days.

Related Fault Codes

P246C often appears with other DPF codes. The combination tells you whether the issue is sensor-related or a genuine blockage.

P244B – DPF Differential Pressure Too High

If both codes appear, the DPF is almost certainly restricted – P244B indicates the pressure difference, P246C points to ash as the cause.

P2002 – DPF Efficiency Below Threshold

Indicates the DPF is not trapping particles effectively – often caused by ash fill or physical damage.

P2463 – DPF Soot Accumulation

This code indicates high soot loading, which can often be resolved by regeneration. If P246C is also present, the soot load is likely a symptom, not the root cause.

P0299 – Turbocharger Underboost

Excessive exhaust backpressure from a blocked DPF can reduce turbo response. See turbocharger failure for more.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P246C on a Land Rover?

For short, local journeys to get to a garage, yes – but avoid long distances, motorways, or heavy acceleration. If the DPF warning light is flashing, stop immediately. The car will soon enter limp mode, and continued driving risks turbocharger damage.

Will a forced regeneration fix P246C?

Almost certainly not. Forced regeneration burns soot, but ash cannot be burned. If the code is triggered by ash, regeneration will not change the reading. You need professional cleaning or replacement.

How long does a DPF last before ash fills it?

Typically 100,000–150,000 miles, depending on driving style and oil quality. Some vehicles may reach 200,000 miles, but urban driving and poor maintenance shorten life.

How much does DPF cleaning cost?

Professional off-car cleaning typically costs £400–£700, while replacement (supply and fit) ranges from £900–£1,200 for most Land Rovers. Replacement is often the cheaper option for high-mileage vehicles where cleaning may not be effective.
