U0100 Land Rover — Lost Communication With ECM/PCM

A critical communication fault code indicating that the Engine Control Module (ECM) or Powertrain Control Module (PCM) is not responding on the vehicle's network. Usually a wiring, power, or ground issue — but it can leave you stranded if ignored.

U0100 means the ECU has lost communication with the engine control module (ECM) or powertrain control module (PCM) on the vehicle's CAN bus network. This is almost always an electrical issue — a wiring fault, a blown fuse, a poor ground, or a failed module. On Land Rover and Range Rover models, it can also be caused by a battery voltage issue or a faulty CAN bus termination resistor.

  • Most common real cause: a blown fuse or faulty relay — typically £50–£150 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: failed ECM/PCM module, £600–£1,200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside engine management light and cranks but won't start
  • Frequently appears with U0101 or U0102 — the combination helps narrow the cause

Do not let anyone sell you a new ECM before the fuses, relays, wiring, and battery voltage have been ruled out. Full diagnostic order below.

What U0100 Actually Means

The Engine Control Module (ECM) and Powertrain Control Module (PCM) are the central computers that manage engine operation. They communicate with other modules — such as the transmission control module, ABS module, and instrument cluster — via a controller area network (CAN) bus. U0100 is a network diagnostic trouble code (DTC) that is logged when the ECM/PCM does not respond to a request for communication from another module on the network. This code indicates a communication failure, not a mechanical engine failure. The ECM/PCM itself could be faulty, but equally the issue could be a loss of power or ground to the module, a damaged CAN bus wire, a faulty CAN bus termination resistor, or even a dead battery or poor battery connection. This distinction is the difference between a £50 fix and a £1,200 module replacement. This code appears across the full range of JLR vehicles, from the 204DTD Ingenium to the 306DT V6, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, usually amber
  • Engine will not start or cranks but won't fire
  • Limp mode or complete loss of engine power
  • Instrument cluster may show no RPM, temperature, or fuel readings
  • Other warning lights may appear (ABS, transmission, airbag) due to network communication loss
  • In some cases, the vehicle may not even crank — the starter may be disabled

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check fuses and relays

Locate the ECM/PCM power supply fuses and relays in the fuse box (often under the bonnet or in the passenger footwell). Replace any blown fuses or suspect relays. This is the cheapest and quickest fix.

2

Test battery voltage and connections

Check battery voltage — it should be at least 12.4V with the ignition off. Clean and tighten battery terminals and earth straps. A weak battery can cause module communication issues.

3

Inspect CAN bus wiring

Visually inspect the CAN bus wiring (twisted pair, usually yellow/green or orange/green) for chafing, corrosion, or damage. Check continuity between modules.

4

Check CAN bus termination resistors

Using a multimeter, check the resistance between CAN high and CAN low on the diagnostic connector (OBD-II port). Should read 60 ohms. If it's 120 ohms, one termination resistor is missing or faulty.

5

Only then consider ECM/PCM replacement

If all above check out and the fault persists, the ECM/PCM itself may be faulty. This should only be done by a specialist with the correct diagnostic equipment.

Can You Still Drive It?

Probably not. U0100 indicates that the engine control module is not communicating — which means the engine may not start at all, or if it does, it may be in limp mode with very limited power. In many cases, the vehicle will not move under its own power. If the engine is running and the car is drivable, you may be able to drive a short distance to a garage, but the risk of the engine cutting out is high. In most cases, the vehicle will need to be recovered. Do not attempt to drive if the engine is running poorly, if warning lights are flashing, or if the car is not responding properly to the throttle.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with U0100 on a Land Rover?

Probably not. The engine may not start, or if it does, it will likely be in limp mode or cut out. The vehicle should be recovered to a garage — do not drive it if the engine is running poorly or the car is not responding properly.

Does U0100 always mean the ECM has failed?

No. In most cases, a blown fuse, a dead battery, poor battery connection, or damaged CAN bus wiring is the culprit. The ECM itself is one of the least common causes of U0100. Always check the simple things first.

Can a dead battery cause U0100?

Yes. Low battery voltage can prevent modules from communicating on the CAN bus. If the battery is weak or discharged, charge or replace it first before condemning any module.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one to two hours of diagnostic time at an independent JLR specialist — around £80–£160. Many will offset this against the repair if you proceed.

Can clearing the code fix it?

Only if the fault was intermittent and caused by a temporary glitch. In most cases, the code will return immediately or on the next ignition cycle. Clearing it without fixing the root cause is not a repair.
