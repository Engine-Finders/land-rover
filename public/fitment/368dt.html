The Short Answer

The Land Rover 368DT / 3.6 TDV8 is a 3.6-litre V8 turbo-diesel engine fitted to top-tier Land Rover models from 2006 to 2013. It powers the L322 Range Rover and L320 Range Rover Sport, offering strong performance and refinement. However, this engine has known crankshaft fracture and timing chain tensioner risks that make it a high-stakes choice for buyers. To confirm if your Land Rover has this engine, check the engine code on the timing cover or use the Land Rover VIN decoder.

  • Power outputs: 268–272 PS (TDV8) with 640 Nm torque.
  • Key models: Range Rover L322 (2006–2010), Range Rover Sport L320 (2006–2013).

How to Confirm Which Engine Is in Your Land Rover

There are three reliable ways to confirm whether your Land Rover is fitted with the 368DT / 3.6 TDV8 engine. Use the method that best suits your situation.

  1. Check the engine code label.

    Open the bonnet and locate the engine code label on the timing cover or cylinder head. The code will be clearly stamped as 368DT or 3.6 TDV8. This is the most direct method. On L322 models, the label is often on the front of the engine, near the oil filler cap.

  2. Use the Land Rover VIN decoder.

    Your vehicle's VIN (Vehicle Identification Number) can be decoded to reveal the engine type. The 8th character of the VIN indicates the engine. For the 368DT, this will be a specific letter (often 'G' for TDV8). You can verify this using a free online Land Rover VIN decoder.

  3. Check your Land Rover's registration document.

    The V5C logbook includes the engine number, which can be cross-referenced with Land Rover's engine list. While this is less direct than the other methods, it is a useful secondary check if the engine label is not accessible.

Why Correct Engine Identification Matters

Getting the engine code right is critical — especially if you are considering a replacement. Installing the wrong engine variant can lead to:

  • ECU compatibility issues — The engine control unit is programmed for a specific power output and emissions setup; a mismatch will cause running problems or immobilisation.
  • Mounting and fitment problems — Even within the same family, slight variations in auxiliary components (turbocharger, intercooler, exhaust) can prevent a direct fit.
  • Legal compliance failures — Installing an engine not originally homologated for your chassis can create MOT or insurance complications.
  • Voided warranty or specialist support — Most vetted Land Rover specialists will not work on mismatched engine/chassis combinations.

Always check the Engine Code Guide and consult a specialist before purchasing a used or reconditioned engine. The 368DT has specific risks, and cross-referencing the exact engine code with your vehicle's chassis ensures a smooth replacement — and helps you avoid buying an engine with a known problem.

Common Misidentifications: What the 368DT Is Not

The 368DT / 3.6 TDV8 is frequently confused with other Land Rover diesel engines. Here's what to look out for to avoid a costly mistake — particularly important given this engine's reputation.

448DT 4.4 SDV8

The later 4.4-litre V8 diesel (448DT) is a completely different engine — and a much more reliable one. It does not have the crankshaft fracture risk of the 368DT. If you're buying a replacement, the 448DT is the preferred option, but they are not interchangeable. Check the engine code carefully.

Read about the 448DT 4.4 SDV8

276DT 2.7 TDV6

The 2.7-litre V6 diesel (276DT) is a smaller engine with a different block configuration and oil pump design. It was used in Discovery 3 and earlier L320 models. The 368DT is a V8 and physically larger. Do not confuse them.

Read about the 276DT TDV6

508PS 4.4 SDV8

The 508PS is also a 4.4-litre V8 diesel but with a different engine management system and higher power output (508 PS). It appears in some later L405 and L494 models. While related to the 448DT, it is a different engine from the 368DT. Always confirm the exact engine code.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between 368DT and 3.6 TDV8?

368DT is the internal engine code used by Land Rover and Ford (the engine family is Ford's Lion V8). 3.6 TDV8 is the marketing name. They refer to the same engine. The 368DT code is found on the engine block itself.

How can I tell if my Land Rover has the 368DT engine without opening the bonnet?

The easiest way is to check your VIN. The 8th character of the VIN indicates the engine type. For the 368DT, it will be a specific letter (often 'G' for TDV8). You can also use the Land Rover VIN decoder on this site.

Is the 368DT / 3.6 TDV8 a reliable engine?

Mixed. The 368DT has known timing chain tensioner failures (especially on early L322 units, 2006–2009) and a documented crankshaft fracture risk. While some examples have covered high mileages without issue, many specialists advise avoiding this engine unless you have a full documented service history and have had the timing chain tensioners inspected. The later 4.4 SDV8 (448DT) is significantly more reliable. For a detailed verdict, see the 368DT Knowledge Centre.

Can I fit a 448DT 4.4 SDV8 engine instead of a 368DT?

Not directly. While both are V8 diesels, they have different engine mounts, ECU systems, turbocharger configurations, and exhaust layouts. A conversion is technically possible but prohibitively expensive and rarely worthwhile. If your 368DT has failed, the most cost-effective solution is usually a replacement 368DT with a verified service history.

A used engine may be cheaper, but condition and provenance are critical — especially given the known crankshaft risk. See the replacement cost guide for more detail.

What is the engine code for the 3.6 TDV8 in my Land Rover?

The engine code is 368DT. You can find it on a label on the timing cover, or by decoding the VIN. The power output variant (TDV8) does not change the base engine code.
