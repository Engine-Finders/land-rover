204DTD Oil in Coolant / Mayonnaise Under Oil Cap — Causes, Diagnosis & Repair Costs

Finding a creamy mayonnaise‑like sludge under your 204DTD (2.0 Ingenium diesel) oil cap, or noticing oil in the coolant expansion tank, is a clear sign of coolant and oil mixing. This is almost always caused by a failed head gasket, a cracked cylinder head, or a leaking oil cooler. This guide explains what this symptom means for your Discovery Sport, Evoque, or Freelander 2, what it costs to fix in the UK in 2026, and how to decide whether to repair or replace your engine.

The Short Answer

Oil in the coolant or mayonnaise under the oil cap on a 204DTD means oil and coolant are mixing – a sign of a breached head gasket, a porous cylinder head, or a failed oil cooler. This is a serious issue that requires immediate attention. Driving with this condition can cause overheating, loss of lubrication, and catastrophic engine failure. Repair costs range from £1,500–£3,000 for a head gasket replacement to £2,500–£4,500 for a cylinder head replacement, and up to £4,500–£6,500 for a replacement engine. What the 204DTD Specifically Does

Oil and coolant mixing on a 204DTD usually shows up as one of two signs – either a creamy emulsion on the oil cap or dipstick, or an oily film floating in the coolant expansion tank. The pattern often depends on where the leak is.

🧴 Mayo under the oil cap

A thick, yellowish‑white sludge on the underside of the oil filler cap or on the dipstick. This is coolant emulsifying with oil – a classic sign of a head gasket leak or cracked head.

🛢️ Oil film in coolant tank

An oily sheen or dark streaks floating in the coolant expansion tank. This indicates oil is entering the cooling system – often through a failed oil cooler or a head gasket breach.

🌡️ Overheating or coolant loss

The engine may run hotter than normal, or you may notice the coolant level dropping. This is often the underlying cause that led to the gasket failure.

What else to look for: The coolant temperature warning light may illuminate, or the engine management light might come on. You might also notice white smoke from the exhaust (steam). For a full list of 204DTD symptoms, see our 204DTD white smoke and 204DTD engine overheating guides.

Why This Engine In Particular — The 204DTD's Known Weaknesses

The 204DTD (Ingenium D) has several well‑documented failure points that can cause oil and coolant to mix. Understanding these helps you and your specialist pinpoint the issue quickly.

🔧 Head Gasket Failure – The Primary Suspect

The head gasket on the 204DTD can fail, especially if the engine has been overheated. This allows oil and coolant to mix – either through a direct breach between oil and water galleries, or via combustion pressure forcing oil into the coolant. Overheating is often caused by a failing EGR cooler or a coolant leak that went unnoticed.

🔧 Oil Cooler Failure

The 204DTD's oil cooler can develop internal leaks, allowing oil to mix with coolant. This is less common than head gasket failure but can produce similar symptoms – oil in the coolant with no mayo under the cap. It's often misdiagnosed as a head gasket issue.

🔧 Porous Cylinder Head

In some cases, the 204DTD's cylinder head can develop porosity, allowing oil to seep into coolant passages. This is a casting defect that can occur on earlier production units and often requires a replacement head.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

Diagnosing oil/coolant mixing on a 204DTD requires a systematic approach to differentiate between head gasket, oil cooler, and porous head.

1

Coolant Pressure Test

They'll pressurise the cooling system to check for leaks. If pressure drops and no external leak is found, it points to an internal breach – either the head gasket or oil cooler.

2

Combustion Leak Test (Block Test)

They'll check for combustion gases in the coolant using a chemical test. A colour change confirms a head gasket failure.

3

Oil Cooler Integrity Test

They'll isolate the oil cooler and test it for internal leaks. This helps rule out the oil cooler as the cause.

4

Borescope Inspection

They'll use a borescope to inspect the cylinders for signs of coolant intrusion (clean pistons) or scoring, which can indicate head gasket or porous head issues.

5

Oil Analysis

They'll check the oil for coolant contamination – a milky emulsion on the dipstick is a clear sign.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes oil in coolant or mayo under the oil cap on a 204DTD?

The most common causes are a failed head gasket, a porous cylinder head, or a leaking oil cooler. Overheating (often from EGR cooler failure) is a frequent trigger for head gasket issues.

How much does it cost to fix oil in coolant on a 204DTD?

Costs range from £500–£1,000 for an oil cooler, £1,500–£3,000 for a head gasket, £2,500–£4,500 for a cylinder head, and up to £4,500–£6,500 for a replacement engine.

Can I drive with mayo under the oil cap on a 204DTD?

No. This indicates a serious breach. Driving can cause engine damage, overheating, and loss of lubrication. Get it diagnosed immediately.

Is mayo under the oil cap always a head gasket failure?

Not necessarily. Short journeys can cause condensation, but if it's thick and accompanied by oil in coolant, it's almost certainly a head gasket or oil cooler issue.

What should I do if I find oil in the coolant on my 204DTD?

Stop driving immediately. Check the coolant and oil levels. Book a diagnostic with a Land Rover specialist as soon as possible. Continuing to drive risks catastrophic engine failure.

Is oil in coolant covered by a Land Rover warranty?

Manufacturing defects in the head gasket or oil cooler may be covered, but wear and tear or damage from overheating is usually excluded. Always check your warranty terms.
