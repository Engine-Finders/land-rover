What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general white smoke guide covers several possible causes across the whole Land Rover range, on the 204DTD the picture narrows. This engine's dominant white‑smoke failure signatures are coolant entering the combustion chamber (via a leaking EGR cooler or a failed head gasket) or unburnt diesel (from faulty injectors or timing issues). The 204DTD is known for EGR cooler failures, especially on pre‑2019 models, and this symptom should never be ignored.

Why This Engine In Particular

The 204DTD's EGR cooler is a common failure point. It can crack internally, allowing coolant to mix with exhaust gases and be drawn into the intake manifold, producing thick white smoke and a sweet smell. This is especially prevalent on vehicles used for short journeys where the EGR system is more prone to soot buildup and thermal stress. A head gasket failure is less common but can occur if the engine has been overheated – often as a result of coolant loss from the EGR cooler. Faulty injectors that don't atomise fuel properly can also cause white smoke (unburnt diesel), usually accompanied by a rough idle and misfire.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

A specialist will start with a coolant pressure test to check for leaks. If the pressure drops without external leaks, the coolant is going internally. They'll also test the coolant for combustion gases (hydrocarbon test) – a positive result confirms a head gasket or cracked head. An EGR cooler inspection involves checking for coolant seepage or white residue in the intake. For injector issues, a balance test will identify faulty units. Borescope inspection can reveal coolant wash on piston crowns.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is white smoke from a 204DTD always a head gasket?

Not always. The most common cause is a leaking EGR cooler, which allows coolant into the exhaust. A head gasket failure is less common but more serious. A specialist can tell the difference with a coolant pressure test and hydrocarbon test.

At what mileage does a 204DTD typically start blowing white smoke?

Most cases in our data occur between 80,000 and 120,000 miles, especially on pre‑2019 models. Regular coolant changes and EGR system cleaning can reduce the risk.

Can I drive a 204DTD with white smoke?

No – stop driving immediately. White smoke often indicates coolant entering the combustion chamber, which can cause hydro‑lock, further engine damage, or complete failure. Have the vehicle recovered to a specialist.

How much does it cost to fix white smoke on a 204DTD?

An EGR cooler replacement typically costs £400–£800 at an independent specialist. A head gasket repair can cost £1,500–£3,000. If the engine has suffered catastrophic failure, you're looking at £4,500–£6,500 for a reconditioned engine supply and fit.
