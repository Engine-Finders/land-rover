224DT White Smoke From Exhaust — Causes, Diagnosis & Repair Costs

White smoke from your 224DT (2.2 Duratorq) engine is a clear sign of coolant or unburnt diesel entering the combustion chamber. This can be caused by a failed EGR cooler, a blown head gasket, or faulty injectors. This guide explains what this symptom means for your Freelander 2 or Evoque, what it costs to fix in the UK in 2026, and how to decide whether to repair or replace your engine.

The Short Answer

White smoke from a 224DT engine is most often caused by coolant burning in the combustion chamber, typically from a failed EGR cooler or a blown head gasket. The EGR cooler is a known weak point on this Ford-sourced engine – it can leak coolant into the exhaust, producing thick white smoke with a sweet smell. Repair costs range from £500–£1,200 for an EGR cooler replacement to £1,500–£3,000 for a head gasket, and up to £3,000–£5,000 for a replacement engine if the head is warped. Injector failure can also cause white smoke (unburnt diesel), but this is less common. Early diagnosis is critical – continued driving can cause overheating and catastrophic engine damage.

  • Most common cause (224DT): EGR cooler failure – a known weakness on higher-mileage units
  • Typical diagnostic cost: £80–£150 [EM-QUOTE]
  • Key indicator: Thick white smoke with a sweet smell, especially on start-up or under load
  • Verdict: Immediate – do not continue driving. Have the cooling system pressure-tested without delay.

If your 224DT is blowing white smoke, stop driving and book a diagnostic – an EGR cooler replacement is far cheaper than a full engine replacement.

What the 224DT Specifically Does

White smoke from a 224DT is distinct – it has a characteristic sweet smell (coolant) or a raw diesel smell (unburnt fuel) and often follows a pattern depending on the root cause.

💨 Thick white smoke on start-up

A dense cloud of white smoke on cold start that clears as the engine warms up. This is classic coolant burning – often from a leaking EGR cooler or a minor head gasket breach.

💨 Persistent white smoke at all times

Continuous white smoke that doesn't clear, often with a sweet smell. This suggests a significant coolant leak – either a failed EGR cooler or a blown head gasket.

💨 White smoke with a diesel smell

If the smoke smells strongly of diesel, it's unburnt fuel – usually from a failed injector that's over-fuelling or leaking.

What else to look for: White smoke is often accompanied by coolant loss, overheating, or the coolant temperature warning light. If you're topping up coolant regularly without finding a leak, this is a key warning sign. For a full list of 224DT symptoms, see our 224DT black smoke and 224DT blue smoke guides.

Why This Engine In Particular — The 224DT's Known Weaknesses

The 224DT (Ford Duratorq 2.2) is a reliable engine, but it has a few well-documented failure points that can lead to white smoke. Understanding these helps you and your specialist pinpoint the issue quickly.

🔧 EGR Cooler Failure – The Number One Suspect

The EGR cooler on the 224DT is prone to internal leaks, allowing coolant to mix with the exhaust gases. This produces thick white smoke, especially on start-up. It's a common failure on higher-mileage units (80k+), particularly if the vehicle has been used for short journeys or has seen regular DPF regens.

🔧 Head Gasket Failure

A blown head gasket allows coolant to enter the cylinders, producing white smoke. This is more serious and often requires a full strip-down. It can be caused by overheating (often from a failed EGR cooler) or general wear.

🔧 Injector Failure (Unburnt Diesel)

A faulty injector can over-fuel or leak, causing white smoke with a strong diesel smell. This is less common than EGR cooler failure but should be checked, especially if there's no coolant loss.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

Diagnosing white smoke on a 224DT requires a systematic approach to differentiate between EGR cooler, head gasket, and injector issues.

1

Coolant Pressure Test

They'll pressurise the cooling system to check for leaks. A drop in pressure with no external leak points to the EGR cooler or head gasket.

2

Exhaust Gas Test (Block Test)

They'll check for combustion gases in the coolant using a chemical test. A colour change confirms a head gasket failure.

3

EGR Cooler Inspection

They'll visually inspect the EGR cooler for signs of leakage – sometimes a tell-tale white residue or coolant staining is visible on the cooler body.

4

Injector Leak-Off Test

They'll measure fuel return from each injector. Excessive return indicates injector seal leakage or injector failure.

5

Borescope Inspection

They'll use a borescope to inspect the cylinders for signs of coolant intrusion (clean pistons) to confirm a head gasket failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes white smoke from a 224DT engine?

The most common causes are a failed EGR cooler (coolant leaking into the exhaust), a blown head gasket, or faulty fuel injectors (unburnt diesel).

How much does it cost to fix white smoke on a 224DT?

Costs range from £500–£1,200 for an EGR cooler replacement, £1,500–£3,000 for a head gasket, and up to £3,000–£5,000 for a replacement engine.

Can I drive with a 224DT blowing white smoke?

No. If the smoke is thick and persistent, or accompanied by overheating, stop driving immediately. Continued driving can cause serious engine damage.

Is white smoke from a 224DT always the EGR cooler?

Not always, but it is the most common cause, especially on higher-mileage units. A thorough diagnosis is essential to confirm the exact failure point.

What should I do if my 224DT is blowing white smoke?

Check the coolant level when the engine is cold. If it's low, top up with the correct coolant and book a diagnostic with a Land Rover specialist as soon as possible. Do not ignore the warning – a small leak can quickly escalate.

Is white smoke covered by a Land Rover warranty?

Manufacturing defects in the EGR cooler or head gasket may be covered, but wear and tear or neglect is usually excluded. Check your warranty terms carefully.
