306DT Coolant Loss With No Visible Leak — Causes, Diagnosis & Repair Costs

Coolant loss without an external leak on the 306DT (3.0 SDV6/TDV6) is often a sign of an internal leak – most commonly from the EGR cooler, a failing head gasket, or a cracked cylinder head. The Short Answer

If your 306DT is losing coolant but you can’t see a leak, the coolant is escaping internally – most commonly via a cracked EGR cooler, a blown head gasket, or a porous cylinder head. The EGR cooler is the most frequent culprit, allowing coolant to be burned in the exhaust (often with white smoke). Repair costs range from £400–£800 for an EGR cooler, £1,500–£3,000 for a head gasket, and up to £6,500–£9,500 for a replacement engine. Ignoring it can lead to overheating, cylinder head warping, and complete engine failure.

What the 306DT Specifically Does When Losing Coolant

Coolant loss without a visible puddle or drip is a classic sign of an internal leak. Here’s what owners typically experience on the 306DT.

💧 Coolant level drops repeatedly

You top up the coolant, and within a few days or weeks, the level has dropped again. No signs of leaks under the car or around the engine bay. This is the most obvious clue.

💨 White smoke from exhaust (sweet smell)

If the coolant is being burned in the cylinders, you’ll see thick white smoke from the exhaust, often with a sweet smell. This is a strong indicator of a head gasket or cracked head.

🌡️ Intermittent overheating

As coolant is lost, the engine may run hot or overheat intermittently, especially under load. The temperature gauge may rise and fall unpredictably.

What else to look for: A sweet coolant smell inside the cabin (from the heater matrix), or bubbles in the coolant expansion tank (combustion gases entering the cooling system). If you see the coolant temperature warning or 306DT white smoke, the issue is urgent. For more general coolant loss info, see our coolant loss hub.

Why This Engine In Particular – The 306DT’s Cooling Weaknesses

The 306DT has several known internal leak points that can cause coolant loss without external drips. Here are the most common.

🔧 EGR Cooler Internal Leak

The 306DT’s EGR cooler is a known weak point. Internal corrosion or stress fractures allow coolant to leak into the exhaust gas stream, where it is vaporised and lost through the tailpipe. This is the most common cause of unexplained coolant loss, often with white smoke. Replacing the EGR cooler is a routine but labour‑intensive job.

🔧 Head Gasket or Cracked Cylinder Head

If the engine has been overheated or suffered from a poor cooling system, the head gasket can fail or the cylinder head can crack. This allows coolant to enter the combustion chambers, producing thick white smoke and causing rapid coolant loss. This is a much more serious repair, often requiring a head skim or replacement.

🔧 Water Pump Seal Leak (Internal)

While less common, the water pump can develop a small internal weep that allows coolant to escape through the pump’s weep hole, often dripping onto the engine and evaporating before it hits the ground. This can be difficult to spot and may require a water pump replacement.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause of Coolant Loss

Diagnosing internal coolant loss on a 306DT requires a systematic approach to identify whether the leak is from the EGR cooler, the head gasket, or elsewhere.

1

Coolant Pressure Test

They'll pressurise the cooling system and monitor for pressure drop. If the pressure drops without external leaks, the coolant is going internally.

2

Exhaust Gas Test (Hydrocarbon)

Using a chemical test on the coolant, they'll check for combustion gases – a positive result confirms a head gasket or cracked head.

3

Borescope Inspection

Through the glow plug holes, they can inspect the cylinders for signs of coolant washing (clean piston crowns) or head gasket breaches.

4

EGR Cooler Inspection

They'll examine the EGR cooler for signs of coolant seepage – wetness around the cooler or white residue in the intake. This is often the simplest fix.

5

Water Pump Check

They'll check the water pump for weepage and play in the bearing. A failing pump can cause a small internal leak.

6

Coolant System Sniffer Test

A sniffer test on the coolant expansion tank can detect the presence of exhaust gases, confirming a combustion leak.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes a 306DT to lose coolant with no visible leak?

The most common causes are a cracked EGR cooler (coolant leaking into the exhaust), a blown head gasket, or a cracked cylinder head. All allow coolant to escape internally without drips.

How much does it cost to fix coolant loss on a 306DT?

Costs range from £400–£800 for an EGR cooler, £1,500–£3,000 for a head gasket, and up to £6,500–£9,500 for a replacement engine.

Can I drive with a 306DT that's losing coolant?

No – do not drive. Continued driving will cause overheating, which can warp the cylinder head or seize the engine. Have the vehicle recovered to a specialist.

Is coolant loss on a 306DT always serious?

Yes. Even a small internal leak will worsen over time and can lead to catastrophic engine failure. Address it as soon as you notice the coolant level dropping.

What should I do if my 306DT is losing coolant?

Check the coolant level (when cold) and top up if needed. Look for white smoke or a sweet smell. Do not drive. Have the vehicle recovered to a Land Rover specialist for diagnosis.

Is coolant loss covered by a Land Rover warranty?

It depends. EGR cooler or head gasket failure due to a manufacturing defect may be covered under a manufacturer or extended warranty. However, damage caused by neglect or overheating is typically not covered.
