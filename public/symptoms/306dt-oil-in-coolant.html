What the 306DT Specifically Does When Oil and Coolant Mix

Oil and coolant mixing is a serious problem that shows up in a few distinct ways. Here's what owners typically experience on the 306DT.

🧴 Mayonnaise under the oil cap

A thick, creamy, yellowish-brown sludge on the underside of the oil filler cap. While a small amount can be condensation in cold weather, a thick layer is a strong indicator of coolant mixing with oil.

💧 Oil in the coolant expansion tank

The coolant in the expansion tank has an oily film on top, or appears brown and milky. This is clear evidence of an internal leak allowing oil into the cooling system.

🌡️ Engine overheating

As oil contaminates the coolant, it reduces its heat-transfer efficiency. This can cause the engine to overheat, even if the coolant level is full.

What else to look for: A sweet smell from the exhaust (burning coolant), white smoke, or a fluctuating temperature gauge. If you see the coolant temperature warning or 306DT white smoke, the issue is urgent. For more general oil/coolant mixing info, see our oil in coolant hub.

Why This Engine In Particular – The 306DT's Known Weaknesses

The 306DT has several known failure points that can cause oil and coolant to mix. Here are the most common.

🔧 Oil Cooler Failure

The 306DT's oil cooler, mounted between the oil filter housing and the engine block, is a common failure point. The internal gasket can degrade or the cooler itself can crack, allowing high-pressure engine oil to leak into the low-pressure coolant system. This is the most common cause of oil-in-coolant on the 306DT, especially on higher-mileage engines.

🔧 Head Gasket Failure

If the engine has been overheated or suffered from a poor cooling system, the head gasket can fail. This can allow oil and coolant to mix, often accompanied by white smoke and coolant loss. This is a much more serious repair, often requiring a head skim or replacement.

🔧 Cracked Cylinder Head

In severe cases, the 306DT's cylinder head can crack, usually as a result of overheating. This allows oil and coolant to mix internally. A cracked head often requires a complete cylinder head replacement, which is a major and costly repair.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause of Oil/Coolant Mixing

Diagnosing oil/coolant mixing on a 306DT requires a systematic approach to identify whether the leak is from the oil cooler, the head gasket, or elsewhere.

1

Visual Inspection

They'll check the oil filler cap for mayonnaise, the coolant expansion tank for oil contamination, and look for external oil or coolant leaks.

2

Coolant Pressure Test

They'll pressurise the cooling system and monitor for pressure drop. Oil contamination in the coolant is a strong indicator of an internal leak.

3

Exhaust Gas Test (Hydrocarbon)

Using a chemical test on the coolant, they'll check for combustion gases – a positive result confirms a head gasket or cracked head.

4

Oil Cooler Inspection

They'll inspect the oil cooler for signs of internal leakage – this often involves removing the oil filter housing to check for cross-contamination.

5

Borescope Inspection

Through the glow plug holes, they can inspect the cylinders for signs of coolant wash (clean piston crowns) or head gasket breaches.

6

Oil Analysis

They may send an oil sample for lab analysis to detect coolant contamination, which confirms the mixing is occurring.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes oil in coolant on a 306DT?

The most common causes are a failed oil cooler (internal gasket leak), a blown head gasket, or a cracked cylinder head. All allow oil and coolant to mix internally.

How much does it cost to fix oil in coolant on a 306DT?

Costs range from £600–£1,200 for an oil cooler, £1,500–£3,000 for a head gasket, and up to £6,500–£9,500 for a replacement engine.

Can I drive with a 306DT that has oil in the coolant?

No – do not drive. Oil contamination reduces the coolant's ability to transfer heat, leading to overheating and potential engine seizure. Have the vehicle recovered to a specialist.

Is oil in coolant on a 306DT always serious?

Yes. Even a small amount of oil in the coolant indicates an internal leak that will worsen over time. It can also cause damage to the cooling system components and the engine itself.

What should I do if I find oil in my 306DT's coolant?

Do not drive the vehicle. Check the oil cap for mayonnaise and the coolant for oil contamination. Have the vehicle recovered to a Land Rover specialist for diagnosis. A simple oil cooler replacement could save your engine.

Is oil in coolant covered by a Land Rover warranty?

It depends. Oil cooler or head gasket failure due to a manufacturing defect may be covered under a manufacturer or extended warranty. However, damage caused by neglect or overheating is typically not covered.
