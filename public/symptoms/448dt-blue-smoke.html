448DT Blue Smoke From Exhaust — Causes, Diagnosis & Repair Costs

Blue smoke from your 448DT (4.4 V8 diesel) engine is a clear sign that oil is burning in the combustion chamber. While this Ford‑derived V8 is generally robust, blue smoke can point to worn valve stem seals, turbocharger oil seal failure, or (rarely) piston ring wear. This guide explains what this symptom means for your Range Rover L405 or L494, what it costs to fix in the UK in 2026, and how to decide whether to repair or replace your engine.

The Short Answer

Blue smoke from a 448DT engine means oil is entering the combustion chamber and burning. The most common culprits are worn valve stem seals (allowing oil to seep past the valves), a failing turbocharger oil seal (pushing oil into the intake), or – less commonly – worn piston rings. Repair costs range from £1,500–£3,000 for valve stem seals to £800–£2,000 for turbocharger repairs, and up to £6,000–£9,000 for a replacement engine if the rings are worn and the bores are scored.

What Owners Notice First — The 448DT's Telltale Signs

Blue smoke from the 448DT is distinctive. It has a characteristic oily smell and often follows a pattern depending on the root cause.

💨 Blue smoke on startup after sitting

A puff of blue smoke on cold start that clears quickly. This is classic valve stem seal wear – oil seeps past the seals while the engine is off, then burns on startup.

💨 Blue smoke after idling

Blue smoke when you pull away after a long idle, such as at traffic lights. This also points to valve stem seals – oil accumulates during the idle period.

💨 Continuous blue smoke under load

Persistent blue smoke that gets worse as you accelerate. This suggests a turbocharger oil seal failure or, more seriously, worn piston rings and cylinder bore wear.

What else to look for: Blue smoke is often accompanied by a drop in oil level between services, a sooty or oily tailpipe, and a dipstick that reads low. Why This Engine In Particular — The 448DT's Known Weaknesses

The 448DT (Ford Lion V8) is widely regarded as one of Land Rover's more durable diesel engines, but it does have a few failure points that can produce blue smoke. Understanding these helps your specialist pinpoint the cause quickly.

🔧 Valve Stem Seal Wear – The Classic Blue Smoke Cause

The 448DT's valve stem seals can harden and wear over time, especially if the engine has been subjected to high temperatures or extended service intervals. When the seals fail, oil seeps past the valve guides and enters the combustion chamber, producing the characteristic blue smoke on startup and after idling.

🔧 Turbocharger Oil Seal Failure

The 448DT's twin turbochargers rely on oil seals to keep oil out of the intake and exhaust. A failing seal can allow oil to be drawn into the intake, burning and producing blue smoke – often accompanied by a turbo whine or loss of boost.

🔧 Piston Ring Wear & Bore Scoring

While less common on the 448DT, worn piston rings or scored bores can allow oil to pass from the sump into the combustion chamber. This typically produces continuous blue smoke under load and is often accompanied by a loss of compression and power. This is the most serious cause and often requires a full engine rebuild or replacement.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

Diagnosing blue smoke on the 448DT requires a systematic approach to differentiate between valve stem seals, turbocharger seals, and piston ring wear.

1

Visual Inspection & Smoke Pattern

They'll observe the smoke colour, when it appears (startup, idle, load), and the smell – this provides the first clues to the cause.

2

Compression Test

A compression test checks cylinder sealing. Low compression on one or more cylinders indicates piston ring or valve guide wear.

3

Leak-Down Test

A leak‑down test can pinpoint whether the leakage is past the rings, valves, or head gasket – critical for accurate diagnosis.

4

Turbocharger Inspection

They'll check the turbocharger's shaft for play and inspect the intake pipes for oil residue, indicating a failed turbo seal.

5

Borescope Inspection

If ring wear is suspected, they'll use a borescope to inspect cylinder walls for scoring or glazing – a sign of advanced wear.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes blue smoke from a 448DT engine?

The most common causes are worn valve stem seals (oil seeps past the valves), a failing turbocharger oil seal (oil enters the intake), or worn piston rings (oil bypasses the rings). The 448DT's valve stem seals are the most frequent cause, especially on higher‑mileage engines.

How much does it cost to fix blue smoke on a 448DT?

Costs range from £1,500–£3,000 for valve stem seal replacement, £800–£2,000 for turbocharger seal repair, £3,000–£5,500 for piston ring replacement, and up to £6,000–£9,000 for a replacement engine.

Can I drive with a 448DT blowing blue smoke?

It depends. If it's a brief puff on startup, you may be able to drive to a garage. But if the blue smoke is continuous, gets worse under load, or is accompanied by loss of power or a check engine light, do not drive – you risk catastrophic engine damage.

Is blue smoke from a 448DT always terminal?

Not at all. Many blue smoke issues are caused by valve stem seals or turbo seals – both are repairable. The 448DT is a robust engine, and with proper diagnosis, most blue smoke issues can be fixed without replacing the whole engine.

What should I do if my 448DT is blowing blue smoke?

Check the oil level – if it's dropping significantly, that's a strong indicator. Note when the smoke occurs (startup, idle, load). Book a diagnostic with a Land Rover specialist immediately. Early diagnosis could save you thousands.

Is blue smoke covered by a Land Rover warranty?

It depends on the cause. Manufacturing defects in valve stem seals or turbo seals may be covered, but wear‑and‑tear items are typically excluded. Extended warranties may cover some causes – check your policy.
