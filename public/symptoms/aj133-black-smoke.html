What the AJ133 Specifically Does

Black smoke from an AJ133 is distinct from white (coolant) or blue (oil) smoke. It has a strong fuel smell and often follows a pattern depending on the root cause.

💨 Black smoke on acceleration

A thick cloud of black smoke when you press the accelerator, often with a noticeable loss of power. This is classic over‑fuelling – usually from a failing MAF sensor, clogged air filter, or leaking injectors.

💨 Black smoke with rough idle

Continuous black smoke even at idle, often with a lumpy, uneven idle. This suggests a severe injector leak or a MAF sensor issue.

💨 Black smoke with poor fuel economy

If black smoke is accompanied by noticeably worse fuel consumption, the engine is running too rich – a problem with the air‑fuel mixture that needs addressing.

What else to look for: Black smoke is often accompanied by other symptoms – a check engine light, reduced fuel economy, or a failed emissions test. If you see any of these, engine management light or limp mode, the issue is likely severe. For a full list of AJ133 symptoms, see our AJ133 blue smoke and AJ133 white smoke guides.

Why This Engine In Particular — The AJ133's Known Weaknesses

The AJ133 is a high‑performance V8 that relies on precise air‑fuel metering. A few well‑documented issues can cause it to run rich and produce black smoke.

🔧 Failing MAF Sensor

The Mass Air Flow (MAF) sensor measures the amount of air entering the engine. If it fails or becomes contaminated, it sends incorrect readings to the ECU, causing the engine to inject too much fuel. This is a common cause of black smoke on the AJ133.

🔧 Clogged Air Filter

A dirty air filter restricts airflow, causing an overly rich mixture. This is the cheapest and simplest fix – often a £30 air filter change can solve the problem.

🔧 Leaking Fuel Injectors

Injectors can leak or fail to close properly, allowing excess fuel into the combustion chamber. This causes black smoke and can wash the cylinder walls, leading to increased wear.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

Diagnosing black smoke on an AJ133 requires a systematic approach to differentiate between air supply issues, sensor faults, and injector problems.

1

Diagnostic Scan

They'll read fault codes and live data. Common codes include P0100 (MAF circuit), P0172 (system too rich), and P0200 (injector circuit).

2

MAF Sensor Test

Using live data, they'll check the MAF sensor readings against expected values. A contaminated or failing MAF sensor can cause over‑fuelling.

3

Air Filter Inspection

They'll inspect the air filter for clogging or contamination. A simple visual check often reveals the problem.

4

Oxygen Sensor Test

They'll check the oxygen sensor readings to see if the engine is running rich. A faulty O2 sensor can cause incorrect fuel trim adjustments.

5

Injector Leak‑Down Test

They'll perform a leak‑down test on the injectors to check for leaks or poor spray patterns, which can cause over‑fuelling.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes black smoke from an AJ133 engine?

The most common causes are a failing MAF sensor, a clogged air filter, leaking fuel injectors, or a faulty oxygen sensor. All cause an overly rich air‑fuel mixture.

How much does it cost to fix black smoke on an AJ133?

Costs range from £100–£200 for an air filter, £200–£400 for a MAF sensor, £300–£800 for oxygen sensors, £600–£1,500 for injector work, and up to £6,000–£12,000 for a replacement engine.

Can I drive with an AJ133 blowing black smoke?

It depends. A small puff on acceleration may be acceptable, but any continuous black smoke means do not drive – you risk damaging the catalytic converter and oxygen sensors.

Is black smoke from an AJ133 always serious?

Not always – a clogged air filter is a simple fix. However, black smoke from injectors or sensors indicates a problem that needs attention. Ignoring it can lead to catalytic converter failure.

What should I do if my AJ133 is blowing black smoke?

Check the air filter and note when the smoke occurs (acceleration, idle). Book a diagnostic with a Land Rover specialist. Do not ignore it – a simple air filter change today could save you from a £1,500 catalytic converter replacement.

Is this covered by a Land Rover warranty?

It depends. MAF sensor or injector failure due to a manufacturing defect may be covered, but clogged air filters and wear‑and‑tear items are typically not covered. Extended warranties vary, so check your policy.
