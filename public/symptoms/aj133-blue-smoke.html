What the AJ133 Specifically Does

Blue smoke from an AJ133 is distinct from black (fuel) or white (coolant) smoke. It has a characteristic oil smell and often follows a pattern depending on the root cause.

💨 Blue smoke on cold start

A puff of blue smoke on startup that disappears as the engine warms. This is classic worn valve stem seals – oil leaks past the seals into the combustion chamber overnight and burns off on startup.

💨 Blue smoke under acceleration

Continuous blue smoke when accelerating, especially after decelerating. This points to worn piston rings – oil is being drawn past the rings under high vacuum conditions.

💨 Blue smoke with oil consumption

If you're topping up the oil more frequently than usual, and you're seeing blue smoke, oil consumption is the issue. This can be caused by valve seals, piston rings, or a faulty PCV system.

What else to look for: A drop in oil level between services, a failed emissions test, or a catalytic converter efficiency code (P0420/P0430). If you see any of these, engine management light or excessive oil consumption, the issue is likely severe. Why This Engine In Particular — The AJ133's Known Weaknesses

The AJ133 is a high‑performance V8 that can develop oil consumption issues as it ages. Understanding these helps you and your specialist pinpoint the issue quickly.

🔧 Worn Valve Stem Seals (High‑Mileage)

As the AJ133 accumulates high mileage (100k+), the valve stem seals can harden and shrink, allowing oil to leak into the combustion chamber. This is most noticeable on cold starts, where a puff of blue smoke is visible. It's a common repair on higher‑mileage units and is labour‑intensive.

🔧 Worn Piston Rings

Over time, the piston rings can wear, allowing oil to bypass the rings and enter the combustion chamber. This typically causes blue smoke under acceleration and is often accompanied by a noticeable loss of compression and power.

🔧 Faulty PCV System

The PCV (positive crankcase ventilation) system can fail, causing excessive crankcase pressure. This can force oil past the seals and into the intake, where it's burned. A faulty PCV valve is often a cheap and simple fix.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

Diagnosing blue smoke on an AJ133 requires a systematic approach to differentiate between valve seals, piston rings, and PCV issues.

1

Cold‑Start Observation

A specialist will start the engine from cold and observe the smoke. A puff on startup often indicates valve seals.

2

Wet & Dry Compression Test

They'll perform a compression test to check cylinder health. If compression improves after adding oil to the cylinder (wet test), piston rings are worn.

3

Leak‑Down Test

A leak‑down test can pinpoint where the pressure is escaping – past the rings, valves, or head gasket.

4

PCV System Inspection

They'll inspect the PCV valve and associated hoses for blockages or damage, which can cause excessive crankcase pressure and oil consumption.

5

Borescope Inspection

A borescope via the spark plug holes can reveal cylinder wall scoring or excessive carbon buildup, indicating worn rings.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes blue smoke from an AJ133 engine?

The most common causes are worn valve stem seals (especially on high‑mileage engines), worn piston rings, or a faulty PCV system. All allow oil to enter the combustion chamber and be burned.

How much does it cost to fix blue smoke on an AJ133?

Costs range from £500–£1,500 for a PCV valve replacement, £2,500–£4,500 for valve stem seals, £5,000–£9,000 for piston rings, and up to £6,000–£12,000 for a replacement engine.

Can I drive with an AJ133 blowing blue smoke?

It depends. A small puff on startup may be acceptable, but any continuous blue smoke means do not drive – you risk damaging the catalytic converter and oxygen sensors.

Is blue smoke from an AJ133 always serious?

Not always – a faulty PCV valve is a simple fix. However, blue smoke from valve seals or piston rings indicates significant wear and requires attention. Ignoring it can lead to catalytic converter failure and increased emissions.

What should I do if my AJ133 is blowing blue smoke?

Note the conditions (cold start, acceleration, idle). Check the oil level – if it's dropping faster than usual, you have an oil consumption issue. Book a diagnostic with a Land Rover specialist. Do not ignore it – a £500 repair today could save you from a £5,000 rebuild tomorrow.

Is this covered by a Land Rover warranty?

It depends. If the vehicle is under the original manufacturer's warranty or an extended warranty, it may be covered. Wear‑and‑tear items like valve seals and piston rings are typically not covered on older vehicles. Extended warranties vary, so check your policy.
