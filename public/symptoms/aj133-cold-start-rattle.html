What the AJ133 Specifically Does

The AJ133 cold start rattle is a distinctive metallic noise that occurs only on the first start of the day, or after the engine has been sitting for several hours.

🔊 Metallic rattle on cold start

A rattling or clattering noise from the front of the engine that lasts 1–3 seconds after a cold start, then quiets. This is the timing chain tensioners struggling to build pressure, allowing the chain to slap against the guides.

🔊 Rattle that returns after short rest

If the rattle occurs even after the engine has been off for only 30–60 minutes, the tensioner wear is more advanced. The hydraulic system is no longer holding pressure effectively.

🔊 Accompanying oil pressure warning

In severe cases, the rattle may be accompanied by a flickering oil pressure light on start-up, or a delayed oil pressure gauge reading. This indicates the tensioners are not the only issue.

What else to look for: A loss of power, engine management light, or a persistent rattle that doesn't go away. If you hear any rattle, do not ignore it – it can lead to a broken timing chain and catastrophic engine damage. Why This Engine In Particular — The AJ133's Known Weaknesses

The AJ133's timing chain tensioners are a well‑documented weak point, particularly on early (pre‑2013) models. Understanding this helps you and your specialist decide on the best course of action.

🔧 Timing Chain Tensioner Wear (Pre‑2013)

Early AJ133 engines (2009–2012) use plastic tensioner guides that are prone to cracking and wear. The tensioners rely on engine oil pressure to stay tight; when the engine is off, the oil drains, and worn tensioners take a moment to build pressure again. This is the primary cause of the cold start rattle. If ignored, the chain can jump timing or snap, causing massive internal damage.

🔧 Oil Pressure Issues

On higher‑mileage engines, low oil pressure due to a failing oil pump or clogged oil pickup can exacerbate tensioner wear. This is often accompanied by a flickering oil pressure light and can cause the tensioners to struggle even more.

🔧 The Consequence of Ignoring It

If the rattle is ignored, the chain guides will eventually fail entirely, allowing the chain to slip or snap. This leads to piston-to-valve contact, scoring the bores, and often requiring a full engine replacement – a £6,000–£12,000 bill.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

Diagnosing cold start rattle on an AJ133 is straightforward but requires a careful approach to differentiate tensioner wear from other issues.

1

Cold‑Start Observation

A specialist will start the engine from cold and listen for the characteristic rattle. The duration, intensity, and conditions (warm vs cold) give crucial clues.

2

Oil Pressure Test

They'll check the oil pressure at idle and at speed. Low pressure can indicate a failing oil pump or clogged pickup, which can contribute to tensioner wear.

3

Borescope Inspection

Using a borescope via the oil filler or a spark plug hole, they can sometimes visually check for chain slack or guide damage without major disassembly.

4

Oil Analysis

An oil analysis can detect the presence of plastic or metal particles from worn guides or chain, confirming internal wear.

5

Timing Chain Inspection

If tensioner wear is suspected, the only definitive way to confirm is to partially disassemble the timing cover and inspect the guides and chain tension.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes cold start rattle on an AJ133 engine?

The most common cause is worn timing chain tensioners, especially on pre‑2013 models. The tensioners lose oil pressure when the engine is off and take a moment to build it back up, allowing the chain to slap against the guides.

How much does it cost to fix a rattling AJ133 on startup?

Costs range from £2,500–£4,500 for tensioner and guide replacement, £3,500–£6,000 for a full timing chain set, and up to £6,000–£12,000 for a replacement engine if it fails.

Can I drive with an AJ133 that rattles on cold start?

If it's a brief rattle that disappears within 1–3 seconds, you may be able to drive to a garage. However, any rattle that persists or occurs on warm starts means do not drive – you risk chain failure and catastrophic engine damage.

Is a cold start rattle on an AJ133 always serious?

Not always – a short rattle on a pre‑2013 engine is a known wear indicator. However, it should be inspected immediately. Ignoring it can lead to a broken timing chain and a seized engine.

What should I do if my AJ133 rattles on cold start?

Note the duration and conditions. Check the oil level and condition. If the rattle is persistent, have the vehicle recovered to a Land Rover specialist for diagnosis. Do not ignore it – a £3,500 repair today could save you from a £12,000 replacement.

Is this covered by a Land Rover warranty?

It depends. If the vehicle is under the original manufacturer's warranty or an extended warranty, it may be covered. Wear‑and‑tear items like tensioners are typically not covered on older vehicles. Extended warranties vary, so check your policy.
