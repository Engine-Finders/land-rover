This is almost always caused by a leak from the plastic cross-over pipes at the front of the engine, a failed water pump, or a weeping radiator. What the AJ133 Specifically Does

Coolant loss on the AJ133 is often subtle. You may not see a puddle, but the engine will give you clues. Here is what to watch for.

🍃 Sweet smell from the bonnet

A distinct sweet, almost maple-syrup scent when the engine is hot – especially after a drive. This is coolant evaporating on a hot engine block, often from a slow leak at the cross-over pipes.

📉 Dropping coolant level with no puddle

You check the expansion tank and it's low, but there is no wet patch on the driveway. The leak is likely internal to the engine bay, evaporating before it hits the ground.

🌡️ Temperature gauge flickering

The temperature gauge may fluctuate, especially under load, as air pockets form in the cooling system due to low coolant.

What else to look for: If coolant loss is accompanied by white smoke from the exhaust, oil in the coolant, or a misfire, the head gasket may already be compromised. See our AJ133 white smoke and AJ133 oil in coolant guides for more.

Why This Engine In Particular — The AJ133's Cooling System Weaknesses

The AJ133's cooling system has two well-documented weak points that cause slow, invisible coolant loss. Knowing them helps you and your specialist pinpoint the issue.

🔧 Plastic Cross-Over Pipe Cracks

The AJ133 uses plastic coolant pipes at the front of the engine, near the thermostat housing. Over time, heat cycling makes them brittle, causing hairline cracks. Coolant leaks out, hits the hot engine and evaporates – so you never see a puddle. This is the most common cause of unexplained coolant loss on the AJ133.

🔧 Water Pump Weep Hole Leak

The water pump has a weep hole that allows coolant to escape when the internal seal fails. This is designed to warn of impending pump failure. The coolant drips onto the engine block and evaporates, so it rarely leaves a puddle.

🔧 Radiator or Heater Matrix Leaks

Less common, but a small leak in the radiator core or heater matrix can also cause slow coolant loss. These are harder to spot and often require a pressure test to confirm.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

Diagnosing coolant loss on an AJ133 requires a systematic approach to find the invisible leak. Here is the typical process.

1

Cooling System Pressure Test

A pressure test is the definitive test. The system is pressurised to 15-18 psi and held. Any drop indicates a leak, and the specialist can trace it by sound or UV dye.

2

UV Dye Addition

UV dye is added to the coolant. After driving, the specialist uses a UV light to find the dye traces, pinpointing the leak source – often the cross-over pipes.

3

Visual Inspection of Pipes and Pump

Using a mirror and torch, they inspect the cross-over pipes and water pump for signs of dried coolant residue (white/blue stains).

4

Coolant Condition Test

Testing the coolant for acidity and contamination can indicate if the head gasket is failing or the cooling system is being neglected.

5

Block Test (if head gasket suspected)

If there is also white smoke or oil in coolant, a block test is performed to detect combustion gases – a sign of a blown head gasket.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes an AJ133 to lose coolant with no visible leak?

The most common cause is a cracked plastic cross-over pipe at the front of the engine. The coolant leaks onto the hot engine block and evaporates, so you never see a puddle. A failing water pump or a weeping radiator can also cause similar symptoms.

How much does it cost to fix coolant loss on an AJ133?

Costs range from £400–£800 for cross-over pipe replacement, £600–£1,200 for a water pump, up to £5,500–£9,000 for a replacement engine if the leak has caused overheating and head gasket failure.

Can I drive with an AJ133 that is losing coolant but not overheating?

You can drive a short distance to a specialist, but you must top up the coolant regularly and monitor the temperature gauge closely. Any sign of overheating means stop immediately. Driving with low coolant risks catastrophic engine damage.

Is coolant loss on an AJ133 always the cross-over pipe?

Not always, but it is the most common culprit. The water pump, radiator, and heater matrix can also leak. A pressure test with UV dye is the most reliable way to pinpoint the source.

What should I do if my AJ133 is losing coolant?

Check the coolant level when the engine is cold and top up with the correct spec (see our coolant specification). If you smell coolant or notice a drop, book a pressure test with a Land Rover specialist immediately. Do not ignore it.

Is coolant loss covered by a Land Rover warranty?

It depends. If the vehicle is within the manufacturer's warranty period and the failure is due to a defect (e.g., a known cross-over pipe issue), it may be covered. However, wear-and-tear items like plastic pipes are often excluded, especially on older vehicles. Always check your warranty terms.
