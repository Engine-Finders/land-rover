What the AJ133 Specifically Does

The AJ133 is a 5.0‑litre V8 supercharged engine. Knocking noises from it can vary in character, and the pattern often points to the root cause.

🔊 Metallic rattle on cold start

A rattling noise that lasts 1–3 seconds on a cold start, then quiets down. This is classic timing chain tensioner wear – the hydraulic tensioners lose pressure overnight and the chain slaps against the guides until oil pressure builds.

🔊 Consistent knock under load

A deeper, rhythmic knock that increases with engine speed and becomes louder under acceleration. This often points to connecting rod or main bearing wear – a catastrophic failure if ignored.

🔊 Supercharger rattle at idle

A rattling or clattering noise from the top of the engine, especially at idle. This is often the supercharger coupler – a plastic or metal component that wears out, allowing the rotors to knock together.

What else to look for: Loss of power, engine management light, or low oil pressure warnings. If you hear any knocking, do not continue driving – it could lead to a broken timing chain or a seized engine. Why This Engine In Particular — The AJ133's Known Weaknesses

The AJ133 is a powerful and refined V8, but it has a few well‑documented failure points that can cause knocking. Understanding these helps you and your specialist pinpoint the issue.

🔧 Timing Chain Tensioner Wear (Pre‑2013)

Early AJ133 engines (2009–2012) are prone to timing chain tensioner failure. The plastic tensioner guides can break, allowing the chain to slacken and rattle. If ignored, the chain can jump timing or snap, causing catastrophic valve and piston damage. This is the most common cause of cold‑start rattle on the AJ133.

🔧 Supercharger Coupler Wear

The supercharger coupler (a flexible joint between the pulley and the rotor pack) can wear out, leading to a rattle at idle. This is less serious than chain or bearing issues, but it still requires attention to prevent further damage to the supercharger.

🔧 Bottom‑End Bearing Failure

If the knocking is a deep, metallic thump that worsens with load, it may be connecting rod or main bearing failure. This is often caused by oil starvation or prolonged high‑load use. It usually requires a full engine rebuild or replacement.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

Diagnosing knocking on an AJ133 requires careful listening, inspection, and sometimes oil analysis to differentiate between chain, coupler, and bearing issues.

1

Stethoscope Listening

A mechanic will use a stethoscope to pinpoint the source – timing covers, supercharger, or bottom end.

2

Cold‑Start Observation

They'll start the engine from cold and listen for the characteristic rattle. The duration and intensity give clues.

3

Oil Pressure Test

Low oil pressure at idle can indicate bearing wear. A mechanical oil pressure gauge is used to check.

4

Oil Analysis

A lab analysis can detect bearing material (copper, lead) in the oil, confirming bottom‑end wear.

5

Borescope Inspection

If timing chain failure is suspected, a borescope through the oil filler or spark plug holes can sometimes reveal chain slack or guide damage.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes knocking noise on an AJ133 engine?

The most common causes are timing chain tensioner wear (especially on pre‑2013 models), a worn supercharger coupler, or bottom‑end bearing failure. The sound and conditions help narrow it down.

How much does it cost to fix a knocking AJ133?

Costs range from £500–£1,000 for a supercharger coupler, £2,500–£4,500 for timing chain tensioners, and up to £6,000–£12,000 for a replacement engine.

Can I drive with an AJ133 that is knocking?

It depends. A brief rattle on cold start that disappears may be acceptable, but any persistent knock or noise under load means do not drive – you risk catastrophic engine failure.

Is a knocking AJ133 always serious?

Not always – a rattling supercharger coupler is less serious. However, any knocking from the bottom end or timing area should be inspected immediately. Ignoring it can lead to a broken chain or seized engine.

What should I do if my AJ133 is knocking?

Note the conditions (cold start, idle, load). Check the oil level and condition. If the knock is persistent, have the vehicle recovered to a Land Rover specialist for diagnosis. Do not drive it.

Is a knocking AJ133 covered by warranty?

It depends. If the vehicle is under the original manufacturer's warranty, it may be covered. Wear‑and‑tear items like tensioners are typically not covered on older vehicles. Extended warranties vary, so check your policy.
