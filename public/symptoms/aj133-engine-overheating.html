AJ133 Engine Overheating — Causes, Diagnosis & Repair Costs

The AJ133 (5.0-litre V8) is a robust engine, but it has a well-documented weakness: its cooling system. Overheating on the AJ133 is almost always caused by a failure in the cooling system – most commonly the water pump, the plastic cross-over pipes, or the radiator fan module. This guide explains why the AJ133 overheats, what it will cost to fix in the UK in 2026, and when an overheating engine means you should consider scrapping the vehicle.

The Short Answer

An overheating AJ133 is a serious symptom that needs immediate attention. The most common causes are a failed water pump (the impeller can break or the bearing can seize), a leaking plastic cross-over pipe at the front of the engine (a notorious weak point on early models), or a failed radiator fan module that stops air flowing through the radiator at low speeds. Repair costs range from £600–£1,200 for a water pump and £400–£800 for a coolant pipe, up to £3,000–£6,000+ for a replacement engine if a blown head gasket or warped cylinder head occurs.

What the AJ133 Specifically Does

The AJ133 is a high-performance, high-revving V8. Overheating on this engine often appears suddenly and can escalate quickly. Here is what you will notice.

🌡️ Temperature gauge rising rapidly

The temperature gauge climbs quickly into the red, often within a few miles of a cold start. The engine may feel rough or hesitant.

💧 Coolant loss with no visible leak

You may smell coolant but cannot see a leak on the ground. The coolant is often leaking from the plastic cross-over pipes at the front of the engine, where it evaporates on the hot engine block.

💨 Steam from the bonnet

Steam rising from the bonnet, often accompanied by a sweet smell of coolant. This is a strong indicator of a sudden leak or rapid overheating.

What else to look for: An overheating AJ133 may also trigger the engine management light, a coolant temperature warning, or a loss of power as the ECU limits performance to protect the engine.

Why This Engine In Particular — The AJ133's Cooling System Weaknesses

The AJ133 is a powerful 5.0-litre V8 that generates a lot of heat. Its cooling system has three main weak points that are the cause of almost all overheating incidents.

🔧 Water Pump Failure (Most Common)

The AJ133's water pump is a common failure point. The plastic impeller can break apart (often due to age and heat cycling) or the bearing can seize. When the pump fails, it stops circulating coolant through the engine, causing rapid overheating.

🔧 Plastic Cross-Over Pipe Leaks

The AJ133 uses plastic cooling system pipes at the front of the engine. Over time, these become brittle and crack, often causing a slow leak that is hard to spot. Loss of coolant inevitably leads to overheating.

🔧 Radiator Fan Module Failure

The electric fan module on the radiator can fail. At low speeds or in traffic, there is not enough airflow through the radiator to cool the engine, and overheating is the result.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

Diagnosing overheating on an AJ133 requires a methodical approach to avoid misdiagnosis. Here is the typical process.

1

Cooling System Pressure Test

A pressure test of the cooling system will reveal any leaks, including the often-invisible ones from the cross-over pipes.

2

Thermostat Function Test

They will check if the thermostat is opening and closing correctly. A stuck thermostat can cause overheating.

3

Water Pump Inspection

The water pump is visually inspected for leaks and a seized bearing. The impeller can sometimes be inspected through the coolant fill port.

4

Coolant Condition Test

Testing the coolant for acidity and contamination can indicate if a head gasket is failing or the cooling system is being neglected.

5

Block Test (Combustion Leak)

If a head gasket failure is suspected, a block test is performed to detect combustion gases in the coolant.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes an AJ133 engine to overheat?

The most common causes are a failed water pump (impeller or bearing), a leaking plastic cross-over pipe, or a faulty radiator fan module. A stuck thermostat or air in the system can also cause overheating.

How much does it cost to fix an overheating AJ133?

Costs range from £600–£1,200 for a water pump, £400–£800 for a cross-over pipe, £400–£800 for a fan module, and up to £5,500–£9,000 for a replacement engine if the overheating has caused severe damage.

Can I drive with an AJ133 that is overheating?

No. If your AJ133 is overheating, pull over and turn the engine off immediately. Driving a 5.0-litre V8 with no coolant can cause catastrophic engine failure in minutes.

Is an overheating AJ133 always serious?

Yes. Overheating is a sign of a serious cooling system failure. Ignoring it can lead to a blown head gasket, a warped cylinder head, or even a seized engine. It should be treated as an immediate priority.

What should I do if my AJ133 is overheating?

Pull over safely, turn off the engine, and call a specialist. Do not open the coolant reservoir while the engine is hot. Once the engine has cooled, have the vehicle recovered to a Land Rover specialist for diagnosis.

Is overheating covered by a Land Rover warranty?

It depends. A manufacturing defect in a water pump or fan module may be covered under a Land Rover extended warranty, but wear-and-tear items like plastic pipes are often excluded. Damage caused by neglect (such as continuing to drive while overheating) is never covered.
