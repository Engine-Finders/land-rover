AJ133 Oil in Coolant / Mayonnaise Under Oil Cap — Causes, Diagnosis & Repair Costs

Finding oil in your AJ133's coolant or a mayonnaise-like sludge under the oil cap is a serious warning sign. It almost always indicates a failure of the head gasket or the engine oil cooler – both of which allow engine oil and coolant to mix. This guide explains why this happens on the 5.0-litre V8, what it costs to fix in the UK in 2026, and how to decide whether to repair or walk away.

The Short Answer

Oil in the coolant or mayonnaise under the oil cap on an AJ133 is a sign of a breached barrier between oil and coolant systems – most commonly a failed head gasket or a leaking oil cooler. Left unchecked, this can lead to overheating, bearing damage, and complete engine failure. Repair costs range from £2,000–£4,000 for a head gasket replacement (one bank) to £5,500–£9,000 for a reconditioned engine if the engine has been damaged by overheating. The oil cooler itself is a cheaper fix, typically £800–£1,500.

What the AJ133 Specifically Does

Oil in the coolant or mayonnaise under the oil cap is a visual and olfactory warning. Here is what to look for on your AJ133.

🧴 Mayonnaise under the oil cap

A thick, yellowish-white paste on the underside of the oil filler cap is the classic sign of oil and water mixing. On the AJ133, this is often accompanied by a sweet smell of coolant in the oil.

💧 Milky residue in the expansion tank

The coolant in the reservoir may appear milky or have an oily film on top. This is a clear sign that oil is entering the cooling system.

🔥 Sweet exhaust smell or white smoke

If the head gasket is the cause, coolant may be burning in the combustion chamber, producing a sweet smell and white smoke from the exhaust. See our AJ133 white smoke guide for more.

What else to look for: Oil in coolant often goes hand-in-hand with overheating or coolant loss – because the underlying cause is often a cooling system failure that led to head gasket damage. If you see any of these, do not drive.

Why This Engine In Particular — The AJ133's Vulnerabilities

The AJ133 is a high-performance V8 that generates significant heat. Oil in the coolant almost always traces back to one of two failure points.

🔧 Head Gasket Failure (Most Common)

The AJ133's head gasket can fail, particularly if the engine has been overheated. Overheating is often caused by a coolant leak from the plastic cross-over pipes or a failed water pump – both common weaknesses on this engine. When the head gasket fails, it allows oil and coolant to mix, and may also allow combustion gases into the cooling system.

🔧 Oil Cooler Seal Failure

The AJ133 uses an oil cooler mounted on the engine block. The seals inside the cooler can fail, allowing oil to leak into the coolant system. This is a less catastrophic failure than a head gasket, but still requires attention. The oil cooler itself is a relatively inexpensive part, but access can be labour-intensive.

🔧 Cylinder Liner Porousity (Rare)

In very rare cases, the cylinder liners can develop porosity, allowing coolant to seep into the oil. This is more common on earlier production engines and is difficult to diagnose without specialist equipment.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

Diagnosing oil in coolant on an AJ133 requires a methodical approach to differentiate between head gasket and oil cooler failure.

1

Hydrocarbon (Block) Test

A chemical test of the coolant for combustion gases – if positive, the head gasket is compromised. This is a quick and definitive test.

2

Cooling System Pressure Test

Pressurising the cooling system can reveal leaks, but also helps identify if pressure is building rapidly – a sign of combustion gas entering the coolant.

3

Oil Cooler Leak Test

If the block test is negative, the oil cooler is the next suspect. Specialists can isolate the oil cooler and pressure-test it separately.

4

Visual Inspection of Oil and Coolant

The condition of the oil (milky) and coolant (oily) provides strong clues. A mayonnaise-like sludge on the oil cap is a classic indicator.

5

Cylinder Compression / Leak-Down Test

If head gasket failure is suspected, a leak-down test can pinpoint which cylinder is affected and the severity of the leak.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes oil in the coolant on an AJ133?

The most common causes are a failed head gasket (often after an overheating event) or a leaking oil cooler. Both allow oil and coolant to mix, resulting in the characteristic mayonnaise-like sludge.

How much does it cost to fix oil in coolant on an AJ133?

Costs range from £800–£1,500 for an oil cooler, £2,000–£4,000 for a single head gasket, and up to £5,500–£9,000 for a replacement engine if the damage is extensive.

Can I drive with oil in my AJ133's coolant?

No. Driving with oil in the coolant can cause the oil to lose its lubricating properties, leading to rapid engine wear and bearing failure. Stop driving and call a specialist.

Is mayonnaise under the oil cap always a head gasket failure?

Not always. Short journeys can cause condensation that creates a harmless mayonnaise-like sludge. However, on the AJ133, if it is accompanied by coolant loss, overheating, or oil in the coolant, it is almost certainly a head gasket or oil cooler issue.

What should I do if I find oil in my AJ133's coolant?

Do not drive. Check the oil level and coolant level (when cold). Book a diagnostic with a Land Rover specialist immediately. They will perform a hydrocarbon test to check for exhaust gases in the coolant.

Is oil in coolant covered by a Land Rover warranty?

It depends. If the vehicle is within the manufacturer's warranty period and the failure is due to a manufacturing defect (e.g., a known head gasket issue), it may be covered. However, wear-and-tear items and damage caused by neglect (e.g., continuing to drive while overheating) are typically excluded.
