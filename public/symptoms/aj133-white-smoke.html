What the AJ133 Specifically Does

White smoke from an AJ133 is distinct from blue (oil) or black (fuel) smoke. It has a sweet, sickly smell and often follows a pattern depending on the root cause.

💨 White smoke on cold start

A cloud of white smoke on startup that disappears as the engine warms. This is classic head gasket or porous head – coolant leaks into the combustion chamber overnight and burns off on startup.

💨 White smoke under load

Continuous white smoke when accelerating or under heavy load. This points to a significant coolant leak – the pressure in the cooling system forces coolant past the damaged seal.

💨 White smoke with coolant loss

If you're topping up the coolant regularly and seeing white smoke, coolant is being consumed. This is almost always a head gasket, porous head, or intercooler leak.

What else to look for: A sweet smell from the exhaust, a drop in coolant level without a visible leak, or a failed emissions test. If you see any of these, engine management light or coolant loss with no leak, the issue is likely severe. Why This Engine In Particular — The AJ133's Known Weaknesses

The AJ133 has a few well‑documented failure points that can cause white smoke. Understanding these helps you and your specialist pinpoint the issue quickly.

🔧 Head Gasket Failure

The head gasket can fail between a coolant passage and the combustion chamber, allowing coolant to enter. This is often the result of overheating, which itself can be caused by a previous coolant leak. It's a common repair on higher‑mileage AJ133s.

🔧 Porous or Cracked Cylinder Head

The AJ133's cylinder heads can develop micro‑cracks (porosity) over time, especially if the engine has been overheated. This allows coolant to seep into the combustion chamber, causing white smoke. This is a more serious and expensive repair.

🔧 Supercharger Intercooler Leak

The AJ133's supercharger has an integrated intercooler that can develop internal leaks. This allows coolant to be drawn into the intake and burned, causing white smoke. This is often overlooked but is a cheaper fix than a head gasket.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

Diagnosing white smoke on an AJ133 requires a systematic approach to differentiate between head gasket, porous head, and intercooler issues.

1

Cooling System Pressure Test

The system is pressurised with a hand pump. A drop in pressure indicates a leak, and a borescope or UV dye can pinpoint it.

2

Exhaust Gas Test (Block Test)

A chemical tester is placed over the coolant expansion tank. If it changes colour, it confirms the presence of combustion gases, pointing to a head gasket or porous head.

3

Leak‑Down Test

A leak‑down test can pinpoint where the pressure is escaping – past the rings, valves, or head gasket.

4

Borescope Inspection

A borescope via the spark plug holes can reveal cylinder wall scoring or excessive carbon buildup, indicating worn rings or coolant ingress.

5

Intercooler Leak Test

The supercharger intercooler can be pressure‑tested to check for internal leaks that would allow coolant into the intake.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes white smoke from an AJ133 engine?

The most common causes are a failed head gasket, a porous or cracked cylinder head, or a leaking supercharger intercooler. All allow coolant to enter the combustion chamber and be burned.

How much does it cost to fix white smoke on an AJ133?

Costs range from £800–£1,500 for an intercooler repair, £2,500–£4,500 for a head gasket, £4,000–£7,000 for a cylinder head replacement, and up to £6,000–£12,000 for a replacement engine.

Can I drive with an AJ133 blowing white smoke?

No. Do not drive. White smoke indicates coolant is entering the combustion chamber, which can lead to overheating and catastrophic engine damage. Have the vehicle recovered to a specialist.

Is white smoke from an AJ133 always serious?

Yes. White smoke is almost always caused by a coolant leak into the combustion chamber, which is a serious issue that requires immediate attention. Ignoring it can destroy the engine.

What should I do if my AJ133 is blowing white smoke?

Do not drive. Check the coolant level – if it's dropping, you have a leak. Have the vehicle recovered to a Land Rover specialist for diagnosis. A pressure test and block test will confirm the cause.

Is this covered by a Land Rover warranty?

It depends. If the vehicle is under the original manufacturer's warranty or an extended warranty, it may be covered. Wear‑and‑tear items like head gaskets are typically not covered on older vehicles. Extended warranties vary, so check your policy.
