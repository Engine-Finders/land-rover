Land Rover Excessive Oil Consumption

Your Land Rover shouldn't need topping up between services. If it does, something is allowing oil to escape — and the most common cause on this brand is not what you'd expect. Here's how to narrow it down, what it will cost, and when to act.

Oil consumption on a Land Rover is often a symptom of worn valve stem seals, a blocked PCV system, or a leaking turbocharger — but on some engines, it is an early warning of much more serious failure. What It Looks, Sounds and Smells Like

Owners describe it in a handful of distinct ways. The most common is blue smoke from the exhaust — particularly on cold start, under hard acceleration, or when lifting off the throttle after a period of high revs. Others notice the oil level dropping between services, requiring a litre every 1,000 miles or less. Some report a burnt oil smell inside the cabin or near the engine bay. In many cases, the first sign is not smoke at all, but a low oil warning light that appears long before the next scheduled service.

These experiences are not the same across all Land Rover models. On the 306DT (TDV6/SDV6), oil consumption may be the only warning you get before a catastrophic crankshaft bearing failure. On the 204DTD Ingenium diesel, it often points to a different problem entirely.

Blue Smoke

Consistent blue smoke, especially on startup or acceleration, usually means oil is being burned in the combustion chamber.

Frequent Top-Ups

Needing more than 1 litre of oil between services, or 0.5 litres per 1,000 miles, is a clear sign of abnormal consumption.

Oil Smell

A persistent burnt oil smell inside the cabin or around the engine bay indicates a leak or excessive blow-by.

How to Narrow It Down Yourself (Safe Checks Only)

You can do several checks without lifting the car or touching anything hot. First, check the oil level when the engine is cold and level — note the exact reading and re-check after a few hundred miles. This gives you the consumption rate. Second, look at the exhaust smoke in daylight: blue smoke is oil, black smoke is fuel (see our black smoke guide), white smoke is coolant. Third, remove the oil filler cap while the engine is idling (warm) — if there is an excessive amount of visible blow-by (fumes or pressure puffing out), the PCV system is likely blocked or the rings are worn.

When It's Serious vs When It Can Wait

Serious — Act Immediately

  • Oil warning light appears while driving
  • Blue smoke that does not clear after a few seconds
  • Oil consumption exceeding 1 litre per 1,000 miles
  • Knocking noise accompanies oil consumption (especially on 306DT)

Can Wait — But Monitor Closely

  • Minor oil consumption (0.5 litres per 1,000 miles) with no smoke
  • Oil smell only when stationary
  • Consumption is consistent and stable over time

If you fall into the "Serious" column, stop driving and contact a specialist. The cost of a tow is far less than the cost of a new engine.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my Land Rover burning oil but not smoking?

It's possible. A PCV system that is slightly blocked can cause oil consumption without producing visible smoke. The oil is being consumed through the intake system and burned in small quantities. This is still a problem that should be investigated.

How much oil consumption is normal for a Land Rover?

Most Land Rover engines should consume less than 0.5 litres per 1,000 miles. Any consumption above 1 litre per 1,000 miles is abnormal and requires investigation. Some petrol V8s (e.g. AJ133) can consume more, but this is still a sign of wear rather than a design feature.

Can a blocked DPF cause oil consumption?

Indirectly, yes. A blocked DPF increases engine back pressure, which can force oil past seals and into the intake system. This is a secondary effect, not a primary cause. If your DPF is blocked, fix that first and then re-check oil consumption.

Is it worth replacing the engine if my Land Rover is burning oil?

This depends entirely on the engine and the vehicle's value. Apply the 60% threshold: if the cost of a reconditioned engine (supply and fit) exceeds 60% of the vehicle's current market value, scrap may be the better financial decision. We have a repair vs replace calculator that does this for you.

What is the most common cause of oil consumption on the 306DT?

On the 306DT (TDV6/SDV6), a failing turbocharger oil seal is the most common cause. However, any oil consumption on this engine should be treated as a potential early warning of crankshaft bearing failure. Read the 306DT bearing failure page for full details.
