The Short Answer

TD5 coolant loss with no visible leak is almost always caused by one of three things: a faulty header tank cap venting under pressure, a tiny weep from a hose or component that only leaks when hot, or an internal failure — head gasket or cracked head — allowing coolant into the combustion chamber or oil. The loss rate varies from a pint every 1,000 miles to a litre every 30 miles. Diagnosis starts with a pressure test, followed by a careful inspection of the usual suspects: the header tank cap, the top hose bleed screw, the water pump weep hole, the fuel cooler O-rings, and the metal pipe at the back of the engine[reference:0][reference:1][reference:2]. Repair costs range from £20 for a new cap to £1,500–£3,000 for a head gasket and up to £4,500+ for a replacement engine.

  • Most common cause (TD5): Header tank cap failing to hold pressure — vents coolant while driving[reference:3]
  • Typical diagnostic cost: £80–£150 [EM-QUOTE]
  • Key indicator: Coolant level drops but no puddles — often only leaks when hot and pressurised[reference:4]
  • Verdict: Immediate — any coolant loss on a TD5 needs investigating before it causes an overheat and cracks the head

If your TD5 is losing coolant and you can't see where, don't ignore it. A £20 cap today could save you from a £3,000 head gasket job tomorrow.

What the TD5 Specifically Does

TD5 coolant loss with no visible leak follows patterns that point you toward the cause. Here's what owners typically notice.

💧 Slow loss — a pint every 200–300 miles

The coolant level drops slowly over a few hundred miles. No puddles, no overheating. Often a weeping hose or a failing header tank cap that vents pressure[reference:5][reference:6].

💧 Rapid loss — a litre every 30–50 miles

The expansion tank empties in a single journey. Often accompanied by white smoke on startup or hard hoses even when cold. This points to a head gasket failure or cracked head[reference:7][reference:8].

💧 Loss with gurgling from the heater

You hear gurgling behind the dash on startup. The heater blows cold. This is air in the system — often from an airlock or a leak that lets air in as the engine cools[reference:9].

What else to look for: The TD5's cooling system holds about 10 litres[reference:10], so a litre loss is 10% of the system. Check the oil cap for mayo, the exhaust for white smoke, and the header tank for bubbles with the engine running. Why This Engine In Particular — The TD5's Known Weaknesses

The TD5 is a robust engine, but its cooling system has a few well-documented failure points. Understanding these helps you and your specialist pinpoint the issue.

🔧 Header Tank Cap — The £20 Fix

The TD5's header tank cap is a common failure point. If it doesn't hold pressure (typically 15 PSI / 1 bar), coolant will vent out of the overflow pipe while you're driving[reference:11]. You won't see a puddle because it evaporates or blows away. A paper towel zip-tied over the vent line can confirm this[reference:12]. This is the cheapest and most common fix.

🔧 Head Gasket Failure — The Classic

TD5 head gaskets typically fail between 70,000 and 90,000 miles[reference:13]. The failure can be subtle — a very slight leak that allows coolant into the combustion chamber or exhaust ports[reference:14]. This produces white smoke on startup (steam) and can pressurise the cooling system[reference:15]. A chemical block test (sniff test) often passes on a TD5 because the crack may be between the water jacket and exhaust ports, not between the cylinder and coolant[reference:16]. Early TD5s had plastic cylinder head dowels that can shear, allowing the head to move and the gasket to fail[reference:17] — always replace with steel pegs when doing the head.

🔧 Cracked Cylinder Head — The Expensive One

TD5 heads can crack between the water jacket and exhaust ports[reference:18]. This allows coolant into the exhaust without any oil contamination. The chemical block test won't catch it because no combustion gases enter the coolant. Symptoms include white smoke on startup, coolant loss, and sometimes hard hoses[reference:19].

🔧 The Usual Suspects — Hoses, Pipes and Seals

The TD5 has a long list of external leak points: the top hose elbow (the aluminium pipe behind the fan — common failure)[reference:20], the heater pipe across the back of the engine[reference:21], the water pump weep hole (seals failing)[reference:22], the fuel cooler O-rings on the side of the inlet manifold[reference:23][reference:24], the metal coolant pipe along the back of the engine (corrodes from the inside)[reference:25], the oil cooler housing on the other side of the block[reference:26], and the core plug under the exhaust manifold[reference:27]. Many of these only leak when hot and pressurised[reference:28].

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

Diagnosing TD5 coolant loss with no visible leak requires a systematic approach. Here's the step-by-step process a specialist will follow.

1

Pressure Test

Pump the cooling system to 15 PSI (1–1.5 bar) and wait 30 minutes[reference:32]. This forces leaks to appear. Check the header tank, all hoses, the water pump weep hole, and the heater matrix.

2

Header Tank Cap Test

Test the cap for pressure hold. A wimpy cap will vent while driving[reference:33]. A paper towel zip-tied over the vent line can confirm this is happening while you drive[reference:34].

3

Bleed the System

Open the bleed screw on the top hose with the engine running and heater on hot[reference:35]. Air in the system can cause gurgling and erratic coolant loss[reference:36].

4

Chemical Block Test

A sniff test checks for combustion gases in the coolant[reference:37]. Note: This often passes on a TD5 even with a failed head gasket or cracked head[reference:38]. A pass does not rule out internal failure.

5

Leak Detection Dye

Add UV dye to the coolant and run the engine[reference:39]. The dye will show at the leak point under UV light. Works for both external leaks and internal seepage into the combustion chamber.

6

Borescope Inspection

If internal failure is suspected, a borescope down the glow plug holes can reveal coolant in the cylinders or steam-cleaned pistons.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes a TD5 to lose coolant with no visible leak?

The most common causes are a faulty header tank cap that vents pressure[reference:43], a tiny weep from a hose or component that only leaks when hot[reference:44], or an internal failure — head gasket or cracked head — allowing coolant into the combustion chamber or exhaust[reference:45].

How much does it cost to fix TD5 coolant loss?

Costs range from £20–£40 for a new cap, £80–£250 for a hose, £200–£400 for a water pump, £1,500–£3,000 for a head gasket, £2,500–£4,000 for a cracked head, and up to £4,500–£6,500 for a replacement engine.

Can I drive a TD5 that's losing coolant?

It depends. If it's a slow loss (a pint every 500 miles), you can drive to a garage. If it's losing a litre every 30 miles, or if you see white smoke or the temperature gauge rises, stop immediately — continued driving will crack the head.

Will a block test always show a TD5 head gasket failure?

No. TD5 heads can crack between the water jacket and exhaust ports, allowing coolant into the exhaust without combustion gases entering the coolant[reference:46]. The block test (sniff test) often passes even with a failed gasket or cracked head[reference:47].

What should I check first on a TD5 with coolant loss?

Check the header tank cap — it's the cheapest and most common fix[reference:48]. Then check the top hose bleed screw is tight[reference:49]. Then do a pressure test[reference:50]. If none of these find the leak, suspect head gasket or cracked head.

What coolant should I use in a TD5?

Use OAT (Organic Acid Technology) coolant — typically pink or red. Never mix with blue (IAT) coolant. The TD5 system holds about 10 litres[reference:51]. A 50/50 mix of coolant and distilled water is standard.
