The Short Answer

A knocking noise from a TD5 engine is almost always caused by a fuel system issue (injector or timing) or a bottom-end mechanical failure. The most common causes are a failed injector washer (allowing combustion gas to leak), a worn injector (causing a loud metallic knock), or a failing big-end bearing (deep, rhythmic knock that worsens under load). Repair costs range from £200–£500 for an injector washer change to £800–£2,000 for injector replacement, and up to £4,000–£7,500 for a reconditioned engine if the bottom end has failed.

  • Most common cause (TD5): Injector washer failure – leading to gas leakage and a knocking noise

What the TD5 Specifically Does

The TD5 is a 5-cylinder diesel with a distinct, agricultural sound. A knocking noise on a TD5 is not a normal part of its character. Here is what to listen for.

🔊 Metallic tapping at idle

A sharp, metallic tapping noise at idle that often fades slightly as the engine warms up. This is often caused by a worn injector or a failed injector washer.

🔊 Deep, rhythmic knock under load

A heavy, dull knock that becomes louder and more pronounced when accelerating or climbing a hill. This is a classic symptom of a failing big-end bearing – a serious mechanical issue.

🔊 Knocking with a loss of power

If the knocking is accompanied by a loss of power, rough running, or black smoke from the exhaust, the problem is likely injector-related. A faulty injector can dump too much fuel into a cylinder, causing a knock and poor combustion.

What else to look for: TD5 knocking is often accompanied by other symptoms such as a engine management light, excessive black smoke, or a sudden increase in oil consumption. See our TD5 black smoke and TD5 white smoke guides for more clues.

Why This Engine In Particular — The TD5's Known Weaknesses

The TD5 is a robust engine, but it has a few well-known failure points that manifest as a knocking noise. Knowing these helps you and your specialist pinpoint the issue.

🔧 Injector Washer Failure

The TD5 uses copper washers to seal the injectors in the cylinder head. Over time, these washers can fail, allowing combustion gases to leak past the injector. This causes a metallic tapping noise and can lead to carbon buildup around the injector, making it difficult to remove. This is the most common cause of TD5 knocking.

🔧 Big-End Bearing Failure (Catastrophic)

The TD5's big-end bearings can fail, particularly on high-mileage engines or those with poor oil maintenance. A deep, rhythmic knock that worsens under load is the classic symptom. This is a catastrophic failure that usually requires a full engine rebuild or replacement.

🔧 Oil Pump Bolt Failure

On early TD5 engines (pre-2002), the oil pump bolt can loosen or sheer off, causing a loss of oil pressure. This can rapidly lead to big-end bearing failure and a loud knocking noise before the engine seizes. This is a known design flaw that has been fixed on later engines.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

Diagnosing a TD5 knock requires a systematic approach to differentiate between injector issues and bottom-end problems.

1

Injector Knock Test (Cylinder Cut-Out)

A specialist will disconnect each injector one by one. If the knock disappears when a specific cylinder is cut, the problem is likely that injector.

2

Injector Leak-Off Test

They will measure the amount of diesel returning from each injector. Excessive leak-off indicates a worn injector that may be causing the knock.

3

Oil Pressure Test

Low oil pressure, particularly at idle, can be a sign of worn bearings or a failing oil pump. This is a critical test for bottom-end knocks.

4

Oil Analysis

An oil sample can reveal metal particles (copper, lead, tin) from worn bearings, confirming a bottom-end failure.

5

Inspect Oil Pump Bolt (Pre-2002)

On early TD5 engines, the specialist will inspect the oil pump bolt through the sump to ensure it is tight and secure.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes a TD5 engine to knock?

The most common causes are a failed injector washer (metallic tapping), a worn injector (excessive leak-off), or a failing big-end bearing (deep, rhythmic knock). Low oil pressure or a failed oil pump bolt can also lead to bottom-end knocks.

How much does it cost to fix a TD5 knocking noise?

Costs range from £200–£500 for injector washers, £800–£2,000 for injector replacement, £3,000–£5,000 for a bottom-end rebuild, and up to £4,000–£7,500 for a reconditioned engine.

Can I drive with a TD5 that is knocking?

It depends on the nature of the knock. A metallic tick from an injector may be safe to drive a short distance to a specialist. However, a deep, rhythmic knock that worsens under load is a sign of bottom-end failure – do not drive the vehicle.

Is a TD5 knocking noise always a big-end bearing?

No. The most common cause of a TD5 knock is a failed injector washer or a worn injector. However, if the knock is deep and worsens under load, it is likely a big-end bearing failure.

What should I do if my TD5 is knocking?

Stop driving and book a diagnostic with a Land Rover specialist. Do not ignore a TD5 knock – a cheap injector washer fix today could save you from an expensive engine replacement tomorrow.

Is a TD5 knocking noise covered by a warranty?

It depends. If the vehicle is within a warranty period and the failure is due to a manufacturing defect (e.g., a known oil pump bolt issue), it may be covered. However, wear-and-tear items like injector washers and bearings are often excluded.
