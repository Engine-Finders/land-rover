TD5 Oil in Coolant / Mayonnaise Under Oil Cap — Causes, Diagnosis & 2026 Costs

Finding oil in your TD5's coolant or a mayonnaise-like sludge under the oil filler cap is a worrying sight. It means oil and coolant are mixing — usually because of a failed head gasket, a cracked head, or a leaking oil cooler. This guide explains what causes this on the Land Rover TD5, how to diagnose it, what it costs to fix in the UK in 2026, and whether it's worth repairing.

The Short Answer

Oil in the coolant or mayonnaise under the oil cap on a TD5 engine is almost always caused by a breach between the oil and water systems. The three common culprits are: a failed head gasket (most frequent), a cracked cylinder head (especially on early 10P engines with plastic dowels), or leaking O-rings on the engine oil cooler (fitted to the side of the block). The cost to fix ranges from £150–£350 for oil cooler O-rings to £1,500–£3,000 for a head gasket, and up to £2,500–£4,000 for a replacement cylinder head. In severe cases where the engine has overheated and the block is damaged, a reconditioned engine at £4,500+ may be the only option.

  • Most common cause (TD5): Head gasket failure — often due to plastic dowels shearing on early engines
  • Typical diagnostic cost: £80–£150 [ESTIMATED]
  • Key indicator: Thick mayonnaise under oil cap, oily film in expansion tank, or actual oil droplets in coolant
  • Verdict: Immediate — do not drive if the temperature gauge rises or if you see significant contamination

If your TD5 shows signs of oil and coolant mixing, stop driving and get it checked — catching a head gasket early is far cheaper than a cracked head or a seized engine.

What the TD5 Specifically Does

Oil and coolant mixing on a TD5 shows up in a few distinct ways. Here’s what owners typically see.

🧁 Mayonnaise under the oil cap

A creamy, yellowish sludge on the underside of the oil filler cap or inside the rocker cover. This is emulsified oil and water – a classic sign of coolant getting into the oil. A small amount after short trips can be condensation, but thick, persistent mayo points to a leak.

🛢️ Oil film in the expansion tank

The coolant in the header tank has an oily sheen, or you see brown/black globules floating. This is oil entering the cooling system — often from a head gasket or oil cooler leak. The coolant may also turn a milky colour.

🌡️ Overheating or loss of coolant

Oil in the coolant reduces its heat-transfer ability, so the engine may run hotter. You might also notice coolant loss with no external leak – because the coolant is being burned or pumped into the oil system.

What else to look for: Check the oil dipstick – if the oil looks like a milkshake, you have a significant coolant leak into the oil. This can ruin bearings quickly. Also check for white smoke from the exhaust (steam) and a sweet smell. For other TD5 symptoms, see our TD5 coolant loss no visible leak and TD5 engine overheating guides.

Why This Engine In Particular — The TD5's Known Weaknesses

The TD5 has three main points where oil and coolant can mix. Each has a different repair cost and urgency.

🔧 Head Gasket Failure – The Most Common

The TD5's head gasket is a known weak point, especially on early 10P engines (1998–2002) that used plastic dowels to locate the cylinder head. These dowels can shear, allowing the head to shift slightly and the gasket to fail. Later 15P engines (2002–2006) used steel dowels, which are more robust, but head gaskets can still fail from age, overheating, or high mileage. Symptoms include oil in coolant, coolant in oil, white smoke, and pressurisation of the cooling system.

🔧 Cracked Cylinder Head – The Expensive One

TD5 heads can crack between the oil gallery and the water jacket, or between the water jacket and the exhaust port. Cracks often develop due to overheating, but can also be a casting fault. A cracked head is a more serious and costly repair. It often produces the same symptoms as a head gasket failure, but a chemical block test (sniff test) may pass because combustion gases aren't entering the coolant – only oil and water are mixing internally.

🔧 Engine Oil Cooler O-Rings – The Cheaper Fix

The TD5 has an engine oil cooler mounted on the side of the block. Its internal O-rings can fail, allowing engine oil to mix with the coolant. This is a relatively common failure and is much cheaper to fix than a head gasket or cracked head – it usually just involves replacing the O-rings and flushing the system. However, it can be mistaken for a head gasket issue, so proper diagnosis is key.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

Diagnosing oil in coolant on a TD5 requires a systematic approach to distinguish between head gasket, cracked head, and oil cooler O-rings. Here's the step-by-step process.

1

Visual Inspection

Check the oil cap for mayo and the expansion tank for oil film. Look for external oil leaks around the oil cooler (on the side of the block) – if the oil cooler is leaking externally, it may also be leaking internally.

2

Pressure Test

Pressurise the cooling system and look for pressure drop. Also perform a leak-down test on the cooling system to see if it holds pressure. If it doesn't, suspect head gasket or cracked head.

3

Chemical Block Test (Sniff Test)

Check for combustion gases in the coolant. Important: On a TD5, this test often passes even when the head gasket has failed between oil and water galleries, because no combustion gases enter the coolant. A pass does not rule out a head gasket or cracked head.

4

Oil Cooler Bypass Test

The specialist may temporarily bypass the oil cooler to see if the oil contamination stops. If it does, the oil cooler O-rings are the culprit – a much cheaper fix.

5

Borescope Inspection

Using a borescope through the glow plug holes, they can look for coolant in the cylinders or steam-cleaned pistons – a sign of internal coolant leakage.

6

Oil Analysis

Sending an oil sample to a lab can confirm coolant contamination and sometimes indicate the severity of wear. This is optional but useful for high-mileage engines.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes oil in coolant on a TD5 engine?

The most common causes are a failed head gasket (especially on 10P engines with plastic dowels), a cracked cylinder head, or leaking O-rings on the engine oil cooler. Each requires a different repair.

How much does it cost to fix oil in coolant on a TD5?

Costs range from £150–£350 for oil cooler O-rings, £1,500–£3,000 for a head gasket, £2,500–£4,000 for a cracked head, and £4,500–£6,500 for a replacement engine. These are independent specialist prices.

Can I drive a TD5 with oil in the coolant?

It depends. If it's a small amount of mayo under the cap and no overheating, you can drive to a garage. But if the oil is milky on the dipstick, the temperature is rising, or you have white smoke from the exhaust, stop immediately – continued driving will destroy the engine.

Will a block test always show a TD5 head gasket failure?

No. The chemical block test (sniff test) often passes on a TD5 even with a failed head gasket, because the failure may be between oil and water galleries, not between combustion chamber and coolant. A pass does not rule out head gasket or cracked head.

What should I check first on a TD5 with oil in coolant?

Check the oil cap for mayo and the expansion tank for oil. Then check the oil cooler for external leaks – a visual inspection can sometimes reveal O-ring failure. But a proper diagnosis requires pressure testing and possibly bypassing the oil cooler.

Is oil in coolant always a head gasket on a TD5?

No. The oil cooler O-rings are a common failure on TD5s and can cause oil to mix with coolant without affecting the head gasket. Always diagnose thoroughly before assuming the worst.
