The Short Answer

If your Land Rover cranks but won't start, the engine is turning over but not receiving the fuel, spark, or compression it needs to fire. The most common causes are a failed fuel pump (especially on 306DT and Ingenium diesels), a faulty crankshaft or camshaft position sensor, a dead battery that can't supply enough voltage, or an immobiliser issue. Repair costs range from £100–£200 for a battery, £200–£400 for a sensor, £500–£1,200 for a fuel pump, and up to £2,000+ for more complex electrical diagnostics.

  • Most likely cause: Fuel supply issue – pump failure or empty tank
  • Typical diagnosis cost: £80–£150 [EM-QUOTE]
  • Fuel pump replacement (indie): £500–£1,200 [EM-QUOTE]
  • Verdict: Immediate – a non-starting engine needs diagnosis. Don't keep cranking – you risk damaging the starter motor.

If your Land Rover won't start but the engine turns over, listen for the fuel pump priming and check the battery voltage – a simple jump-start could be all you need.

What It Sounds and Feels Like

A cranking but non-starting engine is unmistakable. Here's what owners typically experience.

🔊 Engine turns over but won't fire

The starter motor spins the engine, but it doesn't catch or run. You hear a rhythmic cranking sound, but no combustion. Catastrophic

⚡ Clicking or rapid clicking

A rapid clicking sound when you turn the key, without the engine turning over. This indicates a flat battery or poor electrical connection. Immediate

💨 Fuel smell or no smell

If you smell strong fuel, the engine may be flooded. If there's no smell at all, the fuel pump may not be delivering fuel. Catastrophic

What else to look for: A non-starting engine is often accompanied by other symptoms – a check engine light, a dead battery, or a security light flashing on the dashboard. If you see any of these, engine management light or limp mode, the issue is likely electrical or sensor-related.

How to Narrow It Down Yourself — Safe Checks Only

You can do a few simple, safe checks to gather information before you take the car to a specialist. Do not attempt any work that requires you to raise the vehicle or remove engine covers – we are not a garage, and these are observational checks only.

🔋

1. Check the battery

Turn on the headlights. If they're dim or the interior lights are weak, the battery is flat. Try jump-starting the vehicle – if it starts, the battery or alternator is the problem.

🔊

2. Listen for the fuel pump

Turn the key to the 'on' position (without cranking). Listen for a whirring sound from the rear of the vehicle – that's the fuel pump priming. If you don't hear it, the pump or relay may have failed.

🔑

3. Check the immobiliser

Look for a flashing security light on the dashboard. If it's flashing rapidly or staying on, the immobiliser may be preventing the engine from starting. Try using the spare key.

⛽

4. Check the fuel level

Don't rely on the fuel gauge – it can be inaccurate. If the gauge shows low, add fuel. If it shows half-full but still won't start, the gauge or sender may be faulty.

📝

5. Check for error codes

If you have a diagnostic tool, plug it in. Codes like P0335 (crankshaft position sensor) or P0087 (fuel rail pressure) can point to the exact cause.

These are observational checks only. If your Land Rover won't start, avoid repeated cranking – you could damage the starter motor or flood the engine. For a more structured approach, try our Diagnostic Wizard.

When It's Serious vs When It Can Wait

A non-starting engine is always urgent – but some causes are simple fixes, others are major. Here's how to tell.

⛔ Cannot Wait – Stop Cranking

  • Engine cranks but won't fire – no fuel smell
  • No fuel pump priming sound
  • Engine management light flashing
  • Strange noises from the engine when cranking
  • Car was running fine, then suddenly won't start

These are signs of a major fuel or electrical failure. Stop cranking and call for recovery.

🟢 Can Wait (But Monitor)

  • Battery is weak (dim lights, slow cranking)
  • Car starts with a jump-start
  • Security light is on – try spare key
  • Fuel gauge shows empty – add fuel

Probably a battery or key fob issue. Get a diagnostic soon.

How a Specialist Confirms the Cause

A good Land Rover specialist will follow a systematic diagnostic sequence to pinpoint why the engine won't start. Here's what they'll do.

1

Diagnostic Scan

Using a Land Rover‑compatible tool, they'll read fault codes. Common codes: P0335 (crankshaft sensor), P0340 (camshaft sensor), P0087 (fuel rail pressure), and P0562 (low battery voltage).

2

Fuel Pressure Test

They'll check the fuel rail pressure. If it's below specification, the fuel pump or fuel pressure regulator is likely faulty.

3

Battery and Charging System Test

They'll test the battery voltage and the alternator output. A battery that drops below 10.5V while cranking won't start the engine.

4

Sensor Tests

They'll check the crankshaft and camshaft position sensors for correct operation. Without a signal from these, the ECU won't fire the injectors or ignition coils.

5

Immobiliser System Check

They'll check the key fob battery, the immobiliser antenna, and the security module to ensure the immobiliser isn't preventing the start.

6

Injector and Ignition Tests

They'll test the injectors for proper operation and check for spark at the spark plugs to rule out a complete ignition failure.

A thorough diagnosis is essential to avoid unnecessary repairs. For more on diagnostic tools, see our Diagnostic Wizard.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes a Land Rover to crank but not start?

The most common causes are a fuel pump failure (no fuel pressure), a faulty crankshaft or camshaft position sensor, a dead battery, or an immobiliser issue. A blocked fuel filter or failed injectors can also be responsible.

Can I fix a cranking no-start myself?

You can check the battery, listen for the fuel pump, and try the spare key. If the battery is flat, a jump-start may work. But if the fuel pump or sensor has failed, you'll need a specialist. Don't keep cranking – it can damage the starter motor.

How do I know if it's the fuel pump?

Turn the key to the 'on' position without cranking. Listen for a whirring sound from the rear of the vehicle for 2-3 seconds. If you don't hear it, the fuel pump or relay has likely failed.

How much does it cost to fix a cranking no-start on a Land Rover?

Costs range from £100–£200 for a battery, £200–£400 for a sensor, £500–£1,200 for a fuel pump, and up to £2,000+ for ECU or immobiliser repairs.

What should I do if my Land Rover won't start?

Check the battery voltage, listen for the fuel pump, and check the security light. If the battery is flat, try a jump-start. If it still won't start, call a specialist and have it recovered – don't keep cranking.

Is a no-start condition covered by a Land Rover warranty?

It depends. Fuel pump and sensor failures may be covered under a manufacturer's warranty if they fail due to a defect. However, battery failure is typically wear and tear and is not covered. Always check your policy.
