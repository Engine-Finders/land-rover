The 276DT (2.7‑litre TDV6 diesel) takes approximately 6.3 litres of oil with a filter change [PLACEHOLDER — confirm vs OEM workshop data], using a 5W‑30 viscosity oil meeting JLR specification STJLR.03.5005.

  • Manufacturer service interval: 16,000 miles or 12 months (whichever comes first) on qualifying oil [PLACEHOLDER — confirm]
  • Real‑world caveat: the 276DT is sensitive to oil quality and viscosity, and its well‑documented oil pump housing failure is not oil‑related but correct oil gives the engine its best chance
  • Fitted to Discovery 3, Range Rover Sport L320, and Jaguar XF X250, among others

Notes & Caveats

The manufacturer's 16,000‑mile service interval assumes normal use with a healthy mix of motorway and urban driving. In real‑world UK conditions – particularly short urban journeys – the 276DT's DPF regeneration process can introduce unburned fuel into the oil, leading to dilution. Many independent specialists [EM-INTERVIEW] recommend a 6,000‑mile oil change interval for vehicles used predominantly for short trips, regardless of the service computer indication. This practical adjustment costs a fraction of a major repair and is widely adopted by workshops that see these engines regularly.

The 276DT's well‑documented oil pump housing failure is a mechanical issue, not an oil‑related one, but correct oil viscosity is critical to maintaining adequate oil pressure at the pump. For a deeper look at the engine's reliability and cost‑of‑ownership, see the 276DT Engine Knowledge Centre.

Common Mistakes

  • Using the wrong viscosity grade. 5W‑30 is recommended for UK use; 5W‑40 may be used in warmer climates but check your handbook.
  • Ignoring the low‑SAPS requirement. Using a standard oil can clog the DPF and after‑treatment systems – see our DPF & Emissions Systems Explained.
  • Trusting the service computer unconditionally. It cannot detect your driving pattern; short trips demand more frequent changes.
  • Confusing 276DT with 306DT. The 3.0‑litre V6 diesel (306DT) has a different oil capacity and spec – check your engine code via our Engine Code Guide.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The oil filler cap and dipstick are clearly marked and accessible from the top of the engine bay. Confirm your exact engine code by locating the engine number stamp – see our Engine Number Location guide. Early and late production runs of the 276DT may have minor running changes affecting oil specification; always refer to the latest JLR service bulletin for your VIN.

Why This Specification Matters

Using the correct oil grade and low‑SAPS approval is not optional on the 276DT. The engine's high‑pressure common‑rail system, turbocharger, and emissions after‑treatment are all sensitive to oil viscosity and ash content. Incorrect oil can lead to increased wear, DPF blockage, and potentially expensive repairs – far exceeding the cost of proper maintenance. The 5W‑30 grade is specified for its cold‑start flow characteristics, protecting the engine during the critical warm‑up phase on cold UK mornings. For a full breakdown of the failure modes linked to poor oil maintenance, visit our 276DT oil pump failure page and the 276DT symptom hub.
