The 306DT (3.0 TDV6/SDV6) takes 7.4 litres of oil with a full filter change [PLACEHOLDER — confirm vs OEM workshop data], using a low-SAPS 5W-30 viscosity oil meeting JLR specification STJLR.03.5005.

  • Manufacturer service interval: 16,000 miles or 12 months (whichever comes first) on qualifying oil
  • Real‑world caveat: the 306DT is sensitive to oil quality and viscosity, especially given its known crankshaft bearing failure pattern — which is not caused by oil choice, but correct oil gives it the best chance
  • Applies to Discovery 3/4/5, Range Rover Sport (L320/L494), and L405, with minor capacity variance — see the variant table below

Service Interval & Oil Life

The manufacturer's 16,000‑mile service interval assumes motorway‑weighted use at normal operating temperatures. In real‑world UK conditions — particularly short urban journeys, towing, or low‑speed off‑road work — the oil degrades faster through soot accumulation and fuel dilution, even on a healthy engine. Independent specialists we've spoken to frequently recommend a 6,000‑mile oil change interval for 306DT‑powered vehicles used in these conditions, irrespective of what the service computer indicates. This is a practical adjustment, not a criticism of the engine design — the same recommendation is common across modern high‑output diesel engines with DPF systems.

The key point is simply that oil condition is the single most important variable affecting the 306DT's well‑documented crankshaft bearing failure pattern — which is not caused by oil choice, but by a mechanical design weakness in the bearing coating itself. Correct oil, changed regularly, gives the engine its best possible odds of exceeding 100,000 miles without major intervention. The crankshaft bearing failure page covers that mechanism in full detail.

The 60% Threshold Applied

On the Land Rover site, we apply a 60% repair‑to‑value threshold: if the total cost of a reconditioned engine supply and fit exceeds 60% of the vehicle's current market value, scrapping or breaking the vehicle is the financially rational choice.

For the 306DT, a reconditioned supply‑only engine costs £6,500–£9,500 [EM-QUOTE], with R&R labour at approximately 15–20 hours at independent rates. The total fitted cost typically lands in the £8,500–£11,500 range, depending on location and ancillaries. For a Discovery 4 or early L405 valued at £10,000–£14,000, a full replacement sits right at or just above the 60% threshold, meaning a significant proportion of owners in that value band will conclude that replacement doesn't make financial sense.

This is why the 306DT features so prominently in our Economics & Scrap Decision guide — the combination of a very high replacement cost and the vehicle's relatively low residual value on older examples means the "scrap it" answer is more common than owners expect.

The oil specification and service interval don't change this threshold — but they do affect whether you get to make that decision at 120,000 miles or 180,000 miles.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a non‑low‑SAPS oil. This can damage the DPF and after‑treatment system over time — see our DPF & Emissions Systems Explained for why this matters.
  • Trusting the service computer blindly. It doesn't know your driving pattern involves short urban trips or heavy towing.
  • Assuming the 306DT is a 3.6 TDV8. The 368DT has a different oil capacity and specification entirely — check your engine code first via our Engine Code Guide.
  • Assuming all V6 diesel Land Rovers share identical figures. The 276DT (2.7 TDV6) has a different sump and oil capacity — see the 276DT oil capacity page for that variant.
