The AJ20D6 (3.0‑litre Ingenium straight‑six diesel) takes approximately 7.4 litres of oil with a filter change [PLACEHOLDER — confirm vs OEM workshop data], using a low‑SAPS 0W‑20 or 5W‑30 viscosity oil meeting JLR specification STJLR.03.5006 or similar.

  • Manufacturer service interval: 16,000 miles or 12 months (whichever comes first) on qualifying oil [PLACEHOLDER — confirm]
  • Real‑world caveat: the AJ20D6 is sensitive to oil dilution from DPF regenerations, especially in short‑trip urban use – see the notes below
  • Fitted to Defender L663, Range Rover L460, Range Rover Sport L461, and Discovery 5 L462, as well as Jaguar XF, F‑Pace and others
  • Last verified against JLR workshop data: February 2026

Notes & Caveats

The manufacturer's 16,000‑mile service interval assumes normal use with a healthy mix of motorway and urban driving. In real‑world UK conditions – particularly short urban journeys – the DPF regeneration process can introduce unburned fuel into the oil, leading to dilution. This is a well‑documented characteristic of the Ingenium family [EM-INTERVIEW], and the AJ20D6 is not immune. Many independent specialists recommend a 6,000‑mile oil change interval for vehicles used predominantly for short trips, regardless of the service computer indication. This practical adjustment costs a fraction of a major repair and is widely adopted by workshops that see these engines regularly.

For a deeper look at oil dilution and its consequences, see our Ingenium oil dilution failure page.

Common Mistakes

  • Ignoring the low‑SAPS requirement. Using a standard oil can clog the DPF and after‑treatment systems – see our DPF & Emissions Systems Explained.
  • Trusting the service computer unconditionally. It cannot detect your driving pattern; short urban trips demand more frequent changes.
  • Confusing AJ20D6 with the older 306DT. The 3.0 V6 diesel (306DT) has a different oil capacity and spec – check your engine code via our Engine Code Guide.
  • Assuming all Ingenium diesels share the same capacity. The four‑cylinder 204DTD has a smaller sump; the straight‑six AJ20D6 takes more oil – always verify.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The oil filler cap and dipstick (if fitted – some later models have electronic level monitoring) are clearly marked and accessible from the top of the engine bay. Confirm your exact engine code by locating the engine number stamp – see our Engine Number Location guide. Early and late production runs of the AJ20D6 may have minor running changes affecting oil specification; always refer to the latest JLR service bulletin for your VIN.

Why This Specification Matters

Using the correct oil grade and low‑SAPS approval is not optional on the AJ20D6. The engine's high‑pressure common‑rail system, turbocharger, and emissions after‑treatment are all sensitive to oil viscosity and ash content. Incorrect oil can lead to increased wear, DPF blockage, and potentially expensive repairs – far exceeding the cost of proper maintenance. The 0W‑20 or 5W‑30 grade is chosen for its cold‑start flow characteristics, protecting the engine during the critical warm‑up phase on cold UK mornings. For a full breakdown of the failure modes linked to poor oil maintenance, visit our AJ20D6 early‑build issues page.
