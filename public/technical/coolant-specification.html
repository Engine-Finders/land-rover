Notes & Caveats

Land Rover has used Organic Acid Technology (OAT) coolant since the late 1990s. The factory‑fill is often Havoline XLC (orange/pink) or a JLR‑approved equivalent meeting specification STJLR.03.5003. This is a long‑life coolant designed for aluminium engines and provides excellent corrosion protection.

The change interval is typically 5 years or 100,000 miles – whichever comes first – but many specialists recommend 4 years or 80,000 miles for vehicles used in severe conditions (frequent towing, off‑road, or stop‑start urban driving). Always use a 50/50 mix of concentrate and distilled water – tap water can introduce minerals that damage the cooling system.

Capacities listed are for the entire system, including the radiator, heater matrix, and (where fitted) the intercooler or charge cooler circuit. When draining and refilling, you may not recover the full volume – air locks can occur, so bleeding the system properly is essential. Refer to our Engine Code Decoder to confirm your exact engine variant before ordering coolant.

Common Mistakes

  • Using the wrong coolant type. Mixing OAT with older IAT (green) coolant can cause gel formation, clogging the radiator and heater core – always use the specified OAT.
  • Not using distilled water. Hard tap water introduces scale and minerals that reduce cooling efficiency and accelerate corrosion.
  • Over‑diluting the coolant. A 50/50 mix provides the correct freezing and boiling protection; going weaker reduces corrosion inhibitors and may lead to engine damage.
  • Ignoring the bleed procedure. Many Land Rover engines (especially V8s) are prone to air locks – failing to bleed correctly can cause localised overheating.
  • Assuming all models have the same capacity. The table above shows significant variation – always check your engine code and refer to the workshop manual.

Frequently Asked Questions

What coolant does a Land Rover use?

Land Rover specifies Organic Acid Technology (OAT) coolant, typically Havoline XLC or equivalent meeting JLR spec STJLR.03.5003. It is orange or pink in colour – never use old green IAT coolant.

How much coolant does a 3.0 SDV6 take?

The 306DT (3.0 SDV6) system capacity is approximately 11.5 litres [PLACEHOLDER – confirm] including the radiator, heater matrix and intercooler circuit. Always check your specific vehicle.

How often should Land Rover coolant be changed?

The official interval is 5 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first. Many specialists recommend a shorter 4‑year / 80,000‑mile cycle for heavy‑duty use.

Can I mix different coolant brands?

You should only mix OAT coolants that meet the same specification. Mixing different OAT formulations is generally safe, but always use the same type – never mix with IAT (green) or HOAT.

What is the correct coolant capacity for the 4.4 SDV8?

The 448DT (4.4 SDV8) requires approximately 14 litres [PLACEHOLDER – confirm] for the complete system, including the charge cooler circuit.
