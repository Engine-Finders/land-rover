Defender Service Schedule & Maintenance Intervals

Complete service schedule for the Land Rover Defender across all generations – L316, L663 and classic models. Oil change intervals, major service milestones, cost estimates and the off‑road usage caveat that affects every interval.

The Land Rover Defender service schedule varies by generation and engine. As a rule of thumb, oil changes are due every 12,000 miles or 12 months, with a major service (including filters, fluids and inspection) every 24,000 miles [INDUSTRY ESTIMATE]. For the modern L663, the service indicator is condition‑based, but specialists recommend the same 12‑month maximum [EM‑INTERVIEW].

  • Typical oil & filter service: £180–£350 (indie) [ESTIMATED]
  • Major service (24k/2yr): £400–£700 [ESTIMATED]
  • Off‑road use or frequent towing: halve the interval – specialist consensus [EM‑INTERVIEW]
  • Covers all Defender models: L316, L663, classic, V8 and plug‑in hybrid.

Full schedule, variant differences and common mistakes are detailed below.

Variant Differences – L316 vs L663

The service schedule is not one‑size‑fits‑all. Older Defender models (L316) have fixed intervals, while the L663 uses condition‑based servicing. The key differences are summarised below.

Notes & Caveats

The official schedule assumes normal road use. In practice, many Defenders are used for towing, off‑roading or heavy‑duty work – all of which shorten service intervals.

  • Off‑road use or towing: Specialists recommend halving the oil change interval (e.g., to 6,000 miles for L316, or yearly even if the CBS says longer) [EM‑INTERVIEW].
  • Short journeys: On L663 diesels, frequent cold starts and DPF regens can dilute the oil – see our Ingenium oil dilution page for the mechanism. Short‑trip owners should change oil at least once a year regardless of mileage.
  • Brake fluid and coolant: Often overlooked – JLR recommends brake fluid change every 2 years regardless of mileage [THIRD‑PARTY]. Coolant should be checked annually and replaced every 5 years.
  • Timing chains vs belts: Older 200Tdi/300Tdi have timing belts with a fixed 60,000‑mile interval – see our 300Tdi timing belt page. Modern Ingenium engines use chains (no fixed replacement, but tensioners wear – Ingenium timing chain failure page).

Common Mistakes

  • Ignoring the CBS warning on L663. The system monitors oil quality, but it doesn't know about off‑road use or towing – many specialists reset the CBS and still advise an annual oil change [EM‑INTERVIEW].
  • Using the wrong oil grade. Using 5W‑30 in an L663 that requires 0W‑20 can lead to increased wear and CBS inaccuracy – check your owner's manual or our oil finder tool.
  • Forgetting the diesel fuel filter. On L316 TD5 and 2.2 TDCi, a clogged fuel filter can cause injector damage – replace it at every major service.
  • Not resetting the service indicator after DIY work. The L663 requires a diagnostic tool to reset the CBS – many owners skip this and lose track of the actual mileage since the last service.
  • Assuming the same schedule for all Defenders. The L316 and L663 have completely different service regimes – always check by model year and engine code via our Engine Code Guide.

How to Verify Your Actual Service Schedule

The definitive source is your vehicle's owner's manual and the service book. For L663, you can check the CBS status in the instrument cluster menu. For older Defenders, the service intervals are listed in the handbook. If you've lost the book, you can use our VIN decoder to identify your exact model year and engine, then cross‑reference with the tables above. Always note the mileage and date of the last service – if you're unsure, a specialist can inspect the condition of the oil and filters to give a recommendation.
