Discovery Sport Service Schedule & Maintenance Intervals

Complete service schedule for the Land Rover Discovery Sport across L550 and facelift models. Oil change intervals, major service milestones, cost estimates and the off‑road usage caveat that affects every interval.

The Land Rover Discovery Sport service schedule varies by generation and engine. As a rule of thumb, oil changes are due every 12,000 miles or 12 months, with a major service (including filters, fluids and inspection) every 24,000 miles [INDUSTRY ESTIMATE]. For facelift models with condition‑based servicing, specialists recommend the same 12‑month maximum [EM‑INTERVIEW].

  • Typical oil & filter service: £180–£350 (indie) [ESTIMATED]
  • Major service (24k/2yr): £400–£700 [ESTIMATED]
  • Off‑road use or frequent towing: halve the interval – specialist consensus [EM‑INTERVIEW]
  • Covers all Discovery Sport models: L550 pre‑facelift and facelift, including Ingenium diesel and petrol variants.

Full schedule, variant differences and common mistakes are detailed below.

Notes & Caveats

The official schedule assumes normal road use. In practice, many Discovery Sports are used for towing, off‑roading or family duties – all of which shorten service intervals.

  • Off‑road use or towing: Specialists recommend halving the oil change interval (e.g., to 6,000 miles for pre‑facelift) [EM‑INTERVIEW].
  • Short journeys: On Ingenium diesels, frequent cold starts and DPF regens can dilute the oil – see our Ingenium oil dilution page for the mechanism. Short‑trip owners should change oil at least once a year regardless of mileage.
  • Brake fluid and coolant: Often overlooked – JLR recommends brake fluid change every 2 years regardless of mileage [THIRD‑PARTY]. Coolant should be checked annually and replaced every 5 years.
  • Timing chains vs belts: All Discovery Sport engines use timing chains (no fixed replacement, but tensioners wear – Ingenium timing chain failure page). The 2.2 TD4 uses a chain, not a belt – see our 224DT timing chain page.
  • PHEV specific: The P300e hybrid has additional cooling system checks and high‑voltage inspections at major service intervals – always use a JLR-trained specialist [EM‑INTERVIEW].

Common Mistakes

  • Ignoring the CBS warning on facelift models. The system monitors oil quality, but it doesn't know about off‑road use or towing – many specialists reset the CBS and still advise an annual oil change [EM‑INTERVIEW].
  • Using the wrong oil grade. Using 5W‑30 in a facelift Ingenium that requires 0W‑20 can lead to increased wear and CBS inaccuracy – check your owner's manual or our oil finder tool.
  • Forgetting the diesel fuel filter. On 2.2 TD4 and Ingenium diesels, a clogged fuel filter can cause injector damage – replace it at every major service.
  • Not resetting the service indicator after DIY work. Facelift models require a diagnostic tool to reset the CBS – many owners skip this and lose track of the actual mileage since the last service.
  • Assuming the same schedule for pre‑facelift and facelift. The service regimes are completely different – always check by model year and engine code via our Engine Code Guide.

How to Verify Your Actual Service Schedule

The definitive source is your vehicle's owner's manual and the service book. For facelift models, you can check the CBS status in the instrument cluster menu. For pre‑facelift models, the service intervals are listed in the handbook. If you've lost the book, you can use our VIN decoder to identify your exact model year and engine, then cross‑reference with the tables above. Always note the mileage and date of the last service – if you're unsure, a specialist can inspect the condition of the oil and filters to give a recommendation.
