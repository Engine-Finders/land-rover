Land Rover Glow Plug & Cold Start System Reference

Reference specification for glow plugs and the cold start system on Land Rover diesel engines – from the classic Tdi to the modern Ingenium. Covers type, voltage, replacement intervals and common faults.

Land Rover diesel engines use ceramic or metal sheathed glow plugs rated at 11–12V, with typical resistance between 0.5–1.5Ω. The cold start system pre‑heats the combustion chamber to aid starting in low temperatures. Most modern Land Rovers use fast‑glow (4–6 second) plugs controlled by the ECU based on coolant temperature and engine speed.

  • Glow plug replacement interval: typically every 60,000–80,000 miles or as required [PLACEHOLDER – confirm vs workshop manual]
  • Common glow plug failure: open circuit (no resistance) or carbon fouling – both trigger the glow plug warning light
  • Always use OEM or equivalent quality plugs – cheap aftermarket plugs can swell and seize in the cylinder head

Notes & Caveats

Glow plugs are only part of the cold start system. The glow plug relay, engine coolant temperature sensor, and ECU logic all play a role. On modern Land Rovers, the glow plugs are often used for post‑heating (after the engine has started) to reduce emissions and improve idle stability during warm‑up.

The typical pre‑glow time varies from 1–2 seconds in warm weather to 10–15 seconds in very cold conditions. If the glow plug light flashes or fails to illuminate at all, check the relay and the coolant temperature sensor – a faulty sensor can prevent the glow plugs from activating even when the ambient temperature is low.

For older Tdi engines, glow plugs are simpler but still critical. A failed glow plug on a 200Tdi or 300Tdi will cause hard starting and white smoke on cold mornings. Always replace glow plugs in full sets – mixing old and new plugs can cause uneven heating and potential cylinder damage.

Common Mistakes

  • Fitting the wrong voltage glow plug. Some aftermarket plugs are 12V but have different current draw – ensure the resistance range matches the specification.
  • Not replacing the glow plug relay. If the relay is faulty, even new glow plugs won't heat. Always test the relay and the power supply before condemning the plugs.
  • Forgetting to apply anti‑seize. Glow plugs sit in the cylinder head and can seize over time. Use a high‑temperature anti‑seize compound on the threads to prevent future removal problems.
  • Over‑torquing the glow plugs. The recommended torque is typically 20–30 Nm [PLACEHOLDER] – over‑tightening can snap the plug or damage the cylinder head threads.
  • Ignoring the coolant temperature sensor. A faulty sensor can prevent the glow plug relay from activating, causing cold start issues even with good plugs.

How to Verify Glow Plug Condition

The simplest way to test glow plugs is with a multimeter. Disconnect the wiring harness and measure resistance between the terminal and the engine block (or the plug body). A reading within the spec range (typically 0.5–1.5Ω) indicates a good plug. An open circuit (infinite resistance) means the plug has failed internally.

You can also test the power supply to the glow plugs. With the ignition on and the engine cold, measure the voltage at the glow plug harness – it should be around battery voltage (11–12V) for the pre‑glow period. If there is no power, check the relay and the coolant temperature sensor.

For a visual inspection, remove the glow plug and inspect the heating element. If the tip is swollen, damaged, or covered in heavy carbon, replace it. Always cross‑reference the plug type with your engine code using our Engine Code Decoder before ordering replacements.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should glow plugs be replaced on a Land Rover diesel?

The typical interval is 60,000–80,000 miles [PLACEHOLDER], but many specialists recommend replacing them earlier if you experience cold start issues. Always replace in sets.

What are the symptoms of a faulty glow plug?

Hard starting in cold weather, white smoke from the exhaust on startup, a flashing glow plug warning light, and increased idle roughness until the engine warms up.

Can I test glow plugs without removing them?

Yes – measure the resistance at the harness connector with a multimeter. A good plug will show low resistance (0.5–1.5Ω). An open circuit indicates a failed plug.

What causes glow plug failure?

Common causes include carbon fouling from poor combustion, electrical overload (relay failure), and physical damage (swelling or cracking). Cheap aftermarket plugs are also prone to premature failure.

Can I drive with a faulty glow plug?

Yes, but cold starts will be difficult, and the engine may run poorly until warm. Prolonged driving with failed glow plugs can stress the battery and starter motor.
