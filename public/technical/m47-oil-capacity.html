Notes & Caveats

The M47 is a robust 2.0-litre diesel, but it has a well‑known vulnerability: the timing chain tensioner on later variants. Oil quality and change intervals directly affect tensioner longevity. The recommended 5W-30 or 5W-40 grade with BMW Longlife-04 approval is essential for maintaining oil pressure and protecting the chain tensioner. [EM-INTERVIEW]

In Land Rover applications (Freelander 2, early Evoque), the M47 was often replaced by the Ford‑sourced 2.2‑litre diesel or the later Ingenium family. Using the correct oil specification is particularly important in these applications due to the heavier vehicle weight and different operating conditions. [EM-INTERVIEW]

This engine is also known for timing chain issues on high‑mileage units, and regular oil changes with the correct spec are the single most effective prevention.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a non‑BMW‑approved oil. Many generic 5W‑30 oils do not meet BMW Longlife‑04 approval, which is critical for the M47's hydraulic valve lifters and tensioner.
  • Assuming all 2.0‑litre BMW diesels take the same oil. The later N47 and B47 have different oil specifications and capacities — check your engine code first.
  • Ignoring the oil filter change. The spin‑on filter holds a significant volume; topping up without circulating can lead to overfilling.
  • Confusing the M47 with the later N47. The N47 has a different oil capacity and is more sensitive to oil quality — see our 204DTD Oil Capacity page for modern Ingenium figures.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The M47 dipstick is located on the driver's side of the engine (right‑hand side in RHD vehicles). The oil filler cap is on the cam cover. The oil filter is a spin‑on canister, usually mounted on the side of the engine block. Confirm your exact engine code via the engine number stamp (e.g. M47D20) using our Engine Number Location guide before assuming these figures apply — early and late production runs occasionally carry minor running changes.

Why This Specification Matters

The M47's hydraulic valve lifters and timing chain tensioner rely on oil pressure and viscosity. Using an oil that doesn't meet BMW Longlife‑04 approval can lead to increased wear, noisy lifters, and premature timing chain tensioner failure — a well‑documented issue that can result in a catastrophic engine failure if left unaddressed. [EM-INTERVIEW]

In Land Rover applications, the M47 often sees heavier use than in BMW saloons, which makes oil quality even more critical. Regular oil changes with the correct spec are a relatively low‑cost way to avoid a £2,000+ timing chain repair or a £4,000+ engine replacement. [EM-QUOTE]
