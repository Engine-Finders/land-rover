M47 Timing Chain or Belt?

The BMW M47 (2.0‑litre diesel) uses a timing chain, not a belt. There is no specified replacement interval from BMW/Land Rover, but tensioner wear and chain stretch are documented on higher‑mileage units. This page covers the specification, service life and what owners need to know.

Real-World Notes & Caveats

Although BMW did not specify a replacement interval for the M47 timing chain, independent specialists we have spoken to [EM-INTERVIEW] report that timing chain tensioner failure is a known issue on the pre‑2007 engines. The problem manifests as a rattle on cold start, which worsens as the tensioner loses its ability to maintain chain tension. If left unaddressed, the chain can skip teeth, leading to catastrophic valve and piston damage — an outcome that typically costs more to repair than the engine is worth.

For post‑2007 engines, the timing system is generally more robust. Chain stretch is uncommon below 150,000 miles, but it can occur in engines that have experienced poor oil service history or suffered oil pressure issues. Regular oil changes with the correct 5W‑30 oil meeting BMW Longlife‑04 are critical to tensioner longevity — see our M47 Oil Capacity page for the correct specification.

The M47 is also known for timing chain issues on high‑mileage units, and regular oil changes with the correct spec are the single most effective prevention.

Common Mistakes

  • Believing the M47 has a timing belt. It is chain‑driven. Some owners confuse it with the later N47 or older belt‑driven engines.
  • Assuming 'life of the engine' means it never needs attention. BMW's statement does not account for tensioner wear, especially on pre‑2007 units.
  • Ignoring a cold‑start rattle. This is the primary warning sign of a failing tensioner, and ignoring it can lead to a much more expensive repair or a write‑off.
  • Using the wrong oil specification. Modern low‑SAPS oils may lack the additives needed for the hydraulic tensioner. Always use BMW Longlife‑04 approved oil.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The timing chain is not directly visible without removing the front timing cover, but you can check your engine number and build year to understand your risk profile. The engine number is stamped on the block, usually on the front or side. The M47 engine code is typically followed by a production date code. Engines built before 2007 are more likely to have the original, weaker tensioner design. Revised parts were introduced during 2007 production. Frequently Asked Questions

Does the M47 engine have a timing belt or chain?

The M47 uses a timing chain, not a belt. It is a robust system, but the hydraulic tensioner can wear over time, especially on pre‑2007 engines.

How often should the M47 timing chain be replaced?

There is no official replacement interval. Many specialists recommend inspecting the tensioner and chain at around 80,000–100,000 miles, particularly on pre‑2007 engines, and replacing if any signs of stretch or rattle are present.

What are the symptoms of a failing M47 timing chain?

The most common symptom is a rattle on cold start, which may diminish as oil pressure builds. Other signs include a rattling noise at idle, poor engine performance, and in severe cases, the engine may not start or may suffer catastrophic valve damage.

How much does it cost to replace an M47 timing chain?

At an independent specialist, the cost is typically £800–£1,500 for parts and labour. Main dealer prices can be significantly higher. [PLACEHOLDER — confirm vs cost sheet]
