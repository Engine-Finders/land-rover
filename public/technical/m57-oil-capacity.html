Notes & Caveats

The M57 is a robust 3.0-litre diesel, but it has a well‑known vulnerability: the timing chain tensioner on higher‑mileage units. Oil quality and change intervals directly affect tensioner longevity. The recommended 5W-30 or 5W-40 grade with BMW Longlife-04 approval is essential for maintaining oil pressure and protecting the chain tensioner. [EM-INTERVIEW]

In Land Rover applications (Range Rover L322), the M57 was used as the TD6 diesel option. Using the correct oil specification is particularly important in these applications due to the heavier vehicle weight and different operating conditions. [EM-INTERVIEW]

This engine is also known for timing chain issues on high‑mileage units, and regular oil changes with the correct spec are the single most effective prevention.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a non‑BMW‑approved oil. Many generic 5W‑30 oils do not meet BMW Longlife‑04 approval, which is critical for the M57's hydraulic valve lifters and tensioner.
  • Assuming all 3.0‑litre BMW diesels take the same oil. The later N57 and B57 have different oil specifications and capacities — check your engine code first.
  • Ignoring the oil filter change. The spin‑on filter holds a significant volume; topping up without circulating can lead to overfilling.
  • Confusing the M57 with the later N57. The N57 has a different oil capacity and is more sensitive to oil quality — see our N57 Oil Capacity page for the later engine's figures.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The M57 dipstick is located on the driver's side of the engine (right‑hand side in RHD vehicles). The oil filler cap is on the cam cover. The oil filter is a spin‑on canister, usually mounted on the side of the engine block. Confirm your exact engine code via the engine number stamp (e.g. M57D30) using our Engine Number Location guide before assuming these figures apply — early and late production runs occasionally carry minor running changes.

Why This Specification Matters

The M57's hydraulic valve lifters and timing chain tensioner rely on oil pressure and viscosity. Using an oil that doesn't meet BMW Longlife‑04 approval can lead to increased wear, noisy lifters, and premature timing chain tensioner failure — a well‑documented issue that can result in a catastrophic engine failure if left unaddressed. [EM-INTERVIEW]

In Land Rover applications, the M57 often sees heavier use than in BMW saloons, which makes oil quality even more critical. Regular oil changes with the correct spec are a relatively low‑cost way to avoid a £2,000+ timing chain repair or a £4,000+ engine replacement. [EM-QUOTE]
