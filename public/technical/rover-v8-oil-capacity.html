The Rover V8 takes between 5.2 and 5.7 litres of engine oil with a filter change, depending on displacement and sump type. The recommended grade is 20W-50 for most applications, though 10W-40 is also widely used in milder climates. [PATTERN DATA]

Notes & Caveats

The Rover V8 is one of the most straightforward V8s to service, but there are several nuances that catch owners out. The recommended 20W-50 grade is a carry-over from the engine's 1960s origins and reflects the large bearing clearances and older materials used. In the UK, 10W-40 is also widely used and provides better cold-start protection, but many specialists still prefer 20W-50 for older engines, particularly those with high mileage or marginal oil pressure. [EM-INTERVIEW]

The most critical caveat is zinc (ZDDP) content. Early Rover V8s — particularly the 3.5 and early 3.9-litre units — use flat-tappet camshafts that rely on a high-pressure boundary layer formed by zinc additives in the oil. Modern low-SAPS oils have drastically reduced zinc levels to protect catalytic converters, and using them in an older Rover V8 can cause rapid camshaft and lifter wear. [EM-INTERVIEW] Many specialists recommend dedicated classic oils such as Millers Classic 20W-50 or Valvoline VR1, which retain the higher zinc content these engines require.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a modern low-SAPS oil. Modern oils lack the zinc necessary for flat-tappet camshaft longevity — a mistake that can wipe a camshaft within 10,000 miles on a 3.5-litre engine. See our Rover V8 Timing Chain or Belt? page for related service advice.
  • Assuming all Rover V8s take the same quantity. The 4.6-litre requires nearly half a litre more oil than the 3.5 — overfilling or underfilling is common when the wrong figure is used.
  • Not checking the dipstick after a filter change. The remote oil filter on many applications holds a significant volume, and topping up to the full mark without allowing the oil to circulate can lead to overfilling.
  • Confusing the Rover V8 with later JLR V8s. The AJ-V8 and AJ133 are completely different engine families — see our AJ-V8 Oil Capacity page for the later engine's figures.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The Rover V8's dipstick is located on the driver's side of the engine (right-hand side in LHD, left-hand in RHD, depending on application). The oil filler cap is on the rocker cover of the same bank. On later engines with remote oil filters, the filter is mounted on the inner wing or chassis rail — its capacity should be accounted for when measuring. Confirm your exact displacement via the engine number stamp (e.g. 35D for 3.5, 39D for 3.9, 40D for 4.0, 46D for 4.6) using our Engine Number Location guide before assuming the capacity figures apply.

Why This Specification Matters

The Rover V8 is a robust but ageing engine design, and oil choice is arguably the single most important maintenance decision an owner makes. The large bearing clearances and flat-tappet camshafts mean that using the correct viscosity and additive package is not optional — it directly affects both oil pressure and camshaft life. A 20W-50 high-zinc oil keeps the bearings happy and the cam lobes intact; a modern 5W-30 low-SAPS oil will drain back too quickly at idle and fail to protect the valve train, leading to a noisy top end, low oil pressure at hot idle, and eventually a camshaft replacement that costs several times more than the oil would have over the lifetime of the vehicle. [EM-INTERVIEW]
