What the Oil & Fluid Specs Mean

The oil grade (e.g., 5W‑30) refers to the oil's viscosity — the first number (5W) is the cold-start performance, the second (30) is the viscosity at operating temperature. The JLR specification (e.g., STJLR.03.5006) is the manufacturer's approval standard; using oil that meets this spec is critical for engine longevity and warranty compliance.

The capacity is the total oil required with a new filter. Always check the dipstick after filling and top up as needed — overfilling can be as damaging as underfilling.

For full service intervals and other fluid specifications (coolant, brake fluid, transmission), visit our Technical Reference section or the service schedule for your model.

Frequently Asked Questions About Engine Oil

Is the oil finder tool free?

Yes, completely free with no email required to see your result.

What happens if I use the wrong oil grade?

Using the wrong viscosity or specification can reduce fuel economy, increase wear, and in extreme cases lead to engine damage — especially for modern engines with variable valve timing and turbochargers.

Where can I find my engine code?

Your engine code is on the VIN plate (positions 4‑8) or stamped on the engine block. See our engine code decoder and engine number location guides for help.

Does this tool cover older Land Rover engines?

Yes, we cover common older engines like the TD5 and 300TDI. For very rare engines, refer to the vehicle handbook or our Technical Reference section.

What about other fluids like coolant or transmission fluid?

This tool focuses on engine oil. For coolant, brake fluid, and transmission fluid specifications, see our Technical Reference pages or the service schedule for your model.
