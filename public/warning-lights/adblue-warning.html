What the AdBlue Warning Means

The AdBlue warning light — typically an amber icon showing a container with a droplet or a DEF (diesel exhaust fluid) symbol — indicates a problem with the selective catalytic reduction (SCR) system. AdBlue is a urea-based fluid injected into the exhaust to reduce nitrogen oxide (NOx) emissions. The warning can mean one of three things: the AdBlue tank is nearly empty, the fluid quality is poor, or a component in the SCR system (NOx sensor, injector, heater, or pump) has failed.

On Land Rover and Range Rover diesels — particularly the Ingenium 2.0 and 3.0 SDV6 — AdBlue/SCR issues are a common emissions-related complaint [PATTERN DATA]. The system is sensitive to fluid quality and temperature, and faults often trigger alongside the engine management light.

Amber vs Flashing — What Changes

🟡 Solid Amber AdBlue Light

  • Low fluid level — usually below 10% capacity
  • Safe to drive, but you need to top up AdBlue soon
  • Often accompanied by a countdown (e.g., 1,000 miles before no‑start)
  • Typical fix: top up with quality AdBlue (DIY or garage)

🔶 Flashing with Countdown / Derate

  • System fault — NOx sensor, injector, or heater failure
  • Power may be reduced (limp mode) to protect emissions components
  • If the countdown reaches zero, the vehicle will not restart
  • Typical fix: diagnostic scan + replacement of faulty component (£400–£1,500)

If the light is flashing and the vehicle has entered a countdown, you have limited mileage before the car will refuse to start. Stop and recover if you're unable to refill or diagnose the fault.

What To Do In the Next 24 Hours

1

Check AdBlue level

If the warning is solid amber, the most likely cause is low fluid. Top up with quality AdBlue (ISO 22241) — a 10-litre top-up costs around £20–£30. Do not overfill.

2

Check for a countdown

If the dashboard shows a remaining mileage countdown (e.g., "No restart in 500 miles"), you have limited distance before the car will not restart. Plan to visit a specialist soon.

3

Don't just clear the code

Clearing the code without addressing the cause is a short-term fix — the warning will return, and the underlying problem may worsen.

4

Get a diagnostic scan

If topping up doesn't clear the light, a diagnostic scan is needed. Most independents can read SCR-related codes (e.g., P20E8). This is the only way to pinpoint the fault.

5

Use genuine AdBlue only

Using non-certified AdBlue can crystallise and block the injector, causing expensive repairs. Always use ISO 22241‑compliant fluid.

6

Narrow it down

Can You Drive With the AdBlue Warning On?

✅ Solid Amber (No Countdown) — Yes

  • Safe to drive — but you should top up AdBlue as soon as possible
  • If you have a countdown, plan to visit a specialist within that mileage
  • Avoid prolonged driving if the countdown is below 500 miles

⛔ Flashing / Countdown Reached Zero — Stop

  • Do not drive — the vehicle will not restart after the countdown ends
  • If the light is flashing and you have no countdown, a system fault is present — drive directly to a garage
  • Recover the vehicle if you cannot refill or diagnose the fault

The exception is if the AdBlue warning appears alongside a oil pressure warning or engine management light with a flashing glow plug light — in those cases, stop and recover regardless.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the AdBlue warning mean on a Land Rover?

The AdBlue warning means the selective catalytic reduction (SCR) system has detected a fault — typically low AdBlue fluid, a failed NOx sensor, or a clogged injector. It's a request for attention, not an emergency, but ignoring it can lead to a no‑start condition.

Can I reset the AdBlue warning myself?

You can clear the code with an OBD‑II scanner, but the warning will return if the underlying cause isn't fixed. Topping up the AdBlue fluid is the correct first step; if the light stays on, a diagnostic scan is needed.

How much does it cost to fix an AdBlue warning on a Land Rover?

A simple top-up costs £20–£30. If a component has failed, costs range from £400 for a NOx sensor to £1,500 for an AdBlue heater or pump. Severe SCR catalyst failure can cost £1,200–£2,500 [EM-QUOTE].

Can I drive with the AdBlue warning on?

Solid amber (no countdown): yes, but top up soon. Flashing or countdown: you have limited mileage; if the countdown reaches zero, the car will not restart. Drive directly to a garage or recover the vehicle.

What happens if I ignore the AdBlue warning?

Ignoring a low AdBlue level will eventually trigger a countdown and, when it reaches zero, the vehicle will refuse to start. Ignoring a component fault can lead to more expensive repairs, including a new SCR catalyst (£1,200+) or engine derate.
