What the Suspension Fault Warning Means

The suspension fault warning — typically an amber or red icon showing a vehicle with an arrow or the words "Suspension Fault" — indicates the electronic air suspension (EAS) system has detected a problem. The EAS system uses a compressor, air springs, valve block, and height sensors to maintain ride height and comfort. When a component fails or a sensor gives an out-of-range reading, the system logs a fault and alerts the driver.

On Land Rover and Range Rover models, especially those with complex air suspension (L322, L405, L494, L663), suspension faults are common as mileage increases. The warning is the vehicle's way of telling you that the suspension is not operating within expected parameters. Ignoring it can lead to a full system failure, leaving you stranded or with a very expensive repair bill.

Amber vs Red — What Changes

🟡 Amber / Yellow Warning

  • Fault logged — often a sensor or minor leak
  • Vehicle may still maintain normal ride height
  • Safe to drive short distances, but book a diagnostic check
  • Typical fix: sensor replacement, software update, or leak repair

🔶 Flashing / Red — Stop Immediately

  • Serious fault — compressor failure, major leak, or system pressure loss
  • Vehicle may be sitting low or on bump stops
  • Do not drive — you risk damaging the compressor and other components
  • Recover the vehicle to a specialist

If the suspension fault warning flashes or the vehicle is visibly low, stop driving immediately. Continuing can destroy the compressor and cause further damage to air springs and the valve block.

What To Do In the Next 24 Hours

1

Note the colour and behaviour

Is the warning solid amber, flashing, or is the vehicle sitting low? This tells you how urgent the situation is.

2

Check for visible signs

Look under the vehicle for any obvious leaks or if the vehicle is leaning to one side. If it's on the bump stops, do not drive.

3

Check the compressor

Listen for the compressor running (it's under the bonnet or near the spare wheel). If it runs continuously or not at all, it may be failing.

4

Get a diagnostic scan

A specialist can read the suspension fault codes — this is the only way to know if it's a £150 sensor or a £1,200 compressor. Most independents can do this.

5

Don't just clear the code

Clearing the code without fixing the cause is a temporary fix — the fault will return, and you lose diagnostic data.

Can You Drive With the Suspension Fault Warning On?

🟡 Solid Amber — Yes, With Caution

  • If the warning is solid and the vehicle is at normal ride height, you can drive short distances
  • Get it checked as soon as possible — don't ignore it
  • Avoid heavy loads, towing, or off-road driving

⛔ Flashing / Low — Do Not Drive

  • Stop and recover — the system is compromised
  • If the vehicle is on the bump stops or leaning, driving will damage the compressor and other components
  • Recover the vehicle to a specialist

The exception is if the suspension fault appears alongside a engine management light or restricted performance — in those cases, stop and recover regardless.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the suspension fault warning mean on a Land Rover?

The suspension fault warning indicates the air suspension system has detected a problem — it could be a compressor, air spring, sensor, or valve block issue. The warning itself doesn't tell you the cause; you need a diagnostic scan to read the fault code.

Can I reset the suspension fault warning myself?

You can clear the code with an OBD-II scanner, but the warning will return if the underlying fault isn't fixed. Clearing the code also erases diagnostic data that a specialist would need, so it's not recommended without first diagnosing the cause.

How much does it cost to fix a suspension fault on a Land Rover?

A height sensor replacement costs £150–£350. An air spring replacement is £400–£800 per corner. A new compressor is £600–£1,200. A full system overhaul can exceed £2,500 [ESTIMATED].

Can I drive with the suspension fault warning on?

Solid amber: yes, but get it checked soon. Flashing or low: do not drive — stop and recover. If the vehicle is on the bump stops, do not drive.

What causes the suspension fault warning to come on?

Common causes include a failed compressor, leaking air spring, faulty height sensor, valve block issue, or an air line leak. A diagnostic scan is the only way to pinpoint the exact cause.
